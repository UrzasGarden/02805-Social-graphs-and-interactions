Kristoff Vernard is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer and artist John Byrne, the character first appeared in Fantastic Four #247 (October 1982). A Latverian orphan taken in by Doctor Doom and named his heir, Kristoff has also been known as Kristoff von Doom and, during a period in which he believed himself to be his guardian, as Doctor Doom. He has been both an adversary and an ally of the Fantastic Four.

== Publication history ==

Kristoff was introduced during John Byrne's tenure as writer and artist on Fantastic Four, in a story that returned Doctor Doom to the throne of Latveria. Byrne returned to the character in Fantastic Four #258 (September 1983), an issue devoted to Doom's daily routine, which showed Doom personally tutoring the boy, and in #260 (November 1983), in which Doom's apparent death set the later plot in motion. Byrne developed Kristoff into a principal antagonist in Fantastic Four #278–279 (May–June 1985), a two-part story in which the boy, convinced that he is Doom, destroys the Baxter Building. The storyline also retold Doom's origin from Kristoff's imprinted perspective.

Subsequent writers kept the character in circulation. Under Steve Englehart, Kristoff occupied the Latverian throne while a figure presenting himself as Doom clashed with the team, a situation Walter Simonson resolved in Fantastic Four #350 (March 1991). Tom DeFalco and artist Paul Ryan later restored Kristoff's own personality and made him a supporting cast member from Fantastic Four #396 (January 1995) through the mid-1990s. He returned in Jonathan Hickman's Fantastic Four and FF (2009–2012), in Christopher Cantwell's Doctor Doom (2019–2020) and in Dan Slott's Fantastic Four (2018–2022).

== Fictional character biography ==

Kristoff Vernard is born in Latveria to a peasant family. After Doom is deposed, his successor Zorba becomes a tyrant, and during the resulting unrest Kristoff's mother is killed by robots acting on Zorba's orders. When Doom returns to his homeland with the Fantastic Four to overthrow Zorba, he finds the orphaned boy and takes him into Castle Doom, declaring that Kristoff will be raised as his ward and heir.

Doom apparently dies shortly afterward in a battle involving Terrax and the Silver Surfer. His Doombots, following standing orders, subject Kristoff to a process that imprints Doom's memories and personality onto the boy's mind. Boris, Doom's longtime retainer, interrupts the transfer, and Kristoff receives Doom's recollections only up to an early stage of his career, at the point when Doom had schemed to lift the Baxter Building into orbit. Believing himself to be Doom and unaware that years have passed, Kristoff carries the old plan out: he launches the Baxter Building into space and destroys it. The Fantastic Four, at that time consisting of Mister Fantastic, the Invisible Woman, the Human Torch and She-Hulk, escape before the detonation, pursue Kristoff to Latveria and defeat him. He is held in suspended animation at the team's new headquarters, Four Freedoms Plaza.

Kristoff is eventually freed by his robotic servants and resumes his claim on Latveria. When the genuine Doom, restored to life by the Beyonder, seeks to retake his kingdom, two men bearing the same name and armor contest the throne, and each believes the other to be an impostor. The dispute is settled when Doom confronts Kristoff directly and demonstrates that the boy's memories end decades before the present day. Defeated, Kristoff is removed from power, and Doom reveals that a Doombot had acted in his place during part of the conflict.

Following Doom's apparent death alongside Reed Richards in a struggle with the entity Hunger, Nathaniel Richards, Reed's father, enters Latveria and locates Kristoff. With Nathaniel's assistance the imprinted Doom persona is suppressed, and Kristoff regains his own identity while retaining the knowledge he had absorbed. He accompanies Nathaniel to New York and lives at Four Freedoms Plaza, serving the team as a technician and occasional field operative alongside Ant-Man (Scott Lang) and Lyja during the period when Richards is presumed dead. He takes part in battles against the Dark Raider and in the "Atlantis Rising" crossover. After Reed Richards returns, Kristoff leaves the team and goes back to Latveria.

In later years Kristoff remains part of Doom's household. When Doom's intellect is damaged by the Intelligencia, Kristoff attends him in Latveria and receives Valeria Richards when she arrives to offer her father's help; Doom is subsequently brought to the Baxter Building to join the Future Foundation, whose ranks then include Spider-Man, and Kristoff oversees Latverian affairs in his absence. He is on friendly terms with Valeria and Franklin Richards, who regard him as something close to an older brother. Kristoff is later present in Latveria during the political upheavals that follow Doom's exile from his own country, and during the events surrounding Doom's wedding to Victorious.

== Powers and abilities ==

Kristoff Vernard has no superhuman powers. As a consequence of the memory transfer, he possesses much of Doctor Doom's scientific and technological expertise as it stood at the time the imprinted memories end, together with an elementary grounding in Doom's sorcery. He has continued his studies since regaining his own identity and is an accomplished engineer, strategist and administrator. He is fluent in several languages and trained in the use of Latverian weaponry.

In the field Kristoff has worn battle armor patterned on Doom's. The suit increases his strength and resilience, generates a personal force field, projects concussive energy from its gauntlets and allows flight by means of jet boots. While in control of Latveria he commands the country's military and its contingent of Doombots.

== Other versions ==

In the alternate future depicted in Fantastic Four: The End, Latveria is governed without Doom, and the character does not appear; a version of Kristoff is instead referenced in the "Secret Wars" (2015) Battleworld domain of Doomstadt, where God Emperor Doom's court includes figures drawn from Latverian history. In the MC2 continuity, the Latverian succession passes outside the Vernard line altogether.
