She-Hulk (Jennifer Susan Walters) is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist John Buscema, the character first appeared in The Savage She-Hulk #1 (February 1980). Walters is a lawyer and the cousin of Bruce Banner, the Hulk. After she is shot and badly wounded, an emergency blood transfusion from Banner leaves her with a milder form of his condition, enabling her to become a tall, green-skinned and superhumanly strong version of herself. Unlike her cousin, she generally keeps her intelligence and personality when transformed, and she eventually comes to prefer her She-Hulk form, continuing to practice law in it.

Over the course of her publication history, She-Hulk has been a member of the Avengers, the Fantastic Four, Heroes for Hire, the Defenders, S.H.I.E.L.D., the Future Foundation, the Lady Liberators and A-Force. Her romantic relationships have included Wyatt Wingfoot, Hercules, Luke Cage and John Jameson, whom she briefly married. She has headlined several solo series, among them The Sensational She-Hulk (1989–1994), in which writer-artist John Byrne had her regularly break the fourth wall, and She-Hulk (2004–2009), which centered on her work at a law firm specializing in superhuman cases.

The character has appeared in a number of adaptations in other media. She was voiced by Lisa Zane and Cree Summer in the animated series The Incredible Hulk (1996–1997) and by Eliza Dushku in Hulk and the Agents of S.M.A.S.H. (2013–2015). Tatiana Maslany portrays Jennifer Walters in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, headlining the Disney+ series She-Hulk: Attorney at Law (2022), in which Tim Roth reprised his role as Emil Blonsky (Abomination).

== Publication history ==

== Creation ==

She-Hulk was created largely in response to the success of the live-action television series The Incredible Hulk (1977–1982). Marvel was concerned that the program's producers at Universal might introduce a female counterpart to the Hulk on their own, as had happened when The Six Million Dollar Man generated the spin-off The Bionic Woman, leaving the company with no claim to such a character. Stan Lee, who by that point rarely scripted comics, wrote the first issue so that Marvel would hold the copyright and trademark. Lee later acknowledged that protecting the name had been the principal reason for the character's creation.

Lee and penciller John Buscema worked only on the debut issue of The Savage She-Hulk. From the second issue the series was written by David Anthony Kraft and pencilled by Mike Vosburg, with inks by Chic Stone, and this team remained for most of the run. The title lasted 25 issues, from February 1980 to March 1982. Its stories presented Walters as a Los Angeles defense attorney whose transformed state was more impulsive and aggressive than her human self, and in the final issue a cure for a blood disorder, supplied by Michael Morbius, leaves her in full control of her changes.

== 1980s ==

After the cancellation of her series, She-Hulk joined the Avengers in The Avengers #221 (July 1982) and remained a regular in that title for several years. Following the 1984 limited series Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars, in which the Thing chose to stay behind on an alien world, she took his place in the Fantastic Four beginning with Fantastic Four #265 (April 1984). Writer-artist John Byrne made extensive use of the character during his tenure on the book, and she stayed with the team until the Thing's return. Byrne also wrote and drew Marvel Graphic Novel #18: The Sensational She-Hulk (1985), in which exposure to radiation leaves Walters unable to revert to human form, a status quo that persisted for many years.

Byrne launched a second ongoing series, The Sensational She-Hulk, in May 1989. The title was distinguished by its comedic tone and by the character's awareness that she was in a comic book: she spoke directly to readers, argued with the writer and tore through panel borders to move between scenes. The device predates its association with later Marvel characters such as Deadpool and Gwenpool. Byrne left after the eighth issue following disputes with editorial. Steve Gerber wrote a lengthy stretch of the book, and artists including Bryan Hitch contributed before Byrne returned for issues #31–50. The series ended with issue #60 (February 1994).

== 1990s and 2000s ==

She-Hulk had no solo title for a decade after The Sensational She-Hulk ended. During this period she appeared as a supporting character in The Avengers and as a member of the Heroes for Hire in that team's 1997–1999 series, alongside Luke Cage and Hercules, and made regular appearances in Fantastic Four and in the Hulk's own title. In "Avengers Disassembled" (2004), an attack orchestrated by the Scarlet Witch causes She-Hulk to lose control and tear the Vision in half, an event that later stories revisited.

Writer Dan Slott revived the character in She-Hulk, which began in May 2004 with art by Juan Bobillo. The series reframed Walters as an attorney at the New York firm Goodman, Lieber, Kurtzberg & Holliway, which handled cases involving superhumans, and treated Marvel's own comic books as admissible legal records within the story. Storylines had her represent Spider-Man in a libel action against J. Jonah Jameson, marry John Jameson, and defend Robbie Baldwin (Speedball) after the Stamford disaster that led to "Civil War". The first volume ran 12 issues; a second volume began in December 2005. Peter David took over as writer with issue #22 (2007), recasting Walters as a bounty hunter partnered with the Skrull Jazinda, and the series concluded with issue #38 (2009).

In 2009 Fred Van Lente wrote the limited series All-New Savage She-Hulk, which introduced Lyra, the daughter of the Hulk and Thundra from an alternate future who adopts the She-Hulk name. Walters and Lyra later shared the four-issue series She-Hulks (2010–2011) by Harrison Wilcox, which followed the "Fall of the Hulks" and "World War Hulks" storylines.

== 2010s and 2020s ==

Walters was a member of the Future Foundation in Jonathan Hickman's FF (2011–2012) and received a new solo series in February 2014 written by Charles Soule, himself a practicing attorney, with art by Javier Pulido and Ron Wimberly and covers by Kevin Wada. The 12-issue run returned her to legal practice, this time from a small office in Brooklyn, and featured Patsy Walker (Hellcat) as her investigator. In 2015 she led the all-female team A-Force, first in a limited series tied to "Secret Wars" and then in an ongoing title.

The 2016 crossover "Civil War II" left Walters comatose after an attack by Thanos, and Bruce Banner was killed during the same event. Mariko Tamaki and artist Nico Leon then launched Hulk in December 2016, portraying a grey, traumatized Walters who had inherited her cousin's title. The book was renamed She-Hulk with issue #159 (2017), when Marvel restored legacy numbering, and ended with issue #163 (2018). She was a founding member of the Avengers lineup in Jason Aaron's run beginning in 2018, where she was drawn as a larger and more savage figure than before.

Rainbow Rowell and artist Rogê Antônio began a new She-Hulk series in January 2022, which restored the lighter tone and legal setting of earlier volumes and ran 15 issues. Rowell continued the character's story in The Sensational She-Hulk, launched in October 2023 with artist Andrés Genolet.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Origin ==

Jennifer Walters is born in Los Angeles, the daughter of Sheriff William Morris Walters and Elaine Walters. Her mother dies in a car crash when Jennifer is a teenager, and she grows up close to her cousin Bruce Banner, who spends summers with her family. She studies law at the University of California, Los Angeles, and becomes a criminal defense attorney. Walters is shy and reserved, and Banner's visit to her office is the first time the cousins have seen each other in years.

While defending Lou Monkton, a man accused of murder, Walters is shot by gunmen working for crime boss Nicholas Trask, who wants to prevent her from exposing his part in the killing. Banner, who is with her when the attack occurs, can find no other donor and gives her a transfusion of his own gamma-irradiated blood. The transfusion saves her life but also passes on a version of his condition. When Trask's men later try to finish her in the hospital, her anger triggers her first transformation into a tall green figure, and she drives them off.

In her early appearances Walters' change is involuntary and brought on by anger or stress, and her She-Hulk persona is more reckless and uninhibited than her human self, though she never loses her reason as her cousin does. She continues her legal career while secretly operating as She-Hulk and eventually brings Trask to justice. After Michael Morbius cures her of a blood disease, Walters gains the ability to change at will and retains her full intellect and personality in either state. She soon finds that she prefers being She-Hulk, in which she is more confident and assertive, and she remains in that form most of the time.
