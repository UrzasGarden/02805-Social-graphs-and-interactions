Thunderbolt is the name of two fictional characters appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The first and best-known Thunderbolt, William Carver, was created by writer Marv Wolfman and artist Lee Elias and first appeared in Power Man #41 (March 1977). A speedster operating in New York City, he was a supporting character in the adventures of Luke Cage and Iron Fist before his death. A second, unrelated character later used the name.

== Publication history ==

William Carver debuted in Power Man #41 (March 1977), during Marv Wolfman's run on the title, and was introduced as a costumed crime fighter whose turf overlapped with that of Luke Cage. He reappeared in the retitled Power Man and Iron Fist, where his condition of accelerated aging became the central element of his story. The character was killed off in Power Man and Iron Fist #62 (April 1980), written by Mary Jo Duffy, and has only occasionally been referenced since.

A second Thunderbolt, a costumed figure with no connection to Carver, appeared briefly in later Marvel titles. The name has otherwise been more closely associated with the Thunderbolts team and with the Thunderbolt Ross alias of the Red Hulk.

== Fictional character biography ==

William Carver is an investigator for the district attorney's office in New York City. While pursuing a case against the drug trade in Harlem, he is captured by criminals and subjected to an experimental process intended to produce an obedient enforcer. The procedure instead grants Carver superhuman speed, and he escapes, adopting the costumed identity of Thunderbolt to continue his fight against organized crime outside the law.

Thunderbolt's activities bring him into contact with Luke Cage, then operating as Power Man. The two initially clash over territory and methods but come to respect one another, and Thunderbolt assists Cage against several street-level threats. His sister, Tanya Carver, becomes involved with Cage's circle of acquaintances during this period.

Carver eventually learns that the process which gave him his speed is also aging his body at an accelerated rate. Within a few years of gaining his powers, he has physically aged by decades. Seeking a cure, he turns to Cage and Iron Fist, who attempt to help him track down the people responsible for his condition. The search proves fruitless, and Carver, by then an old man, dies in the arms of his friends shortly after a final confrontation with the criminals who created him.

== Powers and abilities ==

Thunderbolt possessed superhuman speed, allowing him to run at velocities far beyond those of a normal human and to react to events too quickly for opponents to follow. He could deliver rapid series of blows and move short distances faster than the eye could track. The same process that granted these abilities caused his metabolism to burn through his natural lifespan, so that every use of his speed accelerated his aging. Before his transformation Carver was a trained investigator skilled in unarmed combat and criminal investigation.

== Other versions ==

An elderly William Carver appears briefly in flashback sequences recounting Luke Cage's early career, where his death is presented as one of the formative losses of Cage's time as a hero for hire.
