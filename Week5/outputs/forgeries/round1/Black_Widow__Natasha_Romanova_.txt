Black Widow (Natasha Romanova) is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by editor Stan Lee, scripter Don Rico and artist Don Heck, and first appeared in Tales of Suspense #52 (April 1964). Introduced as a Soviet intelligence operative and an adversary of Iron Man, she later defected to the United States, becoming an agent of the espionage agency S.H.I.E.L.D. and a longstanding member of the Avengers. She has also been affiliated with the Champions, the Thunderbolts and the Secret Avengers.

Natasha's real name is usually given as Natalia Alianovna Romanova, with Natasha Romanoff used as an anglicized form. She was trained from childhood in the Red Room, a Soviet program that produced female assassins and spies, and her history has been tied to the Red Guardian (Alexei Shostakov), whom she married before her defection. A second Black Widow, Yelena Belova, was introduced in 1999 as a younger graduate of the same program and has alternated between being her rival and her ally.

The character has been adapted into numerous animated series and video games. Scarlett Johansson portrayed Natasha Romanoff in the Marvel Cinematic Universe films, beginning with Iron Man 2 (2010) and concluding with the solo film Black Widow (2021), in which Florence Pugh appeared as Yelena Belova.

== Publication history ==

== 1960s ==

Black Widow debuted in Tales of Suspense #52 (April 1964) as a non-costumed Soviet agent sent to the United States to assassinate the defector Anton Vanko and to steal from Tony Stark. In her early appearances she wore evening wear and a veil rather than a superhero outfit, and her methods relied on seduction and sabotage rather than combat. Don Heck drew her as a dark-haired femme fatale in the mould of the spy fiction of the period, and her early stories pitted her against Iron Man in a succession of short backup-length adventures.

In Tales of Suspense #57 (September 1964) she recruited the archer Hawkeye (Clint Barton) to her cause, and the two became a recurring pair of antagonists for Iron Man. She received her first costume in Tales of Suspense #64 (April 1965), a dark bodysuit with fishnet stockings, a mask and a cape, along with gadgets such as a wrist-mounted "widow's line" supplied by her Soviet handlers. Her defection to the West was dramatized in The Avengers #29–#30 (June–July 1966) and in subsequent issues written by Roy Thomas, during which she operated unofficially alongside the team and broke with her former superiors.

Her Soviet past was expanded in The Avengers #43–#44 (August–September 1967), which introduced her husband, the Red Guardian, a Soviet counterpart to Captain America presumed killed in the line of duty. Throughout the late 1960s she remained a supporting character in The Avengers and in S.H.I.E.L.D. stories, and briefly undertook missions on the agency's behalf before being made a full Avenger.

== 1970s ==

In The Amazing Spider-Man #86 (July 1970), writer Stan Lee and artist John Romita Sr. gave the character a comprehensive redesign, replacing the fishnets and mask with a skin-tight black jumpsuit, bracelets housing her weapons and a belt, and recoloring her hair from black to red. The issue, in which she tests herself against Spider-Man, was intended to launch her as a solo attraction, and the costume remained her standard look for decades.

She received her own feature in Amazing Adventures #1–#8 (August 1970–September 1971), sharing the title with the Inhumans. The strip was initially written by Gary Friedrich and drawn by John Buscema, with later installments by Roy Thomas and Gene Colan. The series positioned her as an urban adventurer operating from a Manhattan penthouse with her chauffeur and confidant Ivan Petrovitch, whose connection to her childhood would be elaborated in later stories. Following the cancellation of the feature, she moved into Daredevil beginning with issue #81 (November 1971), where writer Gerry Conway paired her romantically with Matt Murdock. The two relocated to San Francisco, and the comic was retitled Daredevil and the Black Widow from issue #92 (October 1972) through issue #107 (January 1974).

After the relationship with Daredevil ended, Black Widow became a founding member and the de facto leader of the Champions, a Los Angeles-based team created by Tony Isabella that also included Hercules, Ghost Rider (Johnny Blaze), the Angel and Iceman. The Champions ran for 17 issues from October 1975 to January 1978. In 1973 she had also been formally inducted into the Avengers in The Avengers #111 (May 1973), and she returned to the team on an intermittent basis through the remainder of the decade, including a short period as chairwoman.

== 1980s and 1990s ==

In the 1980s the character appeared chiefly as a guest star in titles such as Marvel Team-Up, Marvel Two-in-One and Daredevil, including a prominent role in Frank Miller's run on the latter. Her most substantial solo story of the period was "Web of Intrigue", serialized in Marvel Fanfare #10–#13 (August 1983–March 1984) by writer Ralph Macchio and artist George Pérez, which re-examined her origin and her relationship with Ivan. The storyline was later collected as a standalone volume. Uncanny X-Men #268 (September 1990), by Chris Claremont and Jim Lee, retroactively established that Captain America and Wolverine had rescued the young Natasha from the Hand in 1941, linking her to Wolverine's extended backstory and implying that her aging had been artificially slowed.

Marvel published the graphic novel Black Widow: The Coldest War in 1990, written by Gerry Conway and drawn by George Freeman, which revisited her Soviet past in light of the end of the Cold War. She also starred in serials in the anthology Marvel Comics Presents and became a regular member of the Avengers once more in the early 1990s, serving as team leader for an extended period under writer Bob Harras. In 1999 the Marvel Knights imprint published a three-issue Black Widow limited series by writer Devin Grayson and artist J. G. Jones that introduced Yelena Belova as a younger agent trained to replace her; a 2001 sequel by Grayson, Greg Rucka and artist Scott Hampton continued the rivalry.

== 2000s ==

Rucka and artist Igor Kordey produced Black Widow: Pale Little Spider (2002) for the mature-readers MAX imprint, focusing on Belova. Natasha returned to a solo title with Black Widow (2004–2005), a six-issue series written by novelist Richard K. Morgan and illustrated by Bill Sienkiewicz and Goran Parlov, which revealed that the Red Room had conditioned her with false memories and chemically enhanced her. A second Morgan series, Black Widow: The Things They Say About Her (2005–2006), with art by Sean Phillips, followed. During this period she also joined the Avengers in New Avengers and the Mighty Avengers, took part in the "Civil War" and "Secret Invasion" crossovers, and briefly served as Captain America's partner in the Captain America title written by Ed Brubaker after the apparent death of Steve Rogers.

The four-issue Black Widow: Deadly Origin (2010), by Paul Cornell and artists Tom Raney and John Paul Leon, offered an updated account of her life story. It was followed by an ongoing Black Widow series launched in April 2010, written first by Marjorie Liu with art by Daniel Acuña and then by Duane Swierczynski with Manuel Garcia. That series crossed over with Hawkeye & Mockingbird in the "Widowmaker" storyline before its cancellation after eight issues. She was also a founding member of the Secret Avengers in the title of the same name, launched the same year by Brubaker and Mike Deodato.

== 2010s and 2020s ==

A new Black Widow series by writer Nathan Edmondson and artist Phil Noto began in 2014 and ran for 20 issues, depicting Natasha taking freelance missions to atone for her past. Mark Waid and Chris Samnee followed with a 12-issue volume in 2016–2017 that returned her to conflict with the Red Room. During the 2017 crossover "Secret Empire", the character was killed by the Hydra-aligned Captain America, and a clone with her memories was introduced in Tales of Suspense #100–#104 (2018) by Matthew Rosenberg and Travel Foreman.

Filmmakers Jen and Sylvia Soska wrote a five-issue Black Widow miniseries with artist Flaviano in 2019, and Jody Houser and Stephen Mooney produced The Web of Black Widow the same year. A new ongoing title by Kelly Thompson and artist Elena Casagrande launched in 2020 and ran for 15 issues, winning the Eisner Award for Best New Series in 2021. In 2024 Marvel published Black Widow & Hawkeye by Stephanie Phillips and Paolo Villanelli, and the limited series Black Widow: Venomous by Erica Schultz, a tie-in to the "Venom War" event in which Natasha bonded with a symbiote related to Venom.
