She-Hulk (Jennifer Susan Walters) is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist John Buscema, she first appeared in The Savage She-Hulk #1 (February 1980). Walters is a lawyer and the cousin of Bruce Banner, the Hulk. After being shot by criminals, she receives an emergency blood transfusion from Banner and acquires a milder form of his condition, transforming into a tall, green-skinned, superhumanly strong version of herself. Unlike her cousin, she usually keeps her intelligence and personality when transformed, and over time she comes to prefer her She-Hulk form.

She-Hulk has been a member of the Avengers, the Fantastic Four, Heroes for Hire, the Defenders, S.H.I.E.L.D., the Lady Liberators, A-Force and the Fearless Defenders. A skilled attorney, she has frequently been depicted representing superhumans in court, and the character is known for stories in which she breaks the fourth wall and addresses the reader directly. She-Hulk has appeared in several animated series and video games, and Tatiana Maslany portrays Jennifer Walters in the Marvel Cinematic Universe television series She-Hulk: Attorney at Law (2022).

== Publication history ==

== Creation ==

The character was created during the run of the live-action television series The Incredible Hulk, which aired on CBS from 1978 to 1982. Marvel was concerned that the show's producers might introduce a female counterpart to the Hulk, as had happened with the Six Million Dollar Man spin-off The Bionic Woman, and that the television studio would then hold the rights to such a character. Stan Lee wrote the first issue of The Savage She-Hulk in order to establish Marvel's ownership, and it was the last new character he created for the company before leaving for California to pursue film and television projects. John Buscema provided the art for the debut issue.

Lee later stated that he was not especially fond of the name She-Hulk, and that the premise was decided on quickly to secure the trademark. The character was given a background as an attorney, a profession that provided a contrast with her violent alter ego and that would become increasingly important in later interpretations.

== 1980s ==

The Savage She-Hulk ran for 25 issues from February 1980 to March 1982. After Lee's first issue, David Anthony Kraft wrote the remainder of the series, with Mike Vosburg as the regular penciller. The series depicted Walters as largely unable to control her transformations, which were triggered by anger, and it emphasized her strained relationship with her father, Los Angeles County Sheriff Morris Walters. The book was cancelled due to low sales.

Following the cancellation, She-Hulk joined the Avengers in The Avengers #221 (July 1982), where she was written by Jim Shooter and Roger Stern. During this period her personality was gradually revised; she was shown to retain her intellect while transformed and to enjoy her enhanced form. After the 1984–1985 limited series Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars, she replaced the Thing in the Fantastic Four, remaining with the team from Fantastic Four #265 (April 1984) to #300 (March 1987). Writer and artist John Byrne handled these stories and developed the character's confident, humorous persona. Byrne also wrote and drew the graphic novel The Sensational She-Hulk (1985), in which she was captured by S.H.I.E.L.D. and discovered she could no longer revert to human form.

In 1989 Marvel launched a second ongoing series, The Sensational She-Hulk, again written and illustrated by Byrne. The title was notable for its metafictional humor, with She-Hulk aware that she was a comic book character and frequently speaking to the reader, the writer and the editor. Byrne left after issue #8 following disagreements with editorial, and the series continued under writers including Steve Gerber, Louise Simonson and Simon Furman, with artists such as Bryan Hitch. Byrne returned with issue #31 and remained until #50, after which the book continued until its cancellation with issue #60 (February 1994).

== 1990s and 2000s ==

After the end of her second series She-Hulk appeared as a supporting character in team books, including a return to the Avengers under writer Kurt Busiek and a stint in Heroes for Hire alongside Luke Cage. She also featured in the Byrne-written miniseries Fantastic Four and in several one-shots.

A third ongoing series, titled She-Hulk, debuted in May 2004, written by Dan Slott with art by Juan Bobillo. The series returned the character to the legal profession, placing her in the superhuman law division of the firm Goodman, Lieber, Kurtzberg & Holliway, where previous Marvel comics were treated as admissible legal precedent. The series ran for 12 issues and was relaunched in 2005 with a second volume, also written by Slott, which lasted 38 issues until 2009. Peter David took over as writer with issue #22, shifting the focus toward bounty hunting and introducing the Skrull character Jazinda as her partner. The series tied into the crossover storylines "Civil War", "World War Hulk" and "Secret Invasion".

In 2009 the character appeared in All-New Savage She-Hulk, a four-issue limited series written by Fred Van Lente that introduced Lyra, the daughter of the Hulk and Thundra from an alternate future, as a new She-Hulk. The two characters later shared the 2010 limited series She-Hulks, written by Harrison Wilcox.

== 2010s and 2020s ==

A fourth volume of She-Hulk, written by Charles Soule with art by Javier Pulido, was published from February 2014 to February 2015 for 12 issues. Soule, a practicing attorney, focused on Walters establishing her own law practice and included storylines involving Captain America and Doctor Doom. She-Hulk was also a member of the all-female Avengers team in A-Force, launched during the 2015 "Secret Wars" event.

Following the 2016 crossover "Civil War II", in which the Hulk was killed and Walters was gravely injured, the character headlined a series titled simply Hulk, written by Mariko Tamaki with art by Nico Leon. The book, which began in December 2016, portrayed Walters dealing with trauma and a darker, less controllable grey transformation. It was retitled She-Hulk with issue #159 in 2017 and ended with issue #163 in 2018. She-Hulk then appeared as a regular member of the Avengers in the series written by Jason Aaron beginning in 2018, where she was depicted in a larger and more savage form.

In January 2022 Marvel launched a new She-Hulk ongoing series written by Rainbow Rowell with art by Rogê Antônio, which restored the character's classic appearance and legal career and introduced a romantic relationship with Jack of Hearts. The title was relaunched as The Sensational She-Hulk in 2023, continuing under Rowell.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Origin ==

Jennifer Walters is born in Los Angeles, the daughter of Sheriff William Morris Walters and Elaine Banner Walters. Her mother dies in a car crash when Jennifer is seventeen, and she is raised by her father. Walters grows up shy and bookish and is close to her cousin Bruce Banner, who spends summers with her family. She attends UCLA and Harvard Law School and becomes a criminal defense attorney in Los Angeles.

While Banner is visiting Los Angeles, Walters is shot by men working for the crime boss Nicholas Trask, against whom she is building a case. With no other donor available, Banner gives her a transfusion of his own gamma-irradiated blood. The transfusion saves her life but also alters her physiology, and when Trask's men attempt to finish her off in the hospital, Walters transforms for the first time into the She-Hulk. Initially her transformations are triggered by anger, and in this form she is more aggressive and less inhibited than her human self, though she retains far more of her mind than her cousin does. After a series of confrontations with Trask and other enemies, Walters gains control of her changes and begins to remain in her She-Hulk form by choice.

== Avengers and Fantastic Four ==

Walters relocates to New York City and joins the Avengers, fighting alongside the team against enemies such as the Masters of Evil and forming friendships with the Wasp and Hawkeye. She is among the heroes transported to Battleworld by the Beyonder during the first "Secret Wars". When the Thing elects to remain on Battleworld, She-Hulk takes his place in the Fantastic Four at Mister Fantastic's invitation. During her time with the team she develops a close relationship with Wyatt Wingfoot and is exposed to a large dose of radiation while sabotaging a nuclear-powered Skrull vessel. The exposure leaves her unable to transform back into Jennifer Walters, a change she welcomes, although she later regains the ability.
