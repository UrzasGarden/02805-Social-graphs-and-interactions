Vance Astrovik is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. He has operated under the code names Marvel Boy and, later, Justice. The character was created by writer Steve Gerber and artist Don Heck and first appeared in Giant-Size Defenders #5 (July 1975). He is a mutant with telekinetic abilities and the younger, alternate-timeline counterpart of Vance Astro, a founding member of the 31st-century Guardians of the Galaxy. Astrovik has been a member of the New Warriors, the Avengers and the Fifty State Initiative, served on the faculty of Avengers Academy, and has had a long-running relationship with his teammate Firestar.

The character has made only limited appearances outside comics, including the mobile game Marvel Avengers Academy.

== Publication history ==

Vance Astrovik first appeared as a teenager in Giant-Size Defenders #5 (July 1975), an issue plotted by Steve Gerber with several co-writers and drawn by Don Heck, in which the time-traveling Guardians of the Galaxy visited the present-day Marvel Universe. The character is a younger version of Vance Astro (Major Victory), the Guardians member created by Arnold Drake and Gene Colan in Marvel Super-Heroes #18 (January 1969). In Marvel Two-in-One #69 (November 1980), written by Mark Gruenwald and Ralph Macchio and penciled by Ron Wilson, Astro's visit to his younger self changed the boy's future and triggered his mutant powers, establishing the two as separate characters in separate timelines.

Mike Carlin and Ron Wilson turned the teenager into a costumed hero in The Thing #32 (February 1986), where he adopted the name Marvel Boy, a code name used by several unrelated Marvel characters before him. Writer Fabian Nicieza and artist Mark Bagley made him one of the founding members of the New Warriors in The New Warriors #1 (July 1990). During Nicieza's run the character stood trial for the death of his father and was imprisoned; after his release he took the name Justice, which Marvel had previously used for the New Universe character John Tensen.

Kurt Busiek and George Pérez added Justice and Firestar to the Avengers in Avengers vol. 3 #4 (May 1998), and the pair remained with the team for most of Busiek's run. Dan Slott and Christos Gage featured the character prominently in Avengers: The Initiative (2007–2010), and Gage made him a member of the teaching staff in Avengers Academy (2010–2012). Christopher Yost placed Justice in charge of a new team in New Warriors vol. 5 (2014), which ran for twelve issues.

== Fictional character biography ==

Vance Astrovik is born in Saugerties, New York, the son of Arnold and Norma Astrovik. As a boy he is visited by Vance Astro, a version of himself from an alternate future who became an astronaut, spent a thousand years in suspended animation and eventually joined the Guardians of the Galaxy. Astro hopes to spare the boy that solitary fate, but the meeting causes Vance's latent mutant telekinesis to emerge years earlier than it otherwise would have, setting his life on a different path from that of his counterpart.

As a teenager Vance takes the costumed identity of Marvel Boy and befriends the Thing, accompanying him on several adventures. A lifelong admirer of the Avengers, he applies for membership and is turned down. He later becomes a founding member of the New Warriors, a team of young heroes organized by Night Thrasher that also includes Nova, Namorita, Speedball (Robbie Baldwin) and Firestar, with whom Vance begins a romantic relationship.

Arnold Astrovik has long been physically abusive toward his son and is hostile to his being a mutant. During a violent confrontation Vance strikes back telekinetically and kills him. He refuses to go into hiding and stands trial, where he is acquitted of murder but convicted of negligent homicide. He serves his sentence in the Vault, a maximum-security prison for superhumans staffed by armored Guardsmen, and declines to take part in a breakout attempted by other inmates. Released after serving his term, Vance rejoins the New Warriors under the name Justice and later becomes engaged to Firestar.

== Avengers ==

When the Avengers reform following "Heroes Return", Justice and Firestar are asked to join as reserve members and are soon promoted to full status. Justice breaks his leg early in his tenure and is sidelined for several missions, but his detailed knowledge of Avengers history proves useful to the team. During "Ultron Unlimited" he recognizes that Antarctic vibranium can dissolve Ultron's adamantium body, allowing Hank Pym to destroy the robot. Over the following years he serves alongside members including Hawkeye, Warbird, the Vision and She-Hulk. Justice and Firestar eventually take a leave of absence to concentrate on their private lives, and Firestar later calls off the engagement.

== Civil War and the Initiative ==

In "Civil War", Justice registers under the Superhuman Registration Act after the Stamford incident, in which an explosion during a New Warriors mission kills hundreds of civilians and his former teammate Speedball is publicly blamed. He becomes an instructor at Camp Hammond, the training base of the Fifty State Initiative, where he works with the drill instructor Gauntlet (Joseph Green). When Gauntlet is badly beaten in retaliation for his contempt toward the New Warriors, Justice investigates the assault, and he later exposes the cover-up surrounding the death of the recruit MVP.

Disillusioned with the Initiative, Justice resigns and forms Counter Force with Rage, Slapstick, Debrii and Ultra Girl, with whom he begins a relationship, to keep watch on the program from outside. The group, later joined by Night Thrasher (Donyell Taylor), reclaims the New Warriors name. During "Secret Invasion" the team returns to Camp Hammond to help repel the Skrull assault, and in "Dark Reign" it opposes Norman Osborn's control of the Initiative and battles Ragnarok, the cyborg clone of Thor.

== Avengers Academy and later ==

After "Siege", Justice joins the faculty of Avengers Academy, a school founded by Hank Pym for young superhumans who were experimented on by Osborn. He teaches alongside Pym, Tigra, Quicksilver and Speedball. His relationship with Ultra Girl ends when he grows close to Firestar again, and the two eventually reconcile. Justice takes part in defending the school during "Fear Itself" and "Avengers vs. X-Men".

Justice subsequently brings together a new incarnation of the New Warriors that includes Speedball, Nova (Sam Alexander), Scarlet Spider (Kaine), Sun Girl, Hummingbird, Haechi and Water Snake, and leads them against the High Evolutionary's plan to eliminate every non-human species on Earth. In later stories he and Firestar remain a couple and appear with other former Avengers in various team-ups.

== Powers and abilities ==

Vance Astrovik is a mutant with the ability to move objects telekinetically. He can lift and hurl heavy objects, erect protective force fields, project concussive bursts of telekinetic force and propel himself through the air at considerable speed. His power was initially modest and tiring to use, and overexertion caused him nosebleeds and headaches, but it has grown substantially over the years. He kept his abilities after the events of "House of M" removed the powers of most of the world's mutants.

Astrovik is a competent hand-to-hand combatant, having trained with Captain America and other Avengers, and he has an extensive knowledge of superhero history that he frequently applies in the field.

== Other versions ==

Vance Astro (Major Victory) of Earth-691 is the character's original counterpart. In that timeline Astrovik's powers never surface in childhood; he becomes an astronaut, is placed in suspended animation for a thousand-year voyage to Alpha Centauri and awakens in the 31st century, where he co-founds the Guardians of the Galaxy and later carries Captain America's shield. The Guardians' repeated visits to the 20th century are what bring the two versions into contact.
