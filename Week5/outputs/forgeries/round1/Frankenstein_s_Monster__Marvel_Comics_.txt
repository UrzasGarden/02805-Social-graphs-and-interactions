Frankenstein's Monster is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character is based on the creature from Mary Shelley's 1818 novel Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus. Although versions of the creature had appeared in Marvel and Atlas Comics stories since the 1950s, the character who became part of the Marvel Universe was introduced by writer Gary Friedrich and artist Mike Ploog in The Monster of Frankenstein #1 (January 1973).

Within Marvel continuity the Monster, assembled in the late 18th century by Victor Frankenstein, has survived into the present day through long periods of suspended animation. He has operated as a solitary wanderer, as a member of the Legion of Monsters and as an agent of S.H.I.E.L.D.'s Howling Commandos, and he has been associated with the monster hunters Ulysses Bloodstone and Elsa Bloodstone. Spider-Man, Ghost Rider (Johnny Blaze), Werewolf by Night, Man-Thing and She-Hulk are among the characters who have appeared alongside him. The character has also appeared in several Marvel animated television series and video games.

== Publication history ==

Marvel's predecessor, Atlas Comics, used Shelley's creature in horror anthology stories before the Comics Code Authority was established. One of these, "Your Name Is Frankenstein" in Menace #7 (September 1953), by writer Stan Lee and artist Joe Maneely, has been reprinted several times. A Frankenstein-like android fought the X-Men in X-Men #40 (January 1968), by Roy Thomas and Don Heck, and Silver Surfer #7 (August 1969), by Stan Lee and John Buscema, featured a descendant of Victor Frankenstein and a newly built creature. None of these stories was later treated as the first appearance of the mainstream character.

The Monster received his own title in the early 1970s, when a revision of the Comics Code permitted Marvel to publish horror series such as The Tomb of Dracula and Werewolf by Night. The Monster of Frankenstein #1 (January 1973) was written by Gary Friedrich and drawn by Mike Ploog, and the first four issues adapted the events of Shelley's novel within a framing sequence set in 1898. The series was retitled The Frankenstein Monster with issue #6 (October 1973). Ploog left the book after that issue and was followed by John Buscema, Val Mayerik and Bob Brown. Doug Moench replaced Friedrich as writer with issue #12 (September 1974) and moved the character into the present day. The series was cancelled with issue #18 (September 1975).

At the same time the character starred in a black-and-white serial in the magazine Monsters Unleashed, beginning in issue #2 (September 1973) and running intermittently through issue #10 (February 1975), written by Friedrich and Moench and drawn mainly by Mayerik. A further story appeared in the one-shot magazine Legion of Monsters #1 (September 1975). After the color series ended, the Monster was used as a guest character in Marvel Team-Up #36–37 (August–September 1975), Iron Man #101–102 (August–September 1977) and The Invaders #31 (August 1978). The 1973–1975 material was collected in Essential Monster of Frankenstein (2004). A separate adaptation of the novel was published in Marvel Classics Comics #20 (1977), which is not part of the character's continuity.

The character was revived periodically in later decades. He appeared in the Bloodstone limited series (December 2001 – March 2002) by Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning, which introduced Elsa Bloodstone, and he was a regular cast member of Nick Fury's Howling Commandos #1–6 (December 2005 – May 2006) by Keith Giffen. He took part in the "Franken-Castle" storyline in Punisher #11–16 (2009–2010) by Rick Remender, in the limited series Fear Itself: Fearsome Four (2011), and in Legion of Monsters (2011–2012) by Dennis Hopeless. The team book Howling Commandos of S.H.I.E.L.D. (2015–2016) by Frank Barbiere again featured him as a central character.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Origin ==

In 1788, the Swiss student Victor Frankenstein assembles a body from parts of corpses and brings it to life in his laboratory in Ingolstadt. Repelled by what he has made, he abandons the creature, who flees into the countryside and teaches himself to speak and read while secretly observing a peasant family. Rejected by everyone who sees him, the Monster kills Victor's younger brother and demands that his creator build him a mate. When Victor destroys the unfinished female, the Monster murders Victor's friend Henry Clerval and, on Victor's wedding night, his bride Elizabeth. Victor pursues the Monster into the Arctic, where he dies aboard the ship of the explorer Robert Walton. The Monster, mourning his creator, vows to destroy himself and disappears onto the ice, where he is trapped and preserved in a frozen state.

== 19th century ==

In 1898 the Monster's body is discovered by an expedition led by Robert Walton IV, a descendant of the original explorer who has spent much of his life searching for the creature. Thawed and revived, the Monster is at first unable to speak after a century in the ice. Walton dies soon afterward, and the Monster sets out to find any living relatives of Victor Frankenstein, hoping that his creator left behind the means of giving him a companion. His journey takes him across Europe, where he is attacked by villagers and briefly held captive and exhibited by a travelling circus.

In Transylvania the Monster encounters the vampire Dracula, who attempts to make use of him, and the two fight on more than one occasion. He later locates Vincent Frankenstein, a descendant of Victor, in Switzerland. Vincent is attempting to build a creature of his own and intends to transplant the brain of his hunchbacked assistant into the Monster's body. The Monster destroys Vincent's work, and Vincent is killed, although his wife gives birth to a child who continues the family line. Still alone, the Monster eventually returns to the far north, where he is frozen in the ice a second time.

== Modern era ==

The Monster is recovered in the 20th century and revived in New York City, where he is studied by scientists who regard him as a specimen rather than a person. He escapes and is befriended by a group of outcasts, among them a woman named Veronica Frankenstein, a modern descendant of Victor, who performs an operation that restores his power of speech. He comes into conflict with a corporation seeking to exploit his body and with a community of mutated children known as the Children of the Damned, who initially take him for their protector.

Shortly afterward, the Monster is captured by Baron Ludwig von Shtupf, a scientist who styles himself the Monster Maker and wants to combine the Monster's physiology with that of other beings. Von Shtupf also captures John Jameson, in his form as the Man-Wolf, and Spider-Man becomes involved while searching for Jameson. After an initial fight, Spider-Man, the Monster and the Man-Wolf escape together and destroy von Shtupf's facility, with the help of a S.H.I.E.L.D. agent assigned to the case. The Monster then departs, unwilling to remain among people.

The Monster later comes to the Frankenstein family castle in Switzerland, now the home of Victoria Frankenstein, a descendant who treats him kindly. When Iron Man arrives at the castle, he and the Monster are attacked by the Dreadknight, a former scientist whom Doctor Doom has transformed and who wants Victoria's research. Iron Man and the Monster defeat the Dreadknight together. A creature resembling the Monster, controlled by the Nazi collaborator Basil Frankenstein, had earlier fought the Invaders, including the Human Torch, Toro and Bucky, during the Second World War; this encounter is set before the Monster's revival in the present day.

In the following years the Monster, sometimes using the name Adam, becomes an ally of Ulysses Bloodstone, an immortal monster hunter, and after Bloodstone's death he acts as guardian and companion to Bloodstone's daughter Elsa, serving as the caretaker of Bloodstone Manor. He later accepts an invitation from S.H.I.E.L.D. to join its Howling Commandos, a unit made up of monsters and supernatural beings, and receives cybernetic enhancements to his body during his service. He subsequently joins the Legion of Monsters based in Monster Metropolis beneath Manhattan, alongside Werewolf by Night, Man-Thing and Morbius, and he fights beside She-Hulk, Howard the Duck and Nighthawk during the "Fear Itself" storyline, when the group tries to contain a rampaging Man-Thing.
