Delroy Garrett Jr. is a fictional superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Kurt Busiek and artist George Pérez, the character first appeared in The Avengers vol. 3 #8 (September 1998). A former Olympic sprinter who lost his medals in a doping scandal, Garrett was introduced as Triathlon, the public face of the Triune Understanding, a fictional religious movement, and was later added to the roster of the Avengers. He subsequently took the name 3-D Man, inheriting the identity of the 1950s hero Chuck Chandler, and has served with the Fifty State Initiative and the Agents of Atlas.

Garrett possesses roughly three times the strength, speed, stamina and agility of a peak human, an ability set he shares with the original 3-D Man. As 3-D Man he also wears a pair of goggles that allow him to see through the disguises of the shape-shifting Skrulls, a trait that was central to his role in the "Secret Invasion" storyline.

== Publication history ==

Delroy Garrett was created by writer Kurt Busiek and artist George Pérez as part of their relaunch of The Avengers following the "Heroes Return" event. He debuted in The Avengers vol. 3 #8 (September 1998) as Triathlon, an operative of the Triune Understanding, a self-help religion that Busiek had seeded as a slow-building subplot in the preceding issues.

Busiek later said the Triune Understanding storyline was intended to confront the Avengers with a problem that could not be solved by force, namely a public relations campaign that painted the team as elitist and discriminatory. Triathlon joined the Avengers in The Avengers vol. 3 #27 (April 2000) amid this pressure, and the character remained a regular presence through the "Kang Dynasty" arc. The subplot concluded in The Avengers vol. 3 #50–55 (2002), which exposed the cult's founder Jonathan Tremont as a villain and tied Triathlon's powers to those of 3-D Man, a Golden Age character who had recently been revived in the limited series Avengers Forever (1998–2000).

After Busiek left the series the character went largely unused for several years. He returned in Avengers: The Initiative, written by Dan Slott and Christos Gage, where he was assigned to the Hawaii-based Initiative team the Point Men. In Avengers: The Initiative #8 (February 2008) he formally adopted the 3-D Man name. The series' "Secret Invasion" tie-in issues (2008) gave the character a prominent role, since his goggles allowed him to identify Skrull infiltrators within the Initiative.

Writer Jeff Parker then added the character to the cast of Atlas (2010), a five-issue continuation of Agents of Atlas that examined the origin of the 3-D Man powers and connected them to the Uranian technology associated with Marvel Boy (Robert Grayson). Garrett has since appeared intermittently in a number of titles, including Deadpool and several Avengers-related series.

== Fictional character biography ==

Delroy Garrett Jr. is a track and field athlete from New Orleans who wins three gold medals at the Olympic Games, only to be stripped of them when a test reveals he used performance-enhancing drugs. Disgraced and without direction, he is drawn to the Triune Understanding, a movement that preaches the harmony of body, mind and spirit under the guidance of its charismatic founder Jonathan Tremont. Tremont, recognizing a useful symbol in the fallen athlete, secretly implants in Garrett a fragment of an alien power source that gives him three times the physical capability of a peak human. Garrett becomes the movement's public champion under the name Triathlon, believing his abilities to be the product of Triune teachings.

== Avengers ==

Triathlon first encounters the Avengers while they are fighting Moses Magnum, and although he assists them he treats the team with open suspicion. At the same time Tremont's followers orchestrate a campaign accusing the Avengers of bigotry, pointing to the small number of Black heroes who have held full membership. The team's government liaison, Duane Freeman, urges the Avengers to blunt the criticism, and Triathlon is added to the active roster along with the newcomer Silverclaw. His appointment is resented by several members, most notably Warbird (Carol Danvers), and Triathlon himself bristles at the suggestion that he has been recruited as a token rather than on merit.

In the storyline "Kang Dynasty", Triathlon fights alongside the Avengers against the forces of Kang the Conqueror and takes part in the defense of Washington, D.C. His conduct during the war earns him the respect of teammates who had earlier doubted him, and he gradually sheds his defensiveness. He nevertheless remains loyal to the Triune Understanding and continues to defend Tremont against the Avengers' misgivings.

The deception unravels when Tremont summons the Avengers to help him confront the Triple-Evil, a cosmic entity approaching Earth. Tremont has gathered three shards of a single power source: one he gave to Triathlon, one he kept for himself, and one he stole from Hal Chandler, the brother of the original 3-D Man, whose abilities derived from the same energy. Tremont intends to absorb the Triple-Evil's power and remake the world in his own image. Learning that his mentor has manipulated him from the beginning, Triathlon turns against Tremont and, with the help of the Avengers and the Chandler brothers, takes command of all three shards and defeats him. Shaken by the revelations, he leaves the team shortly afterwards.

== 3-D Man ==

Following the events of "Civil War", Garrett registers under the Superhuman Registration Act and is posted to the Point Men, the Initiative team for Hawaii, serving alongside Devil-Slayer, Magnitude, Paydirt and Star Sign. Having cut his ties with the Triune Understanding, he is visited by Hal Chandler, who presents him with the goggles that once allowed Hal to summon his brother Chuck as 3-D Man. Garrett accepts the goggles and the 3-D Man name in honor of the brothers, retiring the Triathlon identity.

== "Secret Invasion" ==

When Garrett puts on the goggles he discovers that they allow him to see Skrulls in their true form, and he realizes that his teammate Magnitude is an impostor. The Skrull is killed in the ensuing fight, and Devil-Slayer teleports Garrett to the mainland as the full Skrull invasion begins. Unable to reach the Avengers, he joins forces with Ryder and Riot of the Skrull Kill Krew, and the group travels from state to state exposing and eliminating the Skrulls who have infiltrated the Initiative's teams.

Arriving at Camp Hammond, Garrett identifies Thor Girl as a Skrull sleeper agent, prompting a battle in which the impostor is destroyed. In the aftermath, his goggles reveal that Crusader, who fought on the side of the humans throughout the invasion, is also a Skrull, and Garrett kills him before anyone can intervene. The act troubles his colleagues, several of whom regarded Crusader as a hero, and leaves Garrett uncertain about whether he can trust his own judgment. The genuine Thor Girl is later recovered alive from a Skrull vessel.

== Agents of Atlas ==

Garrett begins experiencing dreams that compel him to seek out the Agents of Atlas, a covert team led by Jimmy Woo whose members include Gorilla-Man, Namora, Venus, M-11 and Marvel Boy (Robert Grayson). Grayson examines the goggles and the energy that empowers Garrett and determines that both are linked to Uranian science, offering the first scientific account of the powers that had passed from the Chandlers to Tremont and then to Garrett. Garrett joins the team, adopts a new costume, and helps the Agents confront an alien infestation that has begun converting humans into duplicates, a crisis that the 3-D Man powers prove uniquely suited to resolving. He develops a close rapport with Namora during this period.

After the Agents of Atlas disband, Garrett continues to operate as 3-D Man, answering calls for assistance from the Avengers and other heroes during subsequent crises and occasionally crossing paths with Deadpool. He remains one of the few heroes able to detect disguised Skrulls, a capability that keeps him in demand whenever the shape-shifters resurface.
