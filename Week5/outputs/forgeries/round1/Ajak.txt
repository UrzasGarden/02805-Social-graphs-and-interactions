Ajak is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer and artist Jack Kirby and first appeared in The Eternals #2 (August 1976). Ajak is a member of the Eternals, a race of long-lived superhumans engineered by the Celestials, and serves as the group's chosen intermediary with its creators. The character was later reworked as a woman in the 2021 Eternals series and was portrayed by Salma Hayek in the Marvel Cinematic Universe film Eternals (2021).

== Publication history ==

Ajak debuted in the second issue of Jack Kirby's The Eternals and appeared regularly throughout that series until its cancellation in 1978. The character returned in the Thor storyline dealing with the Fourth Host of the Celestials and was given a central role in the twelve-issue Eternals limited series (1985–1986) by Peter B. Gillis and Sal Buscema, which depicted his transformation and death. Ajak was revived by Neil Gaiman and John Romita Jr. in Eternals (2006–2007) and remained part of the cast in the follow-up ongoing series by Charles and Daniel Knauf. In Eternals (2021) by Kieron Gillen and Esad Ribić, the character was reintroduced as female.

== Fictional character biography ==

Ajak is one of the Polar Eternals and is among the oldest of his race. In antiquity he was worshipped by the Incas as Tecumotzin, and he is sometimes said to have inspired the Greek hero Ajax. He is noted for his ability to speak directly with the Celestials, and when the Fourth Host arrives on Earth he is the Eternal who greets them in the Andes, accompanied by the archaeologist Daniel Damian and his daughter Margo. During this period Ajak works with Ikaris, Makkari and Thena, and the Eternals cross paths with the Olympian Hercules, whom they once impersonated as gods.

After the Celestials depart, Ajak remains in the City of the Space Gods with Damian. The Deviant priest Ghaur uses his mental powers to twist Ajak into a monstrous form, and in his despair the Eternal destroys both himself and Damian. He is eventually restored by the Celestials. In the storyline "Eternals" (2006), the Dreaming Celestial awakens beneath San Francisco and the Eternals, who have forgotten their identities, slowly recover their memories. Ajak resents that the Celestial has selected Makkari rather than himself as its voice, which leads him to betray his fellow Eternals and attack Makkari.

In the 2021 series, Ajak is depicted as a woman who holds the office of Speaker to the Celestials and leads the Eternals' priesthood. She is killed and resurrected repeatedly through the Eternals' machine Exclusion, and later discovers the truth that every resurrection costs a human life. During the "Judgment Day" crossover, Ajak attempts to communicate with the Progenitor and ultimately merges with Makkari to become a new Celestial.

== Powers and abilities ==

Like all Eternals, Ajak possesses superhuman strength, durability and longevity, and his body regenerates from nearly any injury. He can fly, project cosmic energy from his eyes and hands, teleport, and rearrange matter at the molecular level. Ajak is distinguished by his capacity to understand and communicate with the Celestials, and he can operate Celestial technology that is beyond the comprehension of other Eternals. His mind is vulnerable to Deviant telepaths, who have twisted him into a monstrous shape.

== Other versions ==

In Earth X, Ajak is among the Eternals who oppose the Celestials when their true purpose is revealed. In the Marvel Zombies universe, an infected version of Ajak appears alongside other Eternals.

== In other media ==

Ajak appears in the Marvel Knights motion comic Eternals, voiced by Pat Fry. Ajak, reimagined as a woman, appears in the live-action film Eternals (2021), portrayed by Salma Hayek. In the film, Ajak is the Prime Eternal who leads the group on Earth and is killed by Deviants before the events of the story.
