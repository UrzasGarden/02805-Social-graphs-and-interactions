Black Bolt (Blackagar Boltagon) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby and first appeared in Fantastic Four #45 (December 1965). Black Bolt is the king of the Inhumans, a genetically altered offshoot of humanity, and the ruler of their hidden city of Attilan. He is the husband of Medusa, the brother of Maximus, and a member of the Inhuman Royal Family alongside Gorgon, Karnak, Triton, Crystal and the teleporting dog Lockjaw. His defining trait is a voice so destructive that he almost never speaks, even a whisper being capable of levelling a city, and he communicates through sign language and through Medusa, who acts as his interpreter.

Black Bolt has been a member of the Illuminati, a secret group of powerful figures who shape events behind the scenes, and has frequently been depicted in conflict with the Kree, the Skrulls and the human governments of Earth. The character has appeared in several animated series and video games and was portrayed by Anson Mount in the live-action television series Inhumans (2017) and in the film Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022), set in the Marvel Cinematic Universe.

== Publication history ==

Black Bolt debuted alongside most of the Inhuman Royal Family in Fantastic Four #45 (December 1965), during the Stan Lee and Jack Kirby run on the title, although Medusa had been introduced several issues earlier as a member of the Frightful Four. The Inhumans were introduced through the romance between the Human Torch and Crystal, and Black Bolt was initially presented as a mysterious, mute figure whose nature was gradually explained over the following issues. His origin and the reason for his silence were revealed in Fantastic Four #59 (February 1967). The Inhumans were given a backup feature in Thor #146–152 (November 1967–May 1968), written by Lee and drawn by Kirby, which elaborated on the history of Attilan and the Terrigen Mist.

The characters received their first ongoing feature in Amazing Adventures #1–10 (August 1970–January 1972), which they shared with the Black Widow. Kirby wrote and drew the early installments before the strip was taken over by Roy Thomas and Neal Adams. The Inhumans received their own title with The Inhumans #1–12 (October 1975–August 1977), written for most of its run by Doug Moench with art by George Pérez and Gil Kane. Following its cancellation the Royal Family returned to the pages of Fantastic Four, and in Fantastic Four #240 (March 1982), John Byrne relocated Attilan to the Blue Area of the Moon. A graphic novel, The Inhumans, by Ann Nocenti and Bret Blevins, was published in 1988 and depicted the birth of Black Bolt and Medusa's son Ahura.

Black Bolt was a central figure in Inhumans (vol. 2) #1–12 (November 1998–October 1999), a Marvel Knights limited series by writer Paul Jenkins and artist Jae Lee that won the 1999 Eisner Award for Best New Series. It was followed by Inhumans (vol. 3) #1–4 (2000) by Carlos Pacheco and Rafael Marín and Inhumans (vol. 4) #1–12 (2003–2004) by Sean McKeever. In the 2000s the character became a regular presence in the wider Marvel line, appearing as a founding member of the Illuminati in New Avengers: Illuminati (May 2006) by Brian Michael Bendis and Alex Maleev, and as the lead in Son of M (2006) and Silent War #1–6 (2007), the latter by David Hine and Frazer Irving. He featured prominently in the crossovers "World War Hulk" (2007), "Secret Invasion" (2008) and "War of Kings" (2009), the last of which was written by Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning and ended with the character's apparent death.

Black Bolt returned in Fantastic Four #600 (January 2012) by Jonathan Hickman and subsequently appeared in Hickman's New Avengers (vol. 3) and the crossover "Infinity" (2013), which led into the "Inhumanity" branding and the ongoing series Inhuman (2014–2015) and Uncanny Inhumans (2015–2017) by Charles Soule. The character received his first solo ongoing title with Black Bolt #1–12 (July 2017–June 2018) by writer Saladin Ahmed and artist Christian Ward, which won the 2018 Eisner Award for Best New Series. He next appeared in the limited series Death of the Inhumans (2018) by Donny Cates and Ariel Olivetti.

== Fictional character biography ==

Blackagar Boltagon is the son of Agon, head of Attilan's ruling Genetic Council, and his wife Rynda. His parents expose him to the mutagenic Terrigen Mist while he is still in the womb, and he is born with powers far beyond those of other Inhumans, including a voice capable of causing enormous destruction. As an infant he is placed in a soundproof chamber and fitted with an energy-absorbing harness, and he spends his childhood learning to control his abilities and never to utter a sound. He is released from confinement at the age of nineteen and takes a vow of silence.

Shortly after his release, Black Bolt discovers that his brother Maximus has been secretly negotiating with emissaries of the Kree, the alien race that created the Inhumans. In an attempt to prevent a Kree ship from escaping, Black Bolt uses his voice for the first time. The vessel crashes into the parliament building, killing Agon and Rynda and several members of the Genetic Council, while the sound drives Maximus insane. Black Bolt assumes the throne of Attilan and later marries his cousin Medusa, who becomes his queen and the voice of his decisions.

Maximus repeatedly attempts to usurp the throne. During one such coup, Medusa is struck with amnesia and separated from the Royal Family, and her later membership in the Frightful Four brings the Inhumans into contact with the Fantastic Four. When the Human Torch follows Crystal to the Inhumans' hidden refuge, Black Bolt and his family are forced into the open. Maximus seals Attilan behind an impenetrable "negative barrier", which Black Bolt eventually shatters with his voice, ending the Inhumans' isolation from the outside world. In the years that follow, Black Bolt allies with the Fantastic Four, the Avengers and the Hulk against threats to Attilan, and he permits Crystal's marriage to the mutant Quicksilver.

With pollution in the outside world increasingly dangerous to Inhuman physiology, Black Bolt oversees the relocation of Attilan from the Himalayas to the Blue Area of the Moon. He and Medusa conceive a child in defiance of the Genetic Council, which fears the offspring of two members of the Royal Family; Medusa flees to Earth to give birth to their son, Ahura, who is subsequently taken into the council's custody. During the "Atlantis Rising" storyline, Attilan is forcibly returned to Earth by the sorceress Morgan le Fay, and Black Bolt works with the Fantastic Four, whose membership at that time includes Kristoff Vernard, to restore order before the city is eventually returned to the Moon.

In the storyline written by Paul Jenkins, Black Bolt confronts a crisis in which Maximus manipulates the lower castes of Attilan, the Alpha Primitives, and provokes a human military invasion of the city. Black Bolt refuses to intervene until the last possible moment, then speaks a single word that devastates the attacking forces, after which he leads the Inhumans into space in search of a new home. The Royal Family later returns to the Moon, and Black Bolt is forced to send Inhuman youths to attend a human university under the terms of a treaty, an arrangement that collapses after the death of one of the students.

Following the events of "House of M", in which the mutant population of Earth is drastically reduced, Quicksilver travels to Attilan and steals the Terrigen Crystals in the hope of restoring his own powers and those of other depowered mutants. The crystals are seized by the United States government, and in the storyline "Silent War" Black Bolt formally declares war on the United States to recover them. The conflict ends when Maximus, imprisoned on the Moon, takes control of Black Bolt's mind and installs himself as ruler of Attilan, with the king held captive in his own palace.

Black Bolt is one of the founding members of the Illuminati, together with Iron Man, Mister Fantastic, Doctor Strange, Namor and Professor X, and is party to the decision to exile the Hulk from Earth. When the Hulk returns in "World War Hulk", he travels first to the Moon and defeats Black Bolt in combat before attacking the Illuminati on Earth and ultimately battling the Sentry. It is later revealed in New Avengers: Illuminati #5 (January 2008) that the Black Bolt defeated by the Hulk, and present at the group's recent meetings, had been a Skrull impostor. The genuine Black Bolt is found imprisoned aboard a Skrull vessel during "Secret Invasion", where the Skrulls have attempted to harness his voice as a weapon. After his rescue, Black Bolt declares that the Inhumans will no longer live at the mercy of alien powers, and he leads an assault on the Skrull Empire before turning Attilan into a starship and conquering the Kree.
