Kristoff Vernard is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer-artist John Byrne, the character first appeared in Fantastic Four #247 (October 1982). A Latverian orphan taken in by Doctor Doom, Kristoff was later imprinted with Doom's memories and personality, and for a time believed himself to be Doom. Under the names Kristoff von Doom and Doctor Doom, he served as an antagonist of the Fantastic Four before regaining his own identity and becoming an ally and occasional member of the team.

The character has also been referred to as Kristoff Vernard and, during his time as heir to the Latverian throne, as Kristoff von Doom. He is one of the few people whom Doom has regarded as a legitimate successor.

== Publication history ==

Kristoff Vernard was introduced by John Byrne in Fantastic Four #247 (October 1982), the issue in which Doctor Doom returned to Latveria to retake the throne from Zorba. Byrne used the boy as a device to show a more sympathetic side of Doom, who adopted the orphaned child as his ward. Following Doom's apparent death in Fantastic Four #260 (November 1983), Byrne returned to the character in Fantastic Four #278 (May 1985), in which Doom's robotic servants transferred their master's memories into the boy. The storyline continued into Fantastic Four #279 (June 1985), which ended with the destruction of the Baxter Building.

The character remained in use after Byrne left the title. Steve Englehart and later Walt Simonson depicted Kristoff as a rival claimant to Latveria, with the two Dooms contesting the throne in Fantastic Four #350 (March 1991). During Tom DeFalco and Paul Ryan's run in the mid-1990s, Kristoff was freed of Doom's persona and joined the Fantastic Four's household, appearing regularly from Fantastic Four #396 (January 1995) until the series was suspended during the "Onslaught" crossover in 1996.

Kristoff appeared only sporadically in the following years. He returned in Jonathan Hickman's FF series in 2012, in which he ruled Latveria in Doom's absence, and in Christopher Cantwell's Doctor Doom series (2019–2021), which again placed him at the head of the Latverian government while Doom was in exile.

== Fictional character biography ==

Kristoff Vernard is born in the small European nation of Latveria. During the brief reign of Prince Zorba, who overthrows Doctor Doom with the help of the Fantastic Four, the country falls into disorder. Kristoff's mother is killed by Zorba's robotic enforcers while Doom and the Fantastic Four move against the regime. Doom, who has returned to reclaim his country, takes the orphaned boy under his protection and later names him his ward and heir, intending that Kristoff should one day rule Latveria.

When Doom is seemingly killed in a battle with Terrax, his robot servants carry out a contingency order. They place Kristoff in a machine that imprints him with Doom's memories, stopping at the point just before Doom's first attempt to destroy the Fantastic Four by launching the Baxter Building into space. Convinced that he is Doom and that his previous plan failed only through poor timing, the boy builds a suit of armor fitted to his size and repeats the attack. He succeeds in hurling the Baxter Building into orbit and detonating it, though the Fantastic Four, who at the time include She-Hulk in place of the Thing, escape. Kristoff is captured and held in a stasis cell at the team's new headquarters, Four Freedoms Plaza.

Kristoff eventually escapes and returns to Latveria, where he governs as Doom with the support of Doom's robots. When the real Doom resurfaces, the two contest the throne, each claiming to be the legitimate ruler and each supported by Doombots unable to decide between them. Doom ultimately prevails, and Kristoff is driven from power.

Kristoff later falls into the hands of Nathaniel Richards, the father of Reed Richards, who deactivates the imprinted personality and restores the boy's own mind. With Reed believed dead, Kristoff takes up residence with the Fantastic Four and uses his inherited scientific knowledge and armor in their service. He develops a friendship with the young Franklin Richards and often works alongside the Human Torch, who initially distrusts him because of his attack on the Baxter Building. Kristoff remains with the team until the events of "Onslaught", after which he returns to Latveria.

In the storyline "Tomorrow", Kristoff is shown governing Latveria during a period in which Doom has been absent, and he yields the throne when Doom returns in the company of Reed Richards' Future Foundation, whose ranks then include Spider-Man. Later, when Doom is framed for an attack on the Moon and forced into exile, Kristoff once more assumes control of the Latverian state until Doom clears his name.

== Powers and abilities ==

Kristoff Vernard has no superhuman powers of his own. As a result of the memory transfer performed by Doom's robots, he possesses much of Doctor Doom's scientific and technological knowledge, including an advanced understanding of robotics, weaponry and time travel. He retained this knowledge after his own personality was restored. He is also an able strategist and has training in sorcery, although he is far less accomplished in the mystic arts than Doom.

In combat Kristoff wears a suit of armor modeled on Doom's, scaled to his own body. The armor provides superhuman strength and durability, flight by means of jets, force fields and concussive energy blasts, and it is equipped with sensors and communication systems linking him to the Latverian robot forces.

== Other versions ==

In the Marvel 2099 setting, a descendant of the Vernard line is not established, but the Doom of that era refers to the house's claim to the throne. In the "Heroes Reborn" universe, no counterpart of Kristoff appears, and Doom has no designated heir.

In the "Secret Wars" storyline of 2015, Battleworld is ruled by Doom as God Emperor. Kristoff does not appear, but a version of Doom's household survives on the planet, and later stories set after the restoration of the universe depict Kristoff again as the heir presumptive of Latveria.

== Reception ==

Kristoff has generally been discussed in relation to Doctor Doom rather than as a character in his own right. In 2018, CBR.com included him in a list of characters who had worn Doom's armor, noting that his storyline was among the few in which Doom showed genuine affection for another person. In 2020, Screen Rant listed him among the Fantastic Four's lesser-known members.
