Blackthorn (Aline Pagrovna) is a fictional superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Peter B. Gillis and artist Brent Anderson and first appeared in Strikeforce: Morituri #1 (December 1986). She is one of the original members of the Strikeforce: Morituri, a team of volunteers who undergo an experimental procedure that grants superhuman powers at the cost of certain death within a year.

Unlike most Marvel characters, Blackthorn does not exist in the mainstream Marvel Universe. She belongs to the self-contained near-future setting of Strikeforce: Morituri, in which Earth has been invaded by an alien race known as the Horde. Within the series she is closely associated with her teammate Radian (Louis Armanetti), with whom she becomes romantically involved.

== Publication history ==

Blackthorn debuted in the first issue of Strikeforce: Morituri, a creator-driven series launched by Marvel in late 1986 under editor Carl Potts. Writer Peter B. Gillis conceived the book around the premise that its heroes would die within a year of gaining their powers, which meant that the cast would be continually replaced over the course of the run. Gillis and penciller Brent Anderson designed the initial team of six, of which Aline Pagrovna was one.

The character appeared regularly from Strikeforce: Morituri #1 through the first year of the series, which was published monthly and set in the year 2072. Because the premise required that characters die, Gillis gave each of the original members a limited arc, and Blackthorn was among the earliest casualties after Vyking and Snapdragon. Her final appearance in the main narrative came in 1987.

Strikeforce: Morituri ran for 31 issues before its cancellation in 1989. A sequel limited series, Strikeforce: Morituri: Electric Undertow, written by James D. Hudnall and drawn by Mark Bagley, was published in 1989 and 1990 and was set several years after the original. Blackthorn was by then long dead within the story and did not appear. The original series was later collected in trade paperback form, reprinting her appearances.

== Fictional character biography ==

Aline Pagrovna is a Soviet-born woman living in the late 21st century, a period in which Earth has been under attack for several years by the Horde, a nomadic alien race that raids the planet for plunder and sport. Humanity's conventional forces are unable to repel the invaders, and the Paideia, the world government, sponsors a project led by the geneticist Dr. Kimmo Tuolema to produce superhuman defenders. The procedure, known as the Morituri Process, is fatal within roughly one year of treatment, and only about one in twenty candidates is compatible with it.

Pagrovna volunteers for the program and is among the small group of survivors who emerge with powers. She joins the first operational team alongside Harold Everson (Vyking), Lorna Raeburn (Snapdragon), Jelene Anderson (Adept), Robert Greenbaum (Marathon), and Louis Armanetti (Radian). Taking the code name Blackthorn, she discovers that she can alter the structure of inanimate matter by touch, reshaping and hardening it into weapons and barriers. Her practical temperament and rural upbringing distinguish her from the more idealistic members of the group.

The team's first engagements with the Horde are only partly successful. Vyking is killed in action during an early raid, and the group struggles with the knowledge that their time is limited. During this period Blackthorn and Radian grow close, and the two begin a relationship that is portrayed as one of the few sources of comfort available to the doomed volunteers. Blackthorn also acts as a moderating influence on Radian, whose aggression toward the Horde frequently places the team at risk.

As the series continues, the Strikeforce is used by the Paideia for publicity as much as for combat, and the members become celebrities. Blackthorn is uncomfortable with this role and prefers field duty. Snapdragon dies after the team is sent against a Horde landing party, and new recruits begin to replace the fallen. Blackthorn takes part in several missions against Horde raiding ships and demonstrates her abilities by converting wreckage and terrain into improvised fortifications.

Radian is killed in a confrontation with the Horde when his energy powers burn beyond his control, an event that leaves Blackthorn grieving and increasingly fatalistic. Shortly afterward the Horde locate and attack the Morituri base in an attempt to destroy the program at its source. Blackthorn fights in the defense of the facility and is killed during the assault, her body giving way to the Morituri effect under the strain of extended use of her powers. Her death leaves Adept and Marathon as the only surviving members of the original team.

== Powers and abilities ==

As a result of the Morituri Process, Blackthorn possesses the ability to transmute and reshape non-living matter through physical contact. She can change the density and form of materials such as metal, stone, and soil, drawing them into spikes, blades, and shields, and can render solid objects brittle enough to shatter. Her power is limited by the amount of material within reach and by the physical toll that prolonged use places on her body.

Like all recipients of the Morituri Process, she is slightly stronger and more resilient than an ordinary human, and like all of them she is condemned to die within approximately one year of receiving her powers, as the process eventually causes a fatal breakdown of the subject's body. She has no formal combat training before joining the program but becomes a capable fighter through experience in the field.

== Reception ==

Blackthorn has received little individual attention from critics, who have generally discussed Strikeforce: Morituri as a whole. Reviewers have cited the series as an early example of Marvel's willingness to kill off protagonists as a central premise rather than as an occasional event, and the relationship between Blackthorn and Radian has been noted as one of the storylines that gave the early issues their emotional weight. In retrospectives on the series, writers for comics websites have described the original team's deaths, including Blackthorn's, as effective because the characters were given distinct personalities before being removed from the narrative.
