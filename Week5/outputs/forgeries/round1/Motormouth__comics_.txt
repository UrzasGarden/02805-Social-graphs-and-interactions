Motormouth (Harley Davis) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Graham Marshall and artist Gary Frank, the character first appeared in Motormouth #1 (June 1992), published through the company's British imprint Marvel UK. A homeless teenager from London, Harley Davis comes into possession of a pair of experimental dimension-hopping boots belonging to the corporation Mys-Tech, and is later given an artificial sonic scream by the same organisation. She is most closely associated with the genetically engineered super-soldier Killpower, with whom she shared her title for most of its run.

== Publication history ==

Motormouth was launched in 1992 as part of Marvel UK's expansion into the American direct market under editor-in-chief Paul Neary. Like the imprint's other titles of the period, including Hell's Angel and Warheads, the series was tied to the shared Mys-Tech storyline, in which a cabal of immortal financiers controlled a London-based technology corporation. The first issue was written by Graham Marshall and drawn by Gary Frank, with Frank leaving after the early issues to work on The Incredible Hulk for Marvel's American line.

The series was retitled Motormouth & Killpower with issue #7 (December 1992), reflecting the growing prominence of the second lead. Killpower, a childlike artificial being in an adult body, had been introduced in Motormouth #2 and quickly became a fixture of the book. Guest appearances by established Marvel characters were a regular feature intended to attract American readers; Nick Fury, the Punisher and the Hulk all appeared during the run. The title ended with issue #12 (May 1993) as Marvel UK scaled back its line in the face of a shrinking market.

Both characters subsequently appeared in the crossover Mys-Tech Wars #1–4 (1993) alongside Doctor Strange, the X-Men and other heroes, and in the Battletide sequels. After a long absence, Motormouth returned in Revolutionary War: Motormouth #1 (March 2014), a one-shot written by Glenn Dakin with art by Ronan Cliquet, as part of the "Revolutionary War" event that revived the Marvel UK characters of the early 1990s. She also appeared in the event's concluding chapter, Revolutionary War: Omega #1 (April 2014).

== Fictional character biography ==

Harley Davis is a teenage runaway living on the streets of London, where she survives by petty theft and is known for her constant, profanity-laden talk. While hiding from the police she witnesses a Mys-Tech operative testing a prototype pair of boots that allow the wearer to open portals between dimensions. Harley steals the boots, unaware of what they are, and finds herself transported across a series of alien and alternate worlds whenever she attempts to run. Mys-Tech, unwilling to lose the technology, dispatches agents to retrieve both the boots and the thief.

During her flight Harley encounters Killpower (Julius Mullarkey), a Mys-Tech creation with immense physical strength but the mind and emotional development of a small child. Ordered to capture her, Killpower instead becomes attached to Harley, who treats him with a mixture of exasperation and genuine affection. The two become inseparable, with Harley acting as an older sister figure and attempting to shield him from the people who built him. Their escape attracts the attention of Nick Fury, who sees the boots as a security concern and briefly pursues the pair before concluding that Mys-Tech is the greater threat.

Harley is eventually captured by Mys-Tech, whose scientists subject her to surgery as punishment and experiment. Her vocal cords are replaced with a synthetic larynx that gives her the ability to generate a destructive sonic scream, and it is from this that she takes the name Motormouth. Freed by Killpower, she turns the new ability against her captors. In the following issues the pair travel between worlds, at one point arriving in the United States, where a misunderstanding leads to a confrontation with the Hulk before the three unite against a Mys-Tech strike team.

Harley's uncontrolled jumps through dimensions also carry her through time. In one storyline she is stranded in post-war Europe and crosses paths with Peggy Carter, who helps her evade a precursor of the Mys-Tech organisation before Harley is able to return to her own era. Later, in "Mys-Tech Wars", Motormouth and Killpower join a large gathering of heroes, including Doctor Strange, Captain Britain and the X-Men, in an assault on the Mys-Tech board that leaves many of the participants dead until the timeline is reset.

Following the end of her own series Harley settles into a quieter life, though her connection to Killpower leaves her a target. In Revolutionary War: Motormouth, set some twenty years later, Harley is a single mother in London raising a young daughter. She has largely abandoned her old identity, but when Mys-Tech resurfaces and attempts to reactivate its dormant assets, she discovers that the corporation has been monitoring her family. Reluctantly taking up the boots once more, she reunites with the surviving Marvel UK heroes and is instrumental in locating Killpower, who had been believed lost. In Revolutionary War: Omega she helps turn Killpower against his former masters during the final battle at Mys-Tech headquarters.

== Powers and abilities ==

Harley Davis has no innate superhuman powers. Her primary advantage is the pair of Mys-Tech boots she stole, which allow her to open gateways to other dimensions and, less predictably, to other points in time. In her early appearances she has little control over the destination, and the jumps are often triggered by stress or by physical exertion. Over time she learns to direct them with greater accuracy, though the technology remains unreliable and occasionally strands her for extended periods.

The synthetic larynx implanted by Mys-Tech gives her a sonic scream capable of shattering stone and metal and of incapacitating opponents at close range. The scream can be focused into a narrow blast or released as a wide shockwave. Prolonged use causes her physical pain and leaves her temporarily unable to speak. Harley is also an experienced street fighter and pickpocket, skills developed during her years living rough, and she possesses a sharp, confrontational wit that frequently serves as a weapon in itself.

== Other versions ==

An alternate version of Harley Davis appears in the "Battletide" storyline, where she is shown among the combatants drawn from across the multiverse. A future version of the character, aged and hardened, is glimpsed briefly in Motormouth & Killpower #11 (April 1993) during one of Harley's unintended time jumps, warning her younger self against trusting Mys-Tech.

== Reception ==

Motormouth was one of the more commercially successful launches of Marvel UK's 1992 line, and the character has been cited by commentators as a distinctive example of the imprint's attempt to blend British street culture with American superhero conventions. In 2014, Comic Book Resources described her revival in "Revolutionary War" as one of the stronger entries in the event, noting the contrast between the brash teenager of the original series and the weary adult of the one-shot. In 2019, CBR.com included Motormouth in a list of Marvel UK characters it considered deserving of a return to publication.
