Fandral the Dashing is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, the character first appeared in Journey into Mystery #119 (August 1965). Fandral is an Asgardian warrior and a member of the Warriors Three, together with Hogun the Grim and Volstagg the Voluminous, a trio of adventurers who serve as companions and allies of the thunder god Thor. He is portrayed as a swashbuckling swordsman whose look and manner were patterned on the film actor Errol Flynn.

The character has appeared in several adaptations of Thor in other media. Josh Dallas played Fandral in the Marvel Cinematic Universe film Thor (2011), and Zachary Levi took over the role for Thor: The Dark World (2013) and Thor: Ragnarok (2017).

== Publication history ==

Fandral debuted alongside Hogun and Volstagg in the "Tales of Asgard" backup feature of Journey into Mystery #119 (August 1965), written by Stan Lee and pencilled by Jack Kirby. The three were introduced as members of a crew that Thor assembles for a sea voyage to learn the cause of a crack in the Oversword of Asgard. The backup feature gave Lee and Kirby room to build a supporting cast for Thor out of their own invention rather than Norse myth, and the trio, soon known as the Warriors Three, have no counterparts in the Eddas.

Lee later described the three as deliberate contrasts: the fat and boastful Volstagg, the taciturn Hogun, and Fandral as a cheerful swordsman in the manner of Errol Flynn's screen roles, in particular The Adventures of Robin Hood (1938). Kirby's design, with blond hair, a pointed beard and a green and gold costume, reinforced the resemblance, and later artists kept it largely intact.

When the "Tales of Asgard" feature ended, Fandral and his companions moved into the lead stories of the series, which had been retitled Thor with issue #126 (March 1966). They appeared regularly through the remainder of the Lee and Kirby run and in the issues that followed by writers Gerry Conway, Len Wein and Roy Thomas and artists John Buscema and Walter Simonson. The Warriors Three received a solo outing in Marvel Spotlight #30 (October 1976), written by Wein and drawn by John Buscema, and headlined a four-part serial in Marvel Fanfare #34–37 (September 1987–March 1988), written by Alan Zelenetz and illustrated by Charles Vess, which presented Fandral as the historical basis for the legend of Robin Hood.

Fandral was among the Asgardians killed in the "Ragnarok" storyline in Thor #80–85 (2004), written by Michael Avon Oeming and Daniel Berman with art by Andrea Di Vito. Oeming subsequently wrote the character in the miniseries Thor: Blood Oath #1–6 (2005), with art by Scott Kolins, which was set early in the trio's career. Fandral returned with the rest of Asgard's population in Thor vol. 3 (2007) by J. Michael Straczynski and Olivier Coipel, and the trio starred in Warriors Three #1–4 (2011), by Bill Willingham and Neil Edwards. He has since appeared in Thor titles written by Kieron Gillen, Matt Fraction and Jason Aaron, including the 2019 crossover "War of the Realms".

== Fictional character biography ==

Fandral is born in Asgard and grows up to become one of the realm's finest swordsmen. In his youth he befriends Hogun, a grim warrior from a distant land, and the enormous Volstagg, and the three become inseparable comrades. In the storyline "Blood Oath", set in this early period, the trio and the young Thor kill the son of a giant while hunting and are sentenced by Odin to complete a series of quests across the Nine Realms to pay the blood-debt, a task they accomplish only after Fandral and the others nearly come to ruin through their own impulsiveness.

Their reputation brings them to the attention of Thor, who enlists them for an expedition ordered by Odin to learn why a crack has appeared in the Oversword, the giant blade whose drawing is prophesied to begin Ragnarok. On the voyage the crew suffer a mutiny instigated by Loki and face a succession of monsters and sorcerers before reaching their destination. Fandral distinguishes himself in the fighting, and from this point on he, Hogun and Volstagg are counted among Thor's closest friends.

The Warriors Three take part in most of the major conflicts of Asgard in the years that follow, including the wars against the storm giants, the invasions of Surtur and the schemes of Loki and the Enchantress. They often accompany Thor to Earth. On one such visit the three become stranded in New York City, where they are arrested after a brawl and must stand trial in a Manhattan courtroom, an episode played largely for comedy. Fandral's fondness for women and wine is a recurring source of trouble for the group, usually smoothed over by Hogun's severity and Volstagg's bluster.

During a stay in medieval England, recounted in Marvel Fanfare, Fandral is separated from his companions and takes up with a band of outlaws in Sherwood Forest. His skill with a bow and his habit of robbing the wealthy lead the local population to remember him as Robin Hood, and the story suggests that the English legend grew out of his exploits. Fandral himself regards the matter with amusement and rarely mentions it.

Fandral has on occasion fought beside Thor against the Hulk, whose rampages have brought him into conflict with Asgard's defenders more than once, and the Warriors Three have found themselves outmatched by the Hulk's strength even when fighting as a group. Fandral is also a longtime friend of Balder the Brave, and the two frequently serve together in Asgard's armies.

In the "Ragnarok" storyline, Loki forges an arsenal of hammers for his followers and leads a final assault on Asgard. Fandral fights in the defence of the city and is slain, along with Hogun, during the retreat. Volstagg survives long enough to see Thor break the cycle of Ragnarok by destroying the loom of the Norns, after which Asgard and all its inhabitants pass out of existence.

Years later Thor returns from the void and rebuilds Asgard above the plains near Broxton, Oklahoma. He discovers that the souls of the Asgardians have been reborn in the bodies of mortals scattered across the Earth and sets out to find them. Fandral is among those Thor locates and restores, and he resumes his place among the Warriors Three in the new city. When Loki manipulates Balder into assuming the throne and relocating Asgard to Latveria, Fandral follows his king but shares the misgivings of Thor and Sif about the alliance with Doctor Doom. He later fights in the defence of Asgard when Norman Osborn leads an attack on the city during the "Siege" storyline.

In "Fear Itself", Fandral joins the Asgardian forces against the Serpent, Odin's brother, and his Worthy. Following the destruction of the Oklahoma city he settles in the floating city of Asgardia. In Jason Aaron's run he serves under the All-Mother, the council of Freyja, Gaea and Idunn that governs Asgardia in Odin's absence, and later supports Jane Foster when she takes up Mjolnir. He fights in the War of the Realms against Malekith, in which the Warriors Three are among the defenders of Midgard when the Dark Elf's armies invade.

== Powers and abilities ==

Fandral has the physical attributes typical of an Asgardian. He possesses superhuman strength, allowing him to lift roughly thirty tons, along with superhuman speed, stamina, agility and reflexes. His body is far denser than a human's, making him resistant to injury, and he is immune to terrestrial diseases and ages at a greatly reduced rate. Like other Asgardians he can recover from wounds that would kill a human, although he lacks a true healing factor and does not share Thor's command of weather.

Fandral is regarded as the finest swordsman in Asgard and one of its best archers, and he is a skilled horseman and an experienced unarmed combatant. His weapon of choice is a sword, in later stories identified as the enchanted blade Fimbuldraugr, which he wields with a flourish characteristic of his personality. He is a capable tactician and has commanded Asgardian troops in the field, although he prefers to fight alongside his two companions.
