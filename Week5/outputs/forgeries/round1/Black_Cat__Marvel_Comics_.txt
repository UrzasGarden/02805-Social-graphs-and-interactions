Black Cat (Felicia Hardy) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Marv Wolfman and artist Dave Cockrum, she first appeared in The Amazing Spider-Man #194 (July 1979), with Keith Pollard drawing that debut issue. Felicia Hardy is the daughter of a celebrated cat burglar who takes up her father's trade under a costumed identity, and she has alternated between being an adversary, an ally and a romantic partner of Spider-Man since her introduction. Although usually written as an antiheroine, she has served as a member of the Heroes for Hire, Marvel Knights and, for a time, operated as a crime boss in New York City.

The character has been adapted into numerous media outside comics, including the animated series Spider-Man (1994) and The Spectacular Spider-Man, the film The Amazing Spider-Man 2 (2014), in which Felicity Jones played Felicia Hardy, and the video game Marvel's Spider-Man (2018) and its downloadable expansion The Heist, where she was voiced by Erica Lindbeck.

== Publication history ==

Marv Wolfman originally devised the Black Cat as an opponent for Spider-Woman (Jessica Drew), whose series he was writing at the time. When Wolfman left that title and took over The Amazing Spider-Man, he brought the unused character with him and introduced her in the Spider-Man book instead. Wolfman later said that the idea for a thief whose presence brought misfortune to others came from the Tex Avery cartoon "Bad Luck Blackie", in which a black cat causes accidents to befall a bulldog. Dave Cockrum provided the costume design, and the character's name and visual style quickly drew comparisons with DC Comics' Catwoman, which Wolfman has said was not his intention.

In her first storyline, in The Amazing Spider-Man #194–195 (July–August 1979), Felicia was a burglar freeing her dying father from prison, and she was initially written as a villain with an unstable infatuation for Spider-Man. Under writer Roger Stern and in Bill Mantlo's run on Peter Parker, the Spectacular Spider-Man, the relationship was developed further, and the two characters became a couple in the early 1980s. Writer-artist Al Milgrom gave her superhuman "bad luck" powers in Peter Parker, the Spectacular Spider-Man #89–90 (April–May 1984), and the romance ended under Peter David shortly afterwards. During the late 1980s, a storyline in Strange Tales vol. 2 by Peter B. Gillis had Doctor Strange alter her powers, a change that carried into her appearances in the Spider-Man titles over the following years.

Felicia received her first solo publication with the four-issue miniseries Felicia Hardy: The Black Cat (July–October 1994), written by Terry Kavanagh and drawn by Andrew Wildman. Throughout the 1990s she appeared regularly as a supporting character, including during the "Clone Saga", where she encountered Ben Reilly as the Scarlet Spider, and in Howard Mackie's Peter Parker: Spider-Man, in which she dated Flash Thompson. In 2000, she joined the cast of the team book Marvel Knights by Chuck Dixon alongside Daredevil, Shang-Chi, Dagger and Luke Cage.

Filmmaker Kevin Smith and artist Terry Dodson produced the six-issue miniseries Spider-Man/Black Cat: The Evil That Men Do, which began in 2002 but was not completed until 2006 after a long hiatus. The series restored her probability powers and added a controversial element to her origin by revealing that she had been raped while at college. Jimmy Palmiotti, Justin Gray and Joseph Michael Linsner followed with the miniseries Claws (2006), teaming her with Wolverine, and a sequel, Claws II, in 2011. From 2006 to 2007 she was a regular member of the cast of Heroes for Hire, working beside Misty Knight, Colleen Wing, Paladin and Shang-Chi, and in 2009 she appeared in the miniseries Marvel Divas with Hellcat, Firestar and Photon.

A four-issue limited series, Amazing Spider-Man Presents: Black Cat (2010), was written by Jen Van Meter and drawn by Javier Pulido and tied into "The Gauntlet" storyline running in The Amazing Spider-Man. Dan Slott's Superior Spider-Man and the 2014 relaunch of The Amazing Spider-Man took the character in a darker direction, establishing her as an underworld leader, a status that was gradually reversed in later Slott and Nick Spencer stories.

In June 2019, Marvel launched an ongoing Black Cat series written by Jed MacKay with art by Travel Foreman. The title ran for twelve issues and an annual before being relaunched with a new first issue in December 2020 as a tie-in to the "King in Black" crossover, with art by C. F. Villa. That volume concluded in 2021 with Giant-Size Black Cat: Infinity Score. MacKay subsequently wrote the miniseries Mary Jane & Black Cat (2022–2023), which paired her with Mary Jane Watson, and the character headlined Jackpot and Black Cat (2024) by Celeste Bronfman. A new Black Cat series by G. Willow Wilson and Gleb Melnikov was announced for 2025.

== Fictional character biography ==

Felicia Hardy is born in Queens, New York, the daughter of Walter Hardy, a world-renowned cat burglar who is arrested and imprisoned when she is a child. She is raised by her mother, Lydia, who tells her that her father died in a plane crash. While attending Empire State University, Felicia is raped by her boyfriend, Ryan. Enraged by her inability to fight back, she throws herself into training in acrobatics, martial arts and lock-picking, intending to take revenge. Before she can act, Ryan is killed in a drunk-driving accident, leaving her with skills and no outlet. Having learned the truth about her father, she decides to follow his profession and adopts the identity of the Black Cat.

Her first encounter with Spider-Man occurs when she breaks her terminally ill father out of prison so that he can die at home. She develops a fixation on the wall-crawler, and in a later scheme fakes her own death to win his sympathy, after which she is committed to a psychiatric hospital. On her release, she tries to prove to Spider-Man that she has reformed, and the two begin a relationship. Peter Parker eventually reveals his identity to her, but Felicia is drawn to Spider-Man rather than to the ordinary man beneath the mask and finds it difficult to accept his civilian life.

Aware that she has no powers and worried that she is a liability to him, Felicia secretly turns to the Kingpin, who arranges an experimental procedure that gives her the ability to generate "bad luck" in those around her. She hides the source of her abilities from Peter, who is nonetheless affected by her jinx and suffers a run of misfortunes. When he learns of her deal with the Kingpin, the trust between them collapses and Peter ends the relationship. Felicia then becomes involved with Doctor Strange, who removes the probability power as it threatens to affect Spider-Man permanently; as a side effect she gains cat-like enhanced strength, agility, night vision and retractable claws.

Felicia moves to Europe for a time and returns to New York intent on hurting Peter, dating Flash Thompson as a way of making him jealous. The plan backfires when she develops genuine feelings for Flash, who eventually breaks off the relationship. She also reconciles with Peter, who has by then married Mary Jane Watson, and becomes a friend to both. During this period she occasionally works with other heroes, including Puma (Thomas Fireheart), with whom she has a brief romance, and she assists Spider-Man against the Chameleon and other enemies. In the "Clone Saga", she sides with Ben Reilly, who is operating as the Scarlet Spider, and helps him against Venom.

Her enhanced abilities later fade, and she returns to depending on her training and equipment. She joins the loose alliance of street-level heroes known as the Marvel Knights, formed by Daredevil, and after that works as a private investigator. In the storyline "The Evil That Men Do", she teams with Spider-Man to pursue a drug dealer and is framed for murder; while in custody she is drugged and assaulted, and in the process of escaping her original bad-luck powers manifest once more. Following these events she becomes a paid operative for Misty Knight's Heroes for Hire, alongside Paladin, with whom she shares a short-lived attraction.
