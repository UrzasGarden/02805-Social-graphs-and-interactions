Vulcan (Gabriel Summers) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Ed Brubaker and artist Trevor Hairsine, the character first appeared in X-Men: Deadly Genesis #1 (January 2006). He is a mutant and the youngest of the three Summers brothers, the siblings of the X-Men members Cyclops (Scott Summers) and Havok (Alex Summers), and is usually portrayed as an adversary of the X-Men rather than as a member of the team.

Within the Marvel Universe, Gabriel Summers is the son of Christopher Summers (Corsair) and Katherine Anne Summers, born in captivity after his parents were abducted by the alien Shi'ar. He was a member of a short-lived second team of X-Men that Charles Xavier sent to the living island Krakoa, an episode Xavier later concealed from the rest of the team. After returning to Earth and taking revenge on the X-Men, Vulcan travelled to the Shi'ar Empire, where he seized the throne and ruled as Majestor until his apparent death in the "War of Kings" storyline. He was later revealed to be alive and became associated with the mutant nation of Krakoa.

== Publication history ==

The existence of a third Summers brother had been hinted at since X-Men vol. 2 #23 (August 1993), in which Mister Sinister told Cyclops that his father had a third son. Writer Fabian Nicieza, who scripted that issue, later said he had intended the character Adam-X to fill that role, but the plot thread was abandoned when he left the title. Readers continued to speculate about the identity of the brother for more than a decade, and the question became a recurring topic in letters pages and fan discussion.

Ed Brubaker resolved the mystery in X-Men: Deadly Genesis, a six-issue limited series illustrated by Trevor Hairsine and Pete Woods that ran from January to July 2006. The series was published to mark the thirtieth anniversary of Giant-Size X-Men #1 (May 1975), and it retroactively inserted a previously unknown group of X-Men into the events of that issue. Brubaker has said that he wanted the new brother to be a villain so that the revelation would carry consequences for Cyclops and Xavier rather than simply adding another hero to the cast.

Brubaker continued the character's story in Uncanny X-Men #475–486 (2006–2007), the twelve-part storyline "The Rise and Fall of the Shi'ar Empire", drawn primarily by Billy Tan and Clayton Henry. Vulcan then starred in two limited series written by Christopher Yost: X-Men: Emperor Vulcan #1–5 (2007–2008), with art by Paco Diaz, and X-Men: Kingbreaker #1–4 (2009), with art by Dustin Weaver. These led into the crossover "War of Kings" (2009), written by Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning and pencilled by Paul Pelletier, in which Vulcan served as the principal antagonist and was apparently killed.

After an absence of more than a decade, Vulcan returned in X-Men vol. 5 #10 (June 2020) by Jonathan Hickman and Leinil Francis Yu, which placed him among the Krakoan-era cast. He subsequently appeared as a regular character in S.W.O.R.D. vol. 2 (2020–2021) and X-Men: Red vol. 2 (2022–2023), both written by Al Ewing.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Origin ==

Christopher and Katherine Summers are abducted by a Shi'ar starship while flying over Alaska, having pushed their two older sons, Scott and Alex, out of the plane with a parachute. In captivity, Katherine is murdered by the Shi'ar emperor D'Ken in front of her husband. D'Ken's attendants discover that she was pregnant, and the unborn child is removed and placed in an incubation accelerator, a device that ages him to adolescence in a short time. Named Gabriel, he is raised as a slave in the household of a Shi'ar noble and later taken to Earth, where he escapes his captors.

Gabriel is found by Moira MacTaggert, who brings him to her research facility on Muir Island. There he trains with three other young mutants, Darwin, Petra and Sway, and adopts the codename Vulcan. When the original X-Men are captured by the living island Krakoa, Charles Xavier sends Vulcan's team to rescue them, telepathically accelerating their training beforehand. The mission ends in disaster: Petra and Sway are killed, and Darwin merges his body with Vulcan's to keep him alive. Cyclops, who was briefly freed by the team, watches them apparently die. Xavier erases Cyclops's memories of the group and assembles a new team of X-Men, the one depicted in Giant-Size X-Men #1, to complete the rescue. Vulcan's body, still carrying Darwin inside it, is hurled into space when Krakoa is launched from Earth and remains dormant for years.

== "Deadly Genesis" ==

In the storyline "Deadly Genesis", the energy released on M-Day, when the Scarlet Witch depowers most of the world's mutants, reaches Vulcan in orbit and revives him. He returns to Earth enraged at Xavier, whom he blames for the deaths of his friends. Vulcan attacks the X-Men at the Xavier Institute, crashes the Blackbird into Banshee as he attempts to warn the team, and captures Cyclops and Rachel Summers. He forces Xavier, who has lost his telepathy on M-Day, to confess what happened on Krakoa in front of the surviving X-Men. During the fight Darwin separates from Vulcan's body. When Cyclops is unable to kill his brother and Xavier refuses to beg for his life, Vulcan declares that he is finished with Earth and departs for Shi'ar space to take vengeance on the empire that enslaved him and killed his mother.

== "The Rise and Fall of the Shi'ar Empire" ==

Xavier gathers a team consisting of Havok, Polaris, Nightcrawler, Warpath, Marvel Girl (Rachel Summers) and Darwin to pursue Vulcan, travelling aboard a stolen Shi'ar vessel with the Starjammers, including Corsair, Ch'od and Raza Longknife. Vulcan arrives in the empire ahead of them and destroys several Shi'ar warships before being defeated by the Imperial Guard led by Gladiator (Kallark). He is imprisoned but freed by Deathbird, Lilandra's exiled sister, who sees in him a means of returning to power. The two become lovers, and Vulcan agrees to help restore the catatonic D'Ken, whom he nonetheless intends to kill.

Deathbird and Vulcan revive D'Ken, who offers Vulcan Deathbird's hand in marriage and an honored place in the court. At the wedding ceremony, held before the M'Kraan Crystal, Vulcan kills D'Ken and proclaims himself emperor. Corsair confronts his son and is killed by him. Xavier is thrown into the Crystal, which restores his mutant powers, and the X-Men escape with Lilandra, though Darwin and Xavier are the only members of the rescue party who return to Earth. Havok, Polaris and Rachel Summers remain in Shi'ar space with Ch'od, Raza and the young Shi'ar warrior Korvus, taking over the Starjammers in order to lead a rebellion against Vulcan's rule.

== Emperor Vulcan and "Kingbreaker" ==

In X-Men: Emperor Vulcan, the new emperor consolidates his power while the Starjammers raid Shi'ar outposts. The empire comes under attack by the Scy'ar Tal, an ancient race the Shi'ar displaced from their homeworld centuries earlier, and Vulcan forms a temporary alliance with Havok against the common enemy. Once the Scy'ar Tal are defeated, Vulcan turns on the Starjammers and captures Havok, Polaris, Ch'od and Raza, who are placed in a Shi'ar prison. Vulcan's willingness to use the M'Kraan Crystal as a weapon convinces much of the Imperial Guard to continue serving him out of fear rather than loyalty.

X-Men: Kingbreaker depicts Vulcan's rule as an era of aggressive expansion in which neighboring species are conquered or exterminated. He assembles a personal guard of imprisoned criminals, and Deathbird is revealed to be pregnant with his child before she is left comatose during the rebels' escape. Lilandra, Rachel Summers and Korvus free Havok and the Starjammers, and the series ends with Vulcan ordering his fleets to cross into Kree territory.

== "War of Kings" ==

In "War of Kings", the Shi'ar invade the Kree Empire, now ruled by Black Bolt and the Inhumans, launching their assault during the wedding of Ronan the Accuser and Crystal. Vulcan's forces capture Lilandra, but Gladiator breaks with the emperor and sides with her, and a counter-coup is attempted on the Shi'ar throneworld. During the uprising Lilandra is assassinated by Razor, a member of the Fraternity of Raptors who has taken control of Darkhawk's armor, and Gladiator is left to rally the loyalist fleet. Black Bolt responds by building the T-Bomb, a weapon intended to end the war by altering the Shi'ar population. Vulcan boards the device and fights Black Bolt as it detonates. Both are presumed dead, and the explosion tears open a rift in space known as the Fault. Gladiator accepts the title of Majestor in the aftermath and surrenders the Shi'ar Empire to the Kree.
