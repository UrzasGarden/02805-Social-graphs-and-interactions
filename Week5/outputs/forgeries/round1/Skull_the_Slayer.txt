Skull the Slayer (James Patrick "Jim" Scully) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Marv Wolfman and artist Steve Gan, and first appeared in Skull the Slayer #1 (August 1975). A Vietnam War veteran stranded in a prehistoric land hidden within the Bermuda Triangle, Scully derives his strength from an alien belt. He has no connection to the Blazing Skull, a Timely Comics hero of the 1940s whose name is sometimes confused with his.

== Publication history ==

Skull the Slayer was launched during Marvel's mid-1970s expansion into adventure titles that blended science fiction with sword and sorcery. Wolfman wrote the first three issues with Gan on art, after which Bill Mantlo took over the scripting, with pencils by Sal Buscema and later Ron Wilson. The series was cancelled with Skull the Slayer #8 (November 1976), leaving its central plot unresolved.

The story was concluded two years later in Marvel Two-in-One #35–36 (January–February 1978), in which the Thing travelled to the hidden land and helped the cast return to the present. Scully subsequently made sporadic appearances, most notably in Quasar #16 (November 1990), written by Mark Gruenwald, which revisited his status and the fate of his belt. He later received an entry in the Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe.

== Fictional character biography ==

Jim Scully is a decorated soldier who returns from Vietnam to find his life in disarray. After a confrontation in which his brother is killed, Scully is charged with murder and placed on a flight for extradition. The aircraft passes through the Bermuda Triangle and crashes in a land where dinosaurs, alien ruins and people from many eras of Earth's history exist side by side. Scully survives along with three other passengers: physicist Raymond Corey, graduate student Ann Reynolds and teenager Jeff Turner.

Exploring the jungle, Scully finds the skeleton of an extraterrestrial wearing a belt that grants its wearer great strength. He takes the device and uses it to protect the group, earning the name Skull the Slayer. The survivors cross a region built by the aliens as a time-displaced zoo, pass through a tower whose floors each correspond to a different century, and clash with Roman legions, Egyptian cultists and a rival named Villainous Bitterhorn. Tensions within the group, particularly between Scully and Corey, run through the series.

When the Thing arrives through the Triangle in search of the missing travellers, he and Scully reach the tower that controls the land's barrier and escape to the present day. Scully returns to the United States, where the charges against him are eventually addressed. In the storyline in Quasar, he encounters Wendell Vaughn while trying to live quietly in New York; the issue establishes that the belt has since been taken from him and that he has largely retired from adventuring. He has otherwise been seen only in brief cameos among Marvel's assembled heroes, including a gathering of mystically connected characters alongside Doctor Druid.

== Powers and abilities ==

Scully has no superhuman powers of his own. The alien belt he recovered increases his strength to the point where he can lift several tons and withstand attacks that would injure an ordinary person; its origin and full capabilities were never explained. Without it he remains a highly trained combat veteran, skilled with firearms, knives and improvised weapons, and an experienced survivalist.
