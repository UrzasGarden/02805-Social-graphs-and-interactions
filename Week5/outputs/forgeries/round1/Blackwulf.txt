Blackwulf (Lucian) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Glenn Herdling and artist Angel Medina, and first appeared in Blackwulf #1 (June 1994). Lucian is a member of an alien offshoot of the Deviant race and the son of the warlord Tantalus. After the death of his brother Pelops, the original Blackwulf, Lucian inherits the name and the weapon known as the Black Legacy, and leads a band of rebels called the Underground Legion against his father.

== Publication history ==

Blackwulf was launched in 1994 as part of a wave of new ongoing titles that Marvel introduced during the speculator boom of the early 1990s. The series was written by Glenn Herdling, with pencils by Angel Medina and inks by Bill Anderson. The first issue was published with a cover date of June 1994 and carried a foil-enhanced cover, a common promotional device of the period. The book tied the character into the existing mythology of the Deviants, the genetically unstable race introduced by Jack Kirby in The Eternals, by presenting Tantalus as a Deviant of extraterrestrial origin.

The series was cancelled with Blackwulf #10 (March 1995), as declining sales led Marvel to end a number of its newer titles. The Underground Legion and Tantalus were revived by writer John Ostrander in Heroes for Hire in 1998, where the conflict with Tantalus was tied to the Deviant city of Lemuria. Lucian appeared in that storyline alongside the title's cast. The character has made few appearances since, though he has been listed in Marvel's handbook publications, including entries in the Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe.

== Fictional character biography ==

Lucian is born on the planet Armechadon, a world ruled by his father Tantalus, a long-lived Deviant conqueror. Tantalus is eventually overthrown and exiled to Earth, where he spends centuries building a hidden power base and manipulating human history from the shadows. His wife Nirvana and his sons Pelops and Lucian accompany him into exile. Unlike many of their kind, Lucian and Pelops are outwardly human in appearance, which allows them to move among ordinary people without attracting attention.

Pelops rejects his father's ambitions and takes the identity of Blackwulf, wielding the Black Legacy, an ancient weapon of enormous destructive power that Tantalus covets. He gathers a group of Deviant outcasts and superhumans into the Underground Legion, a resistance movement that operates from concealed bases in the United States. Lucian, by contrast, is initially a scientist employed by one of Tantalus's front companies and is unaware of the full extent of his father's plans. In the storyline that opens the series, Pelops is killed in battle against Tantalus's forces. Before dying he passes the Black Legacy to Lucian, who reluctantly adopts the Blackwulf name and assumes leadership of the Legion.

The Underground Legion under Lucian includes Sparrow, a winged Deviant who becomes his closest ally; Toxin, whose touch is poisonous; the physically powerful Mammoth; and Wildwind, who controls air currents. The team wages a guerrilla campaign against Tantalus's operatives, the Peacekeepers, while Lucian struggles with the weapon's influence and his own inexperience as a leader. The cyborg Deathlok (Michael Collins) becomes involved when his systems are targeted by Tantalus's technology, and he briefly fights alongside the Legion before parting ways with them. In a later storyline, the Legion's activities bring them to the attention of Hank Pym, whose laboratory holds research that Tantalus seeks to acquire for his project to restore his own power.

Over the course of the series Lucian learns that Tantalus intends to use the Black Legacy to conquer Earth and then return to Armechadon. The conflict culminates in a confrontation in which Nirvana turns against her husband, and Tantalus is apparently destroyed. Lucian is left in command of a diminished Legion, uncertain whether his father has truly been defeated.

Tantalus later resurfaces in the pages of Heroes for Hire, where he seizes control of the Deviant population of Lemuria and attempts to use the Deviants as the army he failed to raise on the surface. Lucian and the surviving members of the Underground Legion join forces with Heroes for Hire, including Luke Cage, Iron Fist and the Black Knight, to oppose him. Tantalus is defeated once more, and Lucian returns to his underground existence.

== Powers and abilities ==

As a member of an extraterrestrial Deviant lineage, Lucian possesses superhuman strength, endurance and durability, and his lifespan is far longer than that of a human. He is also a trained scientist with expertise in genetics and energy physics, a background that proves useful in understanding the technology his father employs.

Lucian's principal weapon is the Black Legacy, a device of unknown origin that resembles a staff or wand. It can project blasts of concussive force and energy capable of disintegrating matter, and it can be used to generate protective fields. The weapon responds to the will of its wielder but exerts a corrupting influence over time, and much of the tension in the series arises from Lucian's effort to control it rather than be controlled by it. He also wears a costume of Deviant manufacture that offers some protection against conventional weaponry.

== Other versions ==

Lucian has not appeared in any of Marvel's major alternate-reality imprints. A version of the Underground Legion was briefly referenced in material related to the Deviant civil war depicted in Eternals stories of the 2000s, but Lucian himself did not feature in those narratives.

== Reception ==

Blackwulf has generally been regarded as a minor property from Marvel's 1990s expansion. In 2017, Comic Book Resources included the series in a feature on short-lived Marvel titles of the decade, describing it as an attempt to build a new franchise from Kirby's Deviant mythology that failed to find an audience. The character has occasionally been cited in retrospectives on the Deathlok and Heroes for Hire series in which he guest-starred.
