The Guardsman is the name of several fictional characters appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The first Guardsman, Kevin O'Brien, was created by writer Allyn Brodsky and artist Don Heck and first appeared in Iron Man #31 (November 1970), although he did not don the Guardsman armor until Iron Man #43 (November 1971). His brother Michael O'Brien later took up the identity, and the name was subsequently applied to an entire corps of armored guards employed by the United States government at the superhuman prison known as the Vault.

The Guardsman armor was designed by Tony Stark and has generally been depicted as a less advanced counterpart to the Iron Man armor. The character has usually been associated with Stark International and its supporting cast, and the Vault's Guardsmen have appeared as recurring opponents and occasional allies of heroes such as Iron Man, War Machine, Quasar, U.S. Agent, Luke Cage and Venom (Eddie Brock). Versions of the Guardsman armor have appeared in animated television series and video games based on Iron Man.

== Publication history ==

Kevin O'Brien was introduced in Iron Man #31 (November 1970) by Allyn Brodsky and Don Heck as an engineer in the employ of Stark Industries and a confidant of Tony Stark. Under writer Gerry Conway and artist George Tuska, the character was given the Guardsman armor in Iron Man #43 (November 1971), and his descent into paranoia and death were depicted over the following issues, concluding in Iron Man #46 (May 1972). The storyline is among the earliest in the series to feature a supporting character being destroyed by Stark's own technology, a theme the title returned to repeatedly in later decades.

Michael O'Brien, Kevin's brother, was created by Len Wein and Herb Trimpe and first appeared in Iron Man #82 (January 1976) as a police officer seeking to prove Stark responsible for Kevin's death. He adopted the Guardsman identity in Iron Man #97 (April 1977), written by Bill Mantlo, and remained a member of the book's supporting cast into the early 1980s, during which time he was written as head of security for Stark International. The character was later used by Mantlo and Ralph Macchio in Marvel Two-in-One #53–58 (1979), where he served as security chief of the energy research facility Project Pegasus.

The concept was expanded in Avengers Annual #15 (1986), which introduced the Vault, a maximum-security prison for superhuman criminals in Colorado, and established that its guards wore mass-produced Guardsman armor manufactured under license from Stark. From that point the Guardsmen functioned chiefly as an institution rather than as an individual character, appearing in Iron Man, The Avengers, Captain America, Quasar, Venom and the 1991 graphic novel Avengers: Death Trap, The Vault, written by Danny Fingeroth. Individual Guardsmen were given names and storylines of their own, including Scott Washington, who was introduced in New Warriors and became the symbiote-bonded Hybrid in the Venom: Separation Anxiety limited series (1994–1995). Michael O'Brien returned as a member of the Iron Legion in Iron Man #300 (January 1994), alongside War Machine (James Rhodes) and Force (Carl Walker).

Guardsmen continued to appear in the 2000s and 2010s as generic armored security forces, serving at the Negative Zone prison during the "Civil War" storyline (2006–2007), at the Raft prison in Thunderbolts during the period in which Luke Cage led the team, and in various Iron Man series as members of Stark's security details. A new version of the Guardsman armor was introduced in Iron Man vol. 3 and the identity has periodically been revived for minor antagonists.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Kevin O'Brien ==

Kevin O'Brien is a brilliant Irish engineer who joins Stark Industries and quickly becomes one of Tony Stark's closest friends and most trusted collaborators. He assists Stark on a number of projects, including the development of new armor systems, and is one of the few employees who is aware of the strain Stark's heart condition places on him. Stark designs a suit of armor, intended as a backup for Iron Man, and when he is incapacitated, O'Brien dons the armor to defend the Stark plant, taking the name Guardsman.

Unknown to either man, the cybernetic helmet of the Guardsman armor contains a flaw that gradually amplifies the wearer's aggression and paranoia. O'Brien, who has also grown infatuated with Stark's girlfriend Marianne Rodgers, becomes increasingly resentful of his employer, convinced that Stark is exploiting him and plotting against him. He uses his position to try to take control of Stark Industries and becomes increasingly violent in his dealings with the company's enemies. When a student protest takes place outside the Stark plant, O'Brien attacks the demonstrators in the Guardsman armor, and Iron Man intervenes to stop him. In the ensuing battle, the Guardsman's repulsor fire ruptures a fuel tank, and he is killed in the explosion. Stark is left with lasting guilt over his friend's death, which he attributes to his own failure to detect the armor's defect.

== Michael O'Brien ==

Michael O'Brien, Kevin's younger brother, is a New York City police officer who refuses to accept the official account of Kevin's death and holds Stark responsible. He conducts his own investigation into Stark Industries and briefly steals an experimental suit of armor in an attempt to confront Stark directly. Over time he comes to understand the circumstances of his brother's death and his hostility toward Stark softens into an uneasy respect.

When Stark later refits the Guardsman armor and corrects the flaw in its control systems, he offers it to Michael, who accepts and becomes the second Guardsman. In this capacity he fights alongside Iron Man against several opponents and is eventually appointed head of security at Stark International. O'Brien later leaves Stark's employ to become chief of security at Project Pegasus, a government energy research installation in upstate New York. There he works alongside Wendell Vaughn, the hero later known as Quasar, and Bill Foster (then called Giant-Man), and the three become involved in a conspiracy orchestrated by the Nth Command and the criminal Lightner, who is transformed into the energy being Nuklo's successor, Blacksun. O'Brien participates in the defense of the facility alongside the Thing during the resulting crisis.

O'Brien subsequently appears intermittently in supporting roles. When Stark loses his company to Obadiah Stane and sinks into alcoholism, O'Brien is among the former associates who attempt to help him. After Stark founds Stark Enterprises, O'Brien is brought back as part of the company's security operation. When Stark's body is taken over by the technology of Ultimo and later when Stark is believed dead, O'Brien joins the Iron Legion, a group of Stark's friends and former associates, including James Rhodes as War Machine, Happy Hogan, Bethany Cabe and Carl Walker, the reformed villain Force, who each wear one of Stark's spare armors to defeat Ultimo. He later works in the security division of the Vault alongside the corps of Guardsmen who had by then adopted his codename.

== The Vault Guardsmen ==

Following the construction of the Vault, the federal government licenses Stark's Guardsman design and equips the prison's guard force with mass-produced versions of the armor. The Guardsmen are responsible for containing some of the most dangerous superhuman criminals in the world and are frequently depicted as outmatched by the inmates they are meant to control. The prison has been the site of numerous breakouts, during which the Guardsmen have fought the Avengers, Freedom Force, the Wrecking Crew and Venom.

During the "Armor Wars" storyline, Stark learns that the Guardsman armor incorporates technology stolen from him by Justin Hammer and resolves to disable every suit built on his designs. He breaks into the Vault and deactivates the Guardsmen's armor with a negator pack, leaving the prison defenseless during a riot and bringing him into conflict with Captain America (Steve Rogers), who is at the time operating as the Captain. The incident contributes to a government decision to treat Iron Man as a fugitive. In a later mass escape engineered by Venom (Eddie Brock), the Guardsmen are joined by the Avengers, including U.S. Agent, and by Freedom Force in retaking the prison, which Venom has rigged to self-destruct.

Scott Washington, a young African American Guardsman from New York, is assigned to guard the four symbiotes spawned from Venom's. When he refuses an order to execute the imprisoned creatures, he is dismissed from the Vault. He is later shot and paralyzed by gang members in his home neighborhood, and the escaped symbiotes seek him out and bond with him, restoring his mobility and transforming him into the vigilante Hybrid.
