Quasar is the name of several superheroes appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The best known is Wendell Vaughn, who was created by writers Roy Thomas and Don Glut and artist John Buscema and first appeared under the name Marvel Man in Captain America #217 (January 1978). Each bearer of the name has wielded the Quantum Bands, a pair of alien wristbands that draw on an energy source known as the Quantum Zone and that were previously worn by Marvel Boy (Robert Grayson).

Subsequent characters to use the name include the Kree-Titan hybrid Phyla-Vell, Nova (Richard Rider), who briefly held the bands, and S.H.I.E.L.D. agent Avril Kincaid. The Vaughn version of the character has appeared in animated television and video games.

== Publication history ==

Wendell Vaughn debuted as Marvel Man in Captain America #217 (January 1978) and reappeared in several issues of that series before being renamed Quasar in The Incredible Hulk #234 (April 1979), with the writer explaining the change in a letters page as being prompted by concerns over the earlier name. The character became a regular in Marvel Two-in-One and later served as a security chief for Project Pegasus, a recurring setting in the company's titles of the early 1980s. The Quantum Bands themselves had earlier appeared on Marvel Boy, a 1950s hero revived in Fantastic Four #164–165 (November–December 1975) as the Crusader.

Writer Mark Gruenwald, who had used the character in Marvel Two-in-One, launched a solo title, Quasar, which ran for 60 issues from October 1989 to July 1994. The series established Quasar as Protector of the Universe, a role bestowed by the cosmic entity Eon, and introduced many elements of the character's background, including his father Gilbert Vaughn, the alien Kayla Ballantine and the Stranger. Artists on the run included Paul Ryan, Mike Manley and Greg Capullo. After the series was cancelled Quasar appeared as a supporting character in Avengers, Starblast and other cosmic storylines.

The character was killed in Annihilation: Nova #1 (June 2006), after which the Quantum Bands passed to Phyla-Vell in Annihilation #6 (March 2007). Phyla-Vell used the Quasar name through the Annihilation: Conquest storyline and the 2008 Guardians of the Galaxy series by Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning, before giving it up to become Martyr. Vaughn returned in the 2007 miniseries Annihilation: Conquest – Quasar and was the lead in Guardians of the Galaxy #16–17 and the 2010 miniseries The Thanos Imperative, during which Richard Rider wore the bands for a short time.

Avril Kincaid was created by Nick Spencer and Mark Bagley and first appeared in Avengers Standoff: Welcome to Pleasant Hill #1 (May 2016). She assumed the Quasar identity in Avengers Standoff: Assault on Pleasant Hill Omega #1 (June 2016) and featured in Captain America: Sam Wilson, Secret Empire and the 2018 Infinity Countdown storyline, in which she was killed and the bands returned to Vaughn.

== Fictional character biography ==

Wendell Vaughn is a S.H.I.E.L.D. agent who is assigned to guard the Quantum Bands following the death of the Crusader. When the terrorist group AIM attacks the facility, Vaughn puts on the bands and discovers that, unlike previous wearers, he can control their energy without being destroyed, because he does not strain against the power but relaxes into it. He adopts the name Marvel Man and later Quasar, and becomes head of security at Project Pegasus, where he works alongside the Thing and Giant-Man (Bill Foster) and confronts threats such as the Nth Man and Thundra.

Quasar is summoned by Eon, who names him Protector of the Universe and augments his abilities with quantum vision. He joins the Avengers and operates from New York, founding the security firm Vaughn Security Consultants. In the storyline "Operation: Galactic Storm" he takes part in the war between the Kree and the Shi'ar. He later battles Maelstrom, who steals the bands and nearly destroys the universe before Quasar defeats him, and comes into possession of the Star Brand for a period. During the "Annihilation" event Quasar and Nova (Richard Rider) oppose the Annihilation Wave, and Quasar is killed by Annihilus, who claims the bands.

Phyla-Vell, the daughter of Captain Mar-Vell, takes the bands from the defeated Annihilus and becomes the new Quasar. She fights the Phalanx during "Annihilation: Conquest" alongside her partner Moondragon and becomes a founding member of the Guardians of the Galaxy. Vaughn survives as a being of pure energy and eventually regains the bands when Phyla-Vell relinquishes them. During "The Thanos Imperative" Vaughn lends the bands to Richard Rider, who uses them in the final battle against the Cancerverse.

Avril Kincaid, a S.H.I.E.L.D. agent stationed at Pleasant Hill, is given the Quantum Bands by Vaughn during the Hydra uprising and defends Earth from a Chitauri invasion while Hydra controls the country in "Secret Empire". She is killed fighting in the "Infinity Countdown" storyline, and the bands return to Vaughn, who resumes his role.

== Powers and abilities ==

Each Quasar derives powers from the Quantum Bands, which allow the wearer to generate and manipulate quantum energy in the form of constructs, force fields, energy blasts and beams of light. The bands permit flight at speeds beyond that of light through a dimension called the quantum zone, teleportation over interstellar distances and the absorption of most forms of energy. The bands bond to the wearer and cannot be removed against their will. Vaughn also possesses quantum vision, which lets him perceive energy, and has at times existed as a being of living energy without a physical body. Phyla-Vell additionally had Kree physiology and cosmic awareness inherited from her father.

== Other versions ==

In the Marvel Zombies universe, a zombified Quasar is among the heroes consumed by the plague. An alternate Quasar appears in the "Earth X" trilogy, and a version of Wendell Vaughn is seen in the pages of What If? examining the consequences of Maelstrom's victory.

== In other media ==

Wendell Vaughn appears in The Super Hero Squad Show. Quasar appears as a playable character in the mobile game Marvel Future Fight and in Lego Marvel Super Heroes 2. Phyla-Vell appears in the 2023 film Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3, portrayed by Kai Zen.
