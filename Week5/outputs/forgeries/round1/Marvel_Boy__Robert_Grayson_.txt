Marvel Boy (Robert Grayson), later known as the Uranian, is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Stan Lee and artist Russ Heath and first appeared in Marvel Boy #1 (December 1950), published by Marvel's 1950s predecessor, Atlas Comics. Raised on the planet Uranus by a colony of Eternals, Grayson returns to Earth as a costumed adventurer and is later a member of the 1950s team known as the Agents of Atlas.

== Publication history ==

Marvel Boy was one of several costumed heroes Atlas Comics introduced in the early 1950s, during a brief attempt to revive the superhero genre that also produced characters such as the Black Knight. The series Marvel Boy ran for two issues (December 1950 – February 1951) before being retitled Astonishing with issue #3; the character headlined that title through issue #6 (October 1951), after which it became a horror and suspense anthology. Bill Everett drew most of the later Marvel Boy stories.

The character remained unused for more than two decades. Writer Roy Thomas and artist George Pérez reintroduced Grayson as the villainous Crusader in Fantastic Four #164–165 (November–December 1975), a story that ended with his death. Thomas later wrote a 1950s version of the Avengers that included Marvel Boy in What If? #9 (June 1978). His wristbands were subsequently identified as the Quantum Bands worn by Quasar (Wendell Vaughn), a connection explored in the Quasar series of the early 1990s.

Writer Jeff Parker and artist Leonard Kirk revived the character in the miniseries Agents of Atlas #1–6 (October 2006 – March 2007), which revealed that the Crusader had not been Grayson at all. Grayson went on to appear in the ongoing Agents of Atlas series (2009), the follow-up title Atlas (2010), and later team revivals, including Agents of Atlas (2019) by Greg Pak. Because the name Marvel Boy had by then been used by several other characters, including Noh-Varr, Grayson adopted the codename the Uranian during Parker's run.

== Fictional character biography ==

Robert Grayson is the son of Matthew Grayson, a scientist who flees Europe in the 1930s and builds a rocket to escape a world he believes is heading toward war. Taking his infant son with him, Grayson lands on Uranus, where the two are taken in by a colony of Eternals living beneath a protective dome. The Eternals raise Robert, train his mind, and equip him with a pair of wristbands that generate light and energy. In 1950 he returns to Earth as Marvel Boy and operates as an investigator and crimefighter, dealing with gangsters, spies and the occasional alien menace.

In 1958, Grayson is recruited by FBI agent Jimmy Woo for a mission to rescue President Dwight D. Eisenhower from the Yellow Claw. Alongside Venus, Gorilla-Man, the Human Robot (M-11) and Namora, he succeeds, but the group is disbanded shortly afterward. Grayson returns to Uranus, where the Eternal colony is destroyed. He is placed in suspended animation, and his physiology is altered so that he can no longer survive unaided in Earth's atmosphere.

In the storyline told in Fantastic Four #164–165, a figure wearing Grayson's bands and calling himself the Crusader attacks a New York bank and its elderly founder, blaming them for refusing to fund a rescue of the Uranian colony. The Crusader is destroyed when his bands overload during a battle with the Fantastic Four. The bands pass to S.H.I.E.L.D. and eventually to Wendell Vaughn, who becomes Quasar. In Agents of Atlas it is revealed that the Crusader was Thelius, a native Uranian sent to Earth in Grayson's place, and that the real Grayson remained in stasis on Uranus.

Decades later, Grayson is found in his dormant flying saucer and reunited with Woo, Venus, Gorilla-Man, M-11 and Namora. The reformed team investigates the Atlas Foundation and discovers that the Yellow Claw has been grooming Woo as his successor; Woo accepts leadership of the organization and turns it toward heroic ends. Grayson subsequently fights alongside the team against Norman Osborn's forces during the "Dark Reign" period and takes part in the "Secret Invasion" and "Siege" storylines. He later serves with the expanded Agents of Atlas during the "War of the Realms".

== Powers and abilities ==

Grayson's upbringing among the Eternals of Uranus gave him a range of mental powers, including telepathy, the ability to project illusions, and limited mind control. His original wristbands could emit beams of light intense enough to blind opponents and could be used for flight. These bands were later identified as the Quantum Bands, cosmic artifacts far more powerful than Grayson himself knew; after their loss he used a replacement set of Uranian design.

Because the Uranians modified his body, Grayson cannot breathe Earth's air for long and wears a specially sealed suit and headpiece. He pilots a semi-sentient Uranian saucer that responds to his thoughts and can carry the entire team. He is also an experienced detective and investigator.

== Other versions ==

In What If? #9, Marvel Boy is a member of a version of the Avengers that forms in the 1950s, alongside 3-D Man, Gorilla-Man, Venus, the Human Robot and Jimmy Woo. This team later appears as an alternate timeline in Avengers Forever #4–5 (1999), where its members are recruited for a battle across time.
