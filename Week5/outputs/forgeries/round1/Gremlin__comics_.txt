The Gremlin (Kondrati Topolov) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Steve Englehart and artist Herb Trimpe, the character first appeared in The Incredible Hulk #163 (May 1973). He is the son of the Soviet scientist Yuri Topolov, the Gargoyle, and inherited both his father's deformity and his scientific genius. A recurring opponent of the Hulk, he later served the Soviet government as the second Titanium Man and was a member of the Soviet Super-Soldiers.

== Publication history ==

The Gremlin debuted in The Incredible Hulk #163 (May 1973) as part of a two-issue story in which the Hulk was lured to a Soviet arctic installation. Englehart and Trimpe conceived the character as a legacy villain tied to the Gargoyle, who had appeared in the very first issue of the series in 1962. The Gremlin returned in The Incredible Hulk #187–188 (May–June 1975), written by Len Wein, in a storyline that resolved the fate of Glenn Talbot, who had been held prisoner in the Gremlin's base.

The character was subsequently used as the organizer of the Soviet Super-Soldiers, appearing with the team in The Incredible Hulk #258–259 (April–May 1981) and in Rom #44–45 (July–August 1983). He also appeared in Spider-Woman #46–47 (October–December 1982), where he menaced Jessica Drew. In Iron Man #229 (April 1988), during the "Armor Wars" storyline by David Michelinie and Bob Layton, the Gremlin was killed in combat with Iron Man. He has made only sporadic appearances in flashback since.

== Fictional character biography ==

Kondrati Topolov is born in the Soviet Union to the scientist Yuri Topolov, whose exposure to radiation has left him disfigured and mentally brilliant. Kondrati inherits both traits in exaggerated form: he is extremely short, with a large head and greenish skin, and he possesses an intellect that surpasses his father's. After Yuri Topolov dies in a confrontation with the Hulk, the young Kondrati is raised by the state and placed in charge of a secret research base in Siberia, where he adopts the name Gremlin.

The Gremlin bears a grudge against the Hulk for his father's death. He engineers the capture of Colonel Glenn Talbot, a United States Army officer, and uses him as bait to draw the Hulk into the Soviet Union. The Hulk destroys much of the base, but the Gremlin escapes. He later subjects Talbot to a brainwashing process intended to turn him into an assassin, a scheme that is foiled when Nick Fury and the Hulk free Talbot during a second raid on the facility.

In the following years the Gremlin becomes a senior figure in Soviet superhuman research. He assembles the Soviet Super-Soldiers, a team that includes Darkstar, Vanguard and Ursa Major, and sends them against the Hulk when the monster wanders into Soviet territory. He also directs the team during the Dire Wraith crisis, in which they encounter the Spaceknight Rom. On another occasion his agents clash with Spider-Woman while she is investigating Soviet activity in the United States.

The Gremlin eventually dons a redesigned suit of Titanium Man armor and becomes the second holder of that title. When Tony Stark learns that his armor designs have been stolen and sold to other armored combatants, he hunts down each one in the "Armor Wars" storyline. Iron Man confronts the Titanium Man in the Soviet Union and, during the fight, activates his boot jets while the Gremlin has him in a grip. The intense heat causes the titanium of the armor to ignite and the Gremlin is burned to death, a result Stark had not intended.

== Powers and abilities ==

The Gremlin has no superhuman physical abilities. His mutation gives him a diminutive, misshapen body and a greatly enlarged cranium. His primary attribute is his intellect, which is considered among the most advanced in the Marvel Universe; he is an expert in physics, engineering, genetics and psychology, and he designs weapons, aircraft and conditioning equipment for the Soviet state.

As the second Titanium Man he wears a suit of powered armor of his own construction. The armor grants him superhuman strength and durability, flight by means of boot jets, and the ability to fire concussive blasts and electrical charges from its gauntlets. The suit's titanium construction, while strong, proves vulnerable to extreme heat.
