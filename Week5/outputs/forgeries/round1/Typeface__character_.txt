Typeface (Gordon Thomas) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Paul Jenkins and artist Mark Buckingham and first appeared in Peter Parker: Spider-Man #23 (November 2000). A former sign painter who adopts a costume decorated with letters of the alphabet, he has been depicted both as an antagonist and as a reluctant ally of Spider-Man.

== Publication history ==

Typeface debuted in Peter Parker: Spider-Man #23 (November 2000) during the run of Paul Jenkins and Mark Buckingham, and his origin was concluded in the following issue. He was conceived as a deliberately low-key villain whose gimmick, weaponized typography, reflected the more character-driven tone of the series. The character returned during the "Civil War" crossover in 2006–2007 and later made a brief appearance in the Deadpool series.

== Fictional character biography ==

Gordon Thomas is a veteran who returns from military service to find that his younger brother has been killed and that his family has drifted apart. He takes a job at Ampersand Signs in New York City, where he spends years painting storefront lettering. When the company is sold and he is laid off, Thomas suffers a breakdown. He paints letters across his face, assembles a costume from scrap signage and sets out to destroy the business that discarded him.

Calling himself Typeface, Thomas confronts Spider-Man while attacking the Ampersand building. During the fight he explains his grievances, and Spider-Man comes to sympathize with him even while trying to stop him. The encounter ends with Typeface apparently sacrificing himself to prevent the collapse of the structure, although his body is never recovered.

Typeface resurfaces in the storyline "Civil War", where he refuses to sign the Superhuman Registration Act and joins the underground resistance led by Captain America. He is briefly shown alongside other unregistered heroes, including the former Speedball, Robbie Baldwin, during the conflict with Iron Man's forces. Typeface later appears in Deadpool, where he is among a group of minor costumed figures encountered by the mercenary; the encounter ends with his death.

== Powers and abilities ==

Typeface has no superhuman powers. He is a trained soldier with experience in hand-to-hand combat and the use of firearms, and he is an accomplished sign maker. His arsenal consists of letters and symbols fashioned into weapons, including sharpened metal letters thrown as projectiles, a letter "T" that doubles as a throwing blade and a sign post used as a staff. He also carries signage rigged with explosives and smoke charges, and his costume incorporates light body armor.
