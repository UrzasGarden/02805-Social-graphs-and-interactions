Rocket Raccoon is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Bill Mantlo and artist Keith Giffen, the character first appeared in Marvel Preview #7 (Summer 1976) as "Rocky Raccoon" and was reintroduced under his current name in The Incredible Hulk #271 (May 1982). He is an anthropomorphic raccoon from the planet Halfworld who works as a mercenary, pilot and weapons specialist, and he is best known as a member of the Guardians of the Galaxy alongside Star-Lord, Gamora, Drax the Destroyer and his longtime companion Groot.

Rocket was a minor character for most of his publication history, appearing sporadically after his original stories, until he was revived as part of Marvel's cosmic line of the late 2000s. Since then he has headlined several solo series and has become one of the publisher's more recognizable cosmic characters. The character appears in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, voiced by Bradley Cooper with motion-capture performance by Sean Gunn, beginning with Guardians of the Galaxy (2014).

== Publication history ==

Rocket Raccoon was created by Bill Mantlo and Keith Giffen. His first appearance, in the black-and-white magazine Marvel Preview #7 (Summer 1976), was in a backup story titled "The Sword in the Star", in which a talking raccoon named Rocky encountered the human Prince Wayfinder. Mantlo took the name from the Beatles song "Rocky Raccoon", and the character's eventual catch phrase and origin story contained several further references to the band's work. The Marvel Preview story was a self-contained fantasy piece and was not initially intended to be part of the Marvel Universe.

Mantlo brought the character back, renamed Rocket Raccoon, in The Incredible Hulk #271 (May 1982), with art by Sal Buscema. The issue placed Rocket on Halfworld, a planet in the Keystone Quadrant, and established the supporting cast and setting that later stories would build upon. The story was an anniversary issue of the Hulk's series and introduced the Hulk to a world populated by sentient animals and robots.

Rocket's first starring vehicle was the four-issue limited series Rocket Raccoon (May–August 1985), written by Mantlo and drawn by Mike Mignola, with inks by Al Gordon and Al Milgrom. The series expanded the Halfworld setting, explained the origin of its animal inhabitants, and introduced characters including Lylla, Wal Rus, Blackjack O'Hare, Judson Jakes and Lord Dyvyne. It was one of Mignola's earliest professional assignments at Marvel and remained his most prominent work for the publisher until he moved on to Hellboy at Dark Horse Comics.

Following the limited series, the character appeared only occasionally for roughly two decades. He had a brief role in Quasar #15 (October 1990), in which he was shown as a resident of Halfworld after the events of the limited series, and made cameo appearances in other titles, including a courtroom scene in She-Hulk vol. 2 (2006) in which he was depicted alongside the Starjammer Ch'od. Rocket's original stories were collected in a single volume for the first time in 2011.

Rocket returned to regular publication in the limited series Annihilation: Conquest – Starlord #1–4 (September–December 2007), written by Keith Giffen with art by Timothy Green II. The series assembled a group of expendable prisoners under Star-Lord's command during the Phalanx invasion of Kree space, and it paired Rocket with Groot for the first time. The commercial and critical response to the Annihilation line led to the launch of a new Guardians of the Galaxy ongoing series in 2008, written by Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning, with Rocket as a founding member. He appeared throughout the series' twenty-five issues and in the crossover storylines "War of Kings" and "The Thanos Imperative", as well as in the follow-up limited series Annihilators (2011) and Annihilators: Earthfall (2011–2012), in which he shared a backup feature with Groot.

The character's profile rose sharply in the period surrounding the 2014 film adaptation. Brian Michael Bendis launched a third volume of Guardians of the Galaxy in 2013, and Marvel published Rocket Raccoon vol. 2 (2014–2015), an ongoing series written and drawn by Skottie Young, whose first issue was among the best-selling comics of July 2014. Young continued the character in Rocket Raccoon and Groot (2016), followed by a short-lived Rocket Raccoon vol. 3 (2016–2017) by Matthew Rosenberg and Jorge Coelho that returned the character to Earth. Al Ewing and Adam Gorham produced Rocket (2017), a six-issue heist series written in the style of crime fiction. Rocket subsequently appeared in Ewing's Guardians of the Galaxy (2020–2021) and in the Avengers limited series Avengers: No Road Home (2019), and he returned to the Guardians lineup in the 2023 series by Jackson Lanzing and Collin Kelly.

== Fictional character biography ==

Rocket is a genetically altered raccoon who was uplifted to sentience on Halfworld, a planet in the Keystone Quadrant that had originally been established as an asylum for the mentally ill. The animals of Halfworld were engineered by the asylum's robot caretakers to serve as companions and guardians for the patients, referred to as the "Loonies", after the human staff abandoned the colony. Rocket serves as the colony's chief law officer, patrolling the planet in his spacecraft the Rack 'n' Ruin, with his otter companion Lylla and his first mate Wal Rus.

In his first encounter with a human from Earth, Rocket meets the Hulk, who has been transported to Halfworld. Rocket enlists the Hulk's aid against the mole Judson Jakes and his mercenary Blackjack O'Hare, who have stolen Gideon's Bible, a book containing the knowledge of the colony's founders. The two retrieve the book and the Hulk is returned to Earth. Rocket later becomes embroiled in a war between the toymaker Jakes and the reptilian Lord Dyvyne, both of whom seek control of Halfworld and of Lylla's inheritance, the Mayhem Mekaniks toy company. With the help of Uncle Pyko, a tortoise who has been building weapons for Jakes, Rocket discovers the truth about the planet's history and uses a helmet derived from the original caretakers' technology to cure the Loonies. The newly sane humans leave Halfworld, and Rocket and his companions depart to explore the galaxy aboard a ship built from the remains of the robots' city.

Rocket is subsequently shown to have returned to Halfworld, where he is encountered by Quasar during that hero's search for the missing Eon. Years later, Rocket is a prisoner of the Kree when the techno-organic Phalanx conquer the Kree empire. He is offered a reduced sentence in exchange for joining a strike team led by Star-Lord, whose other members include Groot, Mantis, Bug, Captain Universe and Deathcry. The team infiltrates Hala to sabotage the Phalanx, and Rocket forms a close bond with Groot after the latter sacrifices himself to destroy a Phalanx facility, only to regrow from a surviving sprig that Rocket carries with him. After the Phalanx are defeated, Star-Lord assembles a permanent team to prevent further cosmic catastrophes, and Rocket joins the new Guardians of the Galaxy along with Groot, Gamora, Drax, Adam Warlock, Quasar (Phyla-Vell) and Mantis.

When Star-Lord is exiled to the Negative Zone and the team fractures after learning that Mantis has manipulated their minds, Rocket takes command of a reduced roster and recruits new members, including Jack Flag, Major Victory and the telepathic dog Cosmo's ally, Mantis. The reunited Guardians attempt to stop the escalating conflict between the Shi'ar, now ruled by Vulcan, and the Kree under the Inhuman royal family. In the storyline "War of Kings", Rocket leads a mission to broker peace between the two sides, which fails when Black Bolt's T-bomb tears a rift in space-time known as the Fault. Rocket and the Guardians later fight the forces of the Cancerverse that emerge from the Fault in "The Thanos Imperative", during which Star-Lord and Nova are presumed killed while trapping Thanos in the collapsing universe.

Following the disbanding of the Guardians, Rocket takes a job in the mail room of the Timely Inc. corporation, having had part of his memory suppressed. He is drawn back into action when a killer clown from Halfworld attacks him, and with Groot's help he returns to his home planet to confront the sentient weapon Star-Thief. Rocket learns that he was once Halfworld's chief of security and that he had voluntarily given up his memories of the planet. After defeating Star-Thief, Rocket and Groot resume their partnership and rejoin the Guardians when Star-Lord reforms the team.

With the Guardians, Rocket takes part in the defense of Earth from the Badoon, the pursuit of the renegade Angela, and the conflict with Thanos that follows the destruction of the Black Vortex. He later leaves the Guardians for a time and returns to Earth, where he is briefly a fugitive from the law. During the crisis surrounding the death of the Olympian Queen Nyx's children, Rocket is summoned to join an ad hoc team of Avengers including Hercules, Hulk, Scarlet Witch, Hawkeye, Vision, Spectrum and Voyager, which travels between worlds to prevent Nyx from reassembling the shards of her power. Rocket's expertise with weaponry and his willingness to take extreme measures prove decisive during the team's confrontation with Nyx in the realm of Euphoria.
