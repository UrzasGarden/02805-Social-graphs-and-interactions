Citizen V is the codename of several superheroes appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The original Citizen V, John Watkins, was created by writer and artist Ben Thompson and first appeared in Daring Mystery Comics #8 (January 1942), published by Marvel's predecessor Timely Comics. The identity was revived decades later by Baron Helmut Zemo, who used it as a disguise while leading the Thunderbolts, and it was subsequently taken up by Dallas Riordan and by John Watkins III, the grandson of the original.

== Publication history ==

John Watkins debuted as Citizen V in Daring Mystery Comics #8 (January 1942), a wartime adventurer who led a resistance unit known as the V-Battalion against the Axis powers. The character made only a single Golden Age appearance and was largely forgotten until Kurt Busiek and Mark Bagley reintroduced the name in Thunderbolts #1 (April 1997). In that series, Citizen V was revealed to be Helmut Zemo in disguise, and the Thunderbolts themselves were the Masters of Evil posing as heroes.

Dallas Riordan assumed the identity in Thunderbolts #26 (May 1999), and the modern V-Battalion was explored in the limited series Citizen V and the V-Battalion (2001) by Fabian Nicieza and Citizen V and the V-Battalion: The Everlasting (2002). John Watkins III became the primary bearer of the name during Nicieza's later run on Thunderbolts in the 2000s.

== Fictional character biography ==

John Watkins is a British citizen who fights the Nazi occupation of Europe during the Second World War under the name Citizen V, leading the V-Battalion in acts of sabotage behind enemy lines. He is eventually captured and killed by Baron Heinrich Zemo. After his death, the surviving members of the V-Battalion form a secret organization that continues to operate for decades, pursuing war criminals and monitoring threats to global stability.

Following the apparent deaths of the Avengers and the Fantastic Four in the "Onslaught" storyline, Helmut Zemo adopts the Citizen V identity as a deliberate insult to the man his father killed. He leads the Masters of Evil, rebranded as the Thunderbolts, in a scheme to win public trust before seizing power. When his teammates rebel against him, Zemo abandons the identity and the Thunderbolts continue as genuine heroes.

Dallas Riordan, the former liaison between the Thunderbolts and the office of the Mayor of New York, is recruited by the V-Battalion and given the Citizen V costume and equipment. She fights alongside and against the Thunderbolts before being paralyzed in battle. Later, through a link to the ionic energy of Erik Josten (Atlas), she regains the use of her legs and takes the name Vantage.

John Watkins III, the original's grandson, becomes the next Citizen V as the V-Battalion's field agent. His body is temporarily possessed by Zemo's consciousness after the Baron's apparent death, until Zemo is restored to his own form. Watkins resumes the identity and later serves with the Thunderbolts during the "Civil War" storyline, afterward crossing paths with Cable while the V-Battalion tracks a surviving Hydra cell.

== Powers and abilities ==

None of the people to use the Citizen V name possess superhuman powers. John Watkins was a trained soldier and saboteur. Helmut Zemo is a master strategist and a skilled swordsman and marksman. Dallas Riordan and John Watkins III received intensive combat training from the V-Battalion and used a costume incorporating lightweight armor, a sword, and a "V-shaped" gliding wing. In the later Citizen V suit, Watkins also employed a set of experimental weapons supplied by the V-Battalion's technicians.

== In other media ==

Baron Zemo appears as Citizen V in the Avengers Assemble episodes "Thunderbolts" and "Thunderbolts Revealed", voiced by David Kaye. The Citizen V identity also appears as an alternate costume for Zemo in several Marvel mobile and online games.

== Reception ==

In 2016, Screen Rant listed Zemo's tenure as Citizen V among the most notable disguises adopted by Marvel villains, citing the Thunderbolts reveal as one of the publisher's best-kept secrets of the 1990s.
