The Abomination (Emil Blonsky) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Gil Kane, the character first appeared in Tales to Astonish #90 (April 1967). Blonsky is a Yugoslavian-born agent of the KGB who deliberately exposes himself to a greater dose of gamma radiation than the one that created the Hulk, and is transformed into a scaled, reptilian brute who is stronger than the Hulk in the Hulk's calm state but who cannot return to human form. He is one of the Hulk's most persistent enemies and has also clashed with She-Hulk, Spider-Man, Hercules and other heroes, while occasionally acting as a reluctant ally.

The Abomination has appeared in a number of adaptations outside comics, including animated television series and video games. Tim Roth portrayed Emil Blonsky in the Marvel Cinematic Universe film The Incredible Hulk (2008) and reprised the role in Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings (2021) and the Disney+ series She-Hulk: Attorney at Law (2022).

== Publication history ==

The Abomination debuted in the Hulk feature of Tales to Astonish #90 (April 1967), with the story concluding in the following issue. Stan Lee has said that he wanted the Hulk to face an enemy who was simply bigger and stronger than he was, and that he gave Gil Kane, who had recently taken over the feature, little more than that premise and the name. Kane designed the character's finned, scaly appearance. Lee later recalled that he had been fond of the word "Abomination" and settled on the name before the villain himself had been worked out.

After being taken off Earth at the end of his second appearance, the character resurfaced in Silver Surfer #12 (January 1970) and returned to the Hulk's own title in The Incredible Hulk #137 (March 1971). He was a recurring opponent through the 1970s and 1980s, appearing alongside the Rhino in The Incredible Hulk #159 (January 1973), in #194–196 (December 1975–February 1976) and in #270 (April 1982). Under writer Bill Mantlo the Abomination was apparently disintegrated by MODOK in The Incredible Hulk #290 (December 1983), a fate that was reversed several years later.

Writer Peter David, whose run on The Incredible Hulk lasted from 1987 to 1998, reshaped the character considerably. Beginning with The Incredible Hulk #364 (December 1989), Blonsky was shown living beneath the streets of New York, and his estranged wife Nadia was introduced in #431–432 (July–August 1995). The three-issue limited series Abominations (December 1996–February 1997), written by Ivan Velez Jr., focused on the community of outcasts he had gathered in the sewers. Writer Paul Jenkins used the character in The Incredible Hulk vol. 2 #24–25 (March–April 2001), retroactively establishing him as the cause of Betty Ross's death. David returned to the character for the limited series Hulk: Destruction #1–4 (September–December 2005), which expanded on Blonsky's background.

The Abomination was killed in Hulk #1 (March 2008) by writer Jeph Loeb and artist Ed McGuinness, and his murder served as the initial mystery of the Red Hulk storyline. The character made brief returns in the "Chaos War" tie-ins (2010–2011) and in subsequent Hulk titles before featuring prominently in Al Ewing's The Immortal Hulk (2018–2021), where the Abomination's remains were merged with General Reginald Fortean. Blonsky himself was restored during that series and became a regular cast member of the limited series Gamma Flight (2021), written by Ewing and Crystal Frasier.

== Fictional character biography ==

Emil Blonsky is born in Zagreb, Yugoslavia, and grows up to become an agent of the KGB. He is sent to the United States to spy on the Air Force base in New Mexico where Bruce Banner conducts his research. Blonsky gains access to the installation at a moment when Banner, despairing of ever being cured, has prepared a gamma-ray device with which he intends to end his own life. Unaware of the machine's purpose, Blonsky activates it on himself, receiving a far greater dose of radiation than Banner originally did. He is transformed into a massive, green-scaled creature and, unlike Banner, keeps his full intelligence in his new form.

The newly created Abomination quickly overpowers the Hulk and carries off Betty Ross. Banner, whose own gamma energy has been depleted, allows himself to be exposed to the device again so that the Hulk can be made strong enough to prevail, and the Abomination is defeated in a second encounter. The alien being known as the Stranger then removes the Abomination from Earth, intending to study him. Blonsky eventually escapes the Stranger's control, briefly battles the Silver Surfer, and leads a band of alien outcasts before returning to Earth and the Hulk. He subsequently falls under the control of the Galaxy Master and the Leader, and is paired with the Rhino in an unsuccessful attempt to destroy the Hulk.

Over the years the Abomination's repeated defeats leave him increasingly fearful of the Hulk. Captured by the military, he spends long periods in custody and is at one point left in a near-catatonic state. He is later revived by MODOK, who hopes to use him against the Hulk, but when the Abomination refuses to fight MODOK disintegrates him. His scattered particles are eventually reconstituted, and the restored Blonsky, having regained a measure of his humanity, retreats to the sewers of New York. There he becomes the protector of a group of homeless people and misfits known as the Forgotten, and fights to keep them safe from both the authorities and the Hulk.

Blonsky's past intrudes on this new life when he learns that his wife Nadia Dornova, whom he had believed lost to him, has become a celebrated stage actress in New York. He abducts her after a performance and tries to explain what became of him, but she is horrified by his appearance and he releases her. During this period he also clashes with Spider-Man and with Hercules, and becomes increasingly bitter about the apparent happiness Banner enjoys with Betty despite his own condition. Acting on that jealousy, he secretly transfuses his own irradiated blood into Betty over a period of time. She falls gravely ill and dies of what appears to be radiation poisoning, and Banner blames himself, believing that long exposure to his own body is responsible.

When Banner eventually uncovers the truth, the Hulk tracks the Abomination down and beats him savagely, stopping only after forcing him to watch a recording in which Nadia makes clear that she has no feelings left for him. Blonsky is placed in military custody once again. He later escapes and, in the storyline told in Hulk: Destruction, confronts both Banner and the circumstances of his own origin, with his history as a spy and his relationship with his father explored in detail. She-Hulk is among the heroes who face him after his escape, and he is returned to confinement.

== Death ==

Blonsky is next found dead in Russia, his body riddled with gunfire from a weapon powerful enough to pierce his hide. Doc Samson, She-Hulk and Iron Man investigate the killing, which is eventually revealed to be the work of the Red Hulk, the gamma-powered form of General Thaddeus Ross. The Abomination's murder is the first in a series of attacks that establish the Red Hulk as a new threat, and his death is treated as permanent for several years.

During the "Chaos War" storyline, the Abomination is among the deceased villains returned to the living world when the forces of Amatsu-Mikaboshi disrupt the realms of the dead. He is directed against the Hulk and his allies but is ultimately returned to death when the conflict is resolved. In later years his corpse is recovered by the covert organization Shadow Base, whose commander, General Fortean, hopes to weaponize it against the Hulk. Fortean is instead bonded with the Abomination's hide and becomes a new Abomination in his own right, a transformation that ends with his death at the Hulk's hands.

Blonsky himself is subsequently restored to life through the influence of the gamma dimension known as the Below-Place. He returns with a calmer and more reflective personality, and rather than resuming his vendetta against the Hulk he joins the government-sponsored team Gamma Flight, working alongside figures such as Puck and Absorbing Man to contain gamma-related threats.
