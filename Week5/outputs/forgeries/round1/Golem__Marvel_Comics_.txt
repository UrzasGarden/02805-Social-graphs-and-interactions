The Golem is the name of a number of fictional characters appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The two most prominent are a stone figure animated in the sixteenth century and later revived by the spirit of archaeologist Abraham Adamson, which debuted in Strange Tales #174 (June 1974), and Jacob Goldstein, a Jewish scientist from occupied Warsaw who gained the form of a golem during the Second World War, who first appeared in The Invaders #12 (January 1977). Both draw on the Jewish folklore of the Golem of Prague.

== Publication history ==

The first Marvel character to use the name was created by writer Len Wein and artist John Buscema for Strange Tales #174, the anthology title that had earlier served as the home of Doctor Strange and Brother Voodoo. The feature continued in Strange Tales #176 (October 1974) and #177 (December 1974), with Mike Friedrich writing and Tony DeZuniga providing the art, before it was cancelled. The character returned in Marvel Two-in-One #11 (September 1975) in a team-up with the Thing, and was later revived as a member of the monster-hunting unit in Nick Fury's Howling Commandos (2005–2006).

Jacob Goldstein was created by Roy Thomas and Frank Robbins for The Invaders #12–13 (January–February 1977), a wartime adventure set in the Warsaw Ghetto. The character was revisited in the miniseries Invaders Now! (2010–2011) by Christos Gage and Caio Reis, and appeared in All-New Invaders (2014–2015), written by James Robinson.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Adamson's Golem ==

According to the Marvel account, the Golem is a man-shaped figure of stone brought to life by the rabbi Judah Loew to protect the Jews of Prague. After its task is finished it is lost in the desert of the Middle East, where it lies buried for centuries. In the present day it is excavated by Professor Abraham Adamson, who is accompanied by his niece Rebecca, his nephew Jason and Rebecca's fiancé Wayne Logan. When mercenaries led by Colonel Omar attack the camp, Adamson is mortally wounded, and his dying tears fall on the statue. His spirit passes into the stone and the Golem awakens to drive off the attackers.

The Golem travels with the Adamsons to Florida, where it is pursued by the demon Kaballa the Unclean, who covets its mystical power. In Marvel Two-in-One #11, Kaballa temporarily gains control of the creature and sends it against the Thing, but Adamson's spirit reasserts itself and the two part on good terms. Years later the Golem is recruited into a S.H.I.E.L.D. unit of supernatural operatives led by Nick Fury, serving alongside figures such as the Living Mummy and the Zombie.

== Jacob Goldstein ==

Jacob Goldstein is a chemist living in the Warsaw Ghetto under Nazi occupation. His brother Johann has collaborated with the Germans and been given a suit of powered armor, operating as the Blue Bullet. When the Invaders are captured while attempting to rescue Jacob, he turns to the legend of the golem, mixing clay and reciting the ancient formula. A bolt of lightning strikes him as he works, fusing him with the clay and transforming him into a hulking stone being. As the Golem, he frees the Invaders and defeats the Blue Bullet, but chooses to remain in Warsaw to protect his people rather than leave with the heroes.

In Invaders Now!, it is revealed that during the war the Golem took part in a mission in the Netherlands with the Invaders and Doctor Nemesis, in which a village infected by a Nazi bioweapon was destroyed to prevent an outbreak. The guilt of that act draws the surviving participants back together in the present day, and Goldstein, still in his stone form, aids the reformed team in confronting the consequences.

== Powers and abilities ==

Both Golems possess superhuman strength and durability, with bodies of animated stone or clay that are highly resistant to injury, heat and conventional weapons. Neither requires food, air or sleep. Adamson's Golem is sustained by human emotion; love or compassion strengthens it, while hatred weakens it, and it can be rendered dormant if cut off from such feelings. It cannot speak. Jacob Goldstein retains his intellect and scientific training, and his transformation is believed to have been triggered by a mixture of lightning and mystical energy rather than by purely natural means.

== Other versions ==

A golem under the control of the Hood's criminal organization appears briefly during the "Dark Reign" era, and golem-like constructs are a recurring motif in stories involving Jewish characters such as Kitty Pryde and Doc Samson. In Marvel Zombies 4, an undead version of Adamson's Golem is among the monsters confronted by Morbius and the Midnight Sons.
