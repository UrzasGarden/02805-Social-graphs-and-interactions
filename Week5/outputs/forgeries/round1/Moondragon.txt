Moondragon (Heather Douglas) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writers Bill Everett and Mike Friedrich and artist George Tuska, she first appeared in Iron Man #54 (January 1973) under the alias Madame MacEvil. She is the daughter of Arthur Douglas, who was later resurrected as Drax the Destroyer, and was raised on Titan by the monastic order of Shao-Lom, where she developed powerful telepathic abilities and mastered a number of martial and scientific disciplines.

Over the course of her publication history Moondragon has been a member of the Avengers, the Defenders, the Infinity Watch, the Guardians of the Galaxy and the Annihilators. Her arrogance and her periodic possession by the demonic entity known as the Dragon of the Moon have put her in conflict with her former allies on several occasions. She is one of Marvel's earliest openly bisexual characters, and her relationship with Phyla-Vell became a central element of her later stories. The character has appeared in animated television and in video games.

== Publication history ==

Moondragon debuted in Iron Man #54 (January 1973), where she was introduced as the mysterious Madame MacEvil. The issue was plotted by Bill Everett and scripted by Mike Friedrich, with pencils by George Tuska. The character was renamed Moondragon and given an origin in Daredevil #105 (November 1973), written by Steve Gerber from a plot by Jim Starlin, who had been building the Titan mythology around Thanos and Drax the Destroyer in Captain Marvel. Starlin used her as a supporting character in the "Thanos War" storyline in Captain Marvel #31–33 (1974) and in The Avengers #125 (July 1974).

Steve Englehart made Moondragon one of the candidates for the Celestial Madonna in The Avengers #129–135 and Giant-Size Avengers #2–4 (1974–1975). She became a provisional Avenger in The Avengers #137 (July 1975) and left in The Avengers #151 (September 1976), taking Patsy Walker with her to Titan. Jim Shooter later depicted her turn toward tyranny in The Avengers #219–220 (May–June 1982), which led directly to her joining the Defenders. She was a regular in The Defenders from 1983 until the series ended with #152 (February 1986), in which writer Peter B. Gillis concluded the Dragon of the Moon storyline.

Starlin returned to the character in Warlock and the Infinity Watch #1 (February 1992), in which she received the Mind Gem, and she remained a member of the Infinity Watch for the series' 42 issues. Peter David featured her prominently in the two Captain Marvel series starring Genis-Vell (1999–2004), introducing her relationship with Phyla-Vell. She appeared in the crossover events Annihilation (2006) and Annihilation: Conquest (2007–2008), the latter of which included her death, and was brought back in Guardians of the Galaxy vol. 2 (2008–2010) by Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning. She was a founding member of the team in Annihilators (2011). Al Ewing's Guardians of the Galaxy (2020–2021) revisited the character, merging her with an alternate-universe counterpart introduced during "Infinity Wars" (2018).

== Fictional character biography ==

== Origin ==

Heather Douglas is born in Los Angeles to Arthur and Yvette Douglas. While the family is driving through the Mojave Desert, they witness the landing of a spacecraft belonging to Thanos, who destroys their car to eliminate witnesses. Heather is thrown clear and survives, but her parents are killed. The scene is observed by Mentor, the father of Thanos, who takes the orphaned girl to his home on Saturn's moon Titan. There she is raised by the Shao-Lom monks, who train her body and mind to their limits. Under their instruction she develops latent telepathic abilities, studies a wide range of sciences and becomes an exceptional martial artist.

During her years on Titan Heather is unknowingly exposed to the Dragon of the Moon, a malevolent entity imprisoned on the moon that had earlier influenced Thanos. The Dragon quietly encourages her arrogance, though she believes she has rejected it. Meanwhile, unknown to her, the spirit of her father has been placed by Kronos into a powerful artificial body and sent after Thanos as Drax the Destroyer. When Thanos destroys the Shao-Lom monastery, Heather leaves Titan for Earth, adopting the name Madame MacEvil and planning to gather allies and weapons to use against him.

== Thanos and the Avengers ==

Posing as Madame MacEvil, she manipulates Iron Man and Namor the Sub-Mariner into fighting one another as a test of their capabilities. She afterward takes the name Moondragon and, following an encounter with Daredevil and the Black Widow in which her true history is revealed, allies herself with Captain Mar-Vell. Alongside Mar-Vell, Drax and the Avengers, she helps defeat Thanos's first attempt to seize the Cosmic Cube. During the conflict she learns that Drax is her father, though he initially retains no memory of his human life.

Moondragon is subsequently identified as one of three candidates to become the Celestial Madonna, together with Mantis and the witch Agatha Harkness. The title ultimately goes to Mantis, and Moondragon, who had assumed she was the obvious choice, is embittered by the outcome. She nevertheless accepts provisional membership in the Avengers, serving beside the Beast and Hellcat. Her haughty manner strains her relationships with her teammates, and she eventually departs for Titan, bringing Patsy Walker with her so that the new Hellcat can receive the same training she had. During this period she also assists the Avengers against Michael Korvac, and it is her telepathic probing that first exposes the extent of Korvac's power to the team.

== Ba-Bani and the Defenders ==

Convinced that her abilities give her the right to guide lesser beings, Moondragon travels to the war-torn planet Ba-Bani and uses her telepathy to impose peace by overriding the free will of its population. When Drax attempts to intervene, she kills him with a psychic attack. Thor and the Avengers free the planet, and Moondragon is taken to Asgard, where Odin sentences her to wear a headband that suppresses most of her powers. She is placed in the custody of the Valkyrie and joins the Defenders, then led by Doctor Strange and later reorganized under the Beast and Angel.

Within the Defenders she gradually succumbs to the Dragon of the Moon, which has waited years for her defenses to weaken. Posing as a reformed teammate, she manipulates the group until the Dragon's influence can no longer be hidden, and she turns against the team outright. In the final conflict, the Valkyrie, the ancient Eternal called the Interloper, Andromeda and the mentally unstable Manslaughter sacrifice themselves to bind the Dragon, and Moondragon is apparently destroyed in the process alongside them. Her disembodied spirit later returns, and in time her body is restored, although the Dragon of the Moon remains tied to her.

== Infinity Watch ==

Following the defeat of Thanos in the "Infinity Gauntlet" storyline, Adam Warlock divides the Infinity Gems among a group of guardians he names the Infinity Watch. Moondragon is given the Mind Gem, which greatly amplifies her telepathy, and serves alongside Warlock, Gamora, Pip the Troll and a resurrected Drax, who has been restored with a childlike mind and diminished intellect. Although she never fully reconciles with her father, she comes to protect him, and she assists Warlock during the "Infinity War" and "Infinity Crusade" storylines. Her connection to the Dragon of the Moon resurfaces when the Gem's power draws the entity back toward her. The Infinity Watch disbands after the Gems are stolen by the vampire Rune, and Moondragon returns to Earth.

== Captain Marvel and Phyla-Vell ==

Moondragon later seeks out Genis-Vell, the son of Mar-Vell who has taken the name Captain Marvel, intending to tutor him in the use of his cosmic awareness. She enters into a relationship with Marlo Chandler, the wife of Genis-Vell's bonded companion Rick Jones, which ends when Marlo returns to her husband. When Genis-Vell is driven insane by his cosmic awareness and remakes the universe, a sister, Phyla-Vell, is created in the process. Moondragon helps restore Genis-Vell's sanity and afterward begins a romantic relationship with Phyla-Vell that continues through the cosmic conflicts that follow.

== "Annihilation" and death ==

During the "Annihilation" storyline, Moondragon and Drax are captured by Thanos, who has allied himself with Annihilus and holds her as leverage over her father. She is kept aboard the Annihilation Wave's flagship until Drax breaks free and kills Thanos in front of her. In the subsequent "Annihilation: Conquest" event, Phyla-Vell, now wielding the quantum bands and calling herself Quasar, is guided by a telepathic summons to find the galaxy's "savior", and Moondragon accompanies her, at times assuming a dragon form made possible by her bond with the Dragon of the Moon. The two locate Adam Warlock in his cocoon on the world of the High Evolutionary. Ultron, who has seized control of the technological Phalanx, arrives there and kills Moondragon as Phyla looks on. Warlock's awakening and the eventual defeat of the Phalanx come too late to save her.
