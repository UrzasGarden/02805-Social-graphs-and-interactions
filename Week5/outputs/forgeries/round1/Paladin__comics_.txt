Paladin is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Jim Shooter and artist Carmine Infantino, the character first appeared in Daredevil #150 (January 1978). He is a professional mercenary and bounty hunter whose real name has never been revealed, although he has used the alias Paul Dennis. Over the course of his publication history he has worked both for and against various superheroes, and has been a member of Silver Sable's Wild Pack, two incarnations of the Heroes for Hire, the Thunderbolts under Norman Osborn, and the Villains for Hire.

Though a frequent supporting character in titles featuring Daredevil, Spider-Man and the Avengers, Paladin has rarely been the focus of his own stories. His moral ambiguity, in which he will take almost any job for the right price while generally refusing to kill, has made him a recurring foil for more idealistic heroes.

== Publication history ==

Paladin debuted in Daredevil #150 (January 1978), written by Jim Shooter with art by Carmine Infantino and Klaus Janson, and returned in the following issue. Shooter conceived the character as a mercenary whose skills rivaled those of the heroes he was hired to oppose, and whose motives were commercial rather than criminal. The character received a solo story in Marvel Premiere #43 (August 1978), written by Shooter and drawn by Infantino, which Marvel had intended as a possible pilot for an ongoing series that was never commissioned.

During the 1980s Paladin became a utility character who appeared across several titles. He teamed with Spider-Man in Marvel Team-Up #108 (August 1981) and in Peter Parker, the Spectacular Spider-Man, and he became a supporting character in The Avengers under writer Roger Stern beginning with The Avengers #251 (January 1985), where he was paired romantically with the Wasp. He appeared in the "Under Siege" storyline in The Avengers #273–277 (1986–1987) and in Captain America.

In the 1990s Paladin was a regular member of the cast of Silver Sable and the Wild Pack (1992–1995) by Gregory Wright and Steven Butler. He later joined the cast of Heroes for Hire vol. 2 (2006–2007), written by Justin Gray and Jimmy Palmiotti and later Zeb Wells, and was added to the Thunderbolts by writer Andy Diggle in Thunderbolts #128 (March 2009). Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning made him a lead character in Heroes for Hire vol. 3 (2010–2011) and its follow-up Villains for Hire (2011–2012).

== Fictional character biography ==

Little is known about Paladin's background, and he has deliberately kept his past obscure. He establishes himself as a high-priced mercenary who specializes in retrieval and protection work and who is willing to work with law enforcement when the pay is sufficient. He first encounters Daredevil when he is hired to bring in the Purple Man, and the two initially fight before cooperating to capture the criminal. Shortly afterward he is hired to protect a scientist from the Guardian, a job that brings him into conflict with the Tarantula.

Paladin subsequently crosses paths with Spider-Man on several occasions, at first as an adversary and later as a reluctant ally, and he is briefly hired by Spider-Woman (Jessica Drew) during her time as a private investigator. He enters the orbit of the Avengers when the Wasp, recently divorced from Hank Pym, engages him as a security consultant and the two begin a relationship. When the Masters of Evil seize Avengers Mansion in the storyline "Under Siege", Paladin assists the Wasp in her counterattack, is badly injured in the fighting, and is later treated at the mansion. The relationship ends when the Wasp decides she cannot trust a man whose loyalty can be purchased.

In the following years Paladin works regularly for Silver Sable as a member of her Wild Pack, alongside mercenaries such as Solo and Sandman. He participates in missions against Hydra and other terrorist organizations and is frequently teamed with Spider-Man, with whom he develops a grudging mutual respect. His tenure with the Wild Pack is marked by disputes with Sable over payment and by his refusal to carry out assassinations.

== Heroes for Hire ==

During the storyline "Civil War", Paladin attempts to collect the bounty on Captain America by shooting him with a tranquilizer, but is stopped by Misty Knight, Colleen Wing and Shang-Chi, who release the hero. Knight nonetheless recruits him into her reformed Heroes for Hire, a team licensed by the government to apprehend unregistered superhumans. His teammates include Shang-Chi, the Black Cat, Humbug, Orka and the second Tarantula (Maria Vasquez), with whom he has a volatile working relationship. In the storyline "World War Hulk", the team is captured by the Hulk's Warbound, and Paladin is among those who survive Humbug's betrayal.

== Thunderbolts ==

Following the storyline "Secret Invasion", Norman Osborn rebuilds the Thunderbolts as a covert strike team and hires Paladin as a member alongside the Black Widow (Yelena Belova), Ant-Man (Eric O'Grady), Ghost, Headsman and Mister X. The team hunts Deadpool and pursues Songbird, during which Paladin grows increasingly uneasy with Osborn's methods and the brutality of the government-sanctioned Scourge. In the storyline "Siege", when the team is sent to Asgard, Paladin turns against Osborn, helps the Black Widow (Natasha Romanoff) and Songbird escape, and has Ghost erase the memories of his teammates so that he can leave the team undetected.

== Villains for Hire ==

Paladin is later contacted by Misty Knight, who has established a new Heroes for Hire operation in which she dispatches freelance operatives such as Moon Knight, Spider-Man, Shang-Chi and the Punisher to individual missions. He becomes her most trusted field agent and discovers that Knight has been manipulated by the Puppet Master, whom he helps her defeat. During the storyline "Spider-Island" he works alongside Eddie Brock, then operating as Anti-Venom, to contain the outbreak in Manhattan. When the Purple Man assembles a team of criminals as Villains for Hire, Knight sends Paladin undercover to infiltrate and dismantle the operation. In a subsequent assignment he briefly works alongside Jubilee while tracking a fugitive through the Los Angeles underworld.

== Powers and abilities ==

Paladin has no superhuman powers, but he is in peak physical condition and is an exceptional athlete, marksman and hand-to-hand combatant. He is an experienced strategist with extensive knowledge of covert operations, surveillance and infiltration, and he has been trained in the use of most conventional firearms and military hardware. He is also an accomplished pilot and driver.

His signature weapon is a custom stun gun that fires a concentrated electrical blast capable of rendering an opponent unconscious without permanent injury, which allows him to fulfil his frequent preference for non-lethal contracts. He wears a lightweight body armor of his own design that offers protection against small-arms fire and absorbs much of the impact of physical blows. He also carries a variety of conventional weapons, gadgets and tracking devices depending on the requirements of a given assignment.
