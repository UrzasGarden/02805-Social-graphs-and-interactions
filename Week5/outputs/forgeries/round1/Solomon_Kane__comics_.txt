Solomon Kane is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by pulp author Robert E. Howard, who introduced him in the short story "Red Shadows" in the August 1928 issue of Weird Tales. Marvel's version first appeared in the black-and-white magazine Monsters Unleashed #1 (July 1973), in an adaptation by writer Roy Thomas and artist Ralph Reese. Kane is a sixteenth-century English Puritan who travels through Europe and Africa hunting evildoers and supernatural threats. Marvel held the comics license to the character from 1973 to 1994, after which it passed to other publishers.

== Publication history ==

Marvel acquired the rights to several of Howard's heroes in the early 1970s following the commercial success of Conan the Barbarian, and Solomon Kane was among the first to be tried in the company's magazine line. After his debut in Monsters Unleashed #1, an original story in Dracula Lives! #3 (October 1973), written by Roy Thomas and drawn by Alan Weiss, pitted Kane against Marvel's Dracula. Further adaptations followed in Kull and the Barbarians #2–3 (1975) and in The Savage Sword of Conan, where Kane became a recurring back-up feature through the late 1970s and 1980s.

The character's first color appearances came in Marvel Premiere #33–34 (December 1976 – February 1977), which adapted "Red Shadows" with scripts by Thomas and artwork by Howard Chaykin and Alan Weiss. Kane was one of several grim, cloaked adventurers Marvel promoted in this period; the company's magazines also tried out Moon Knight in a solo issue of Marvel Preview, and both characters were later cited by writers as examples of Marvel's attempt to find an audience outside the superhero genre.

Marvel's most sustained use of the character was The Sword of Solomon Kane, a six-issue limited series cover-dated September 1985 to July 1986. Written by Ralph Macchio, the series featured artists including Bret Blevins, Mike Mignola, Steve Carr and Sandy Plunkett, with inks by Al Williamson among others, and adapted Howard stories such as "Red Shadows", "Blades of the Brotherhood", "The Moon of Skulls", "The Hills of the Dead" and "Wings in the Night". Kane continued to appear in The Savage Sword of Conan into the 1990s, with the final Marvel story published in 1994. Earlier stories were reprinted in Conan Saga. The license was subsequently taken up by Dark Horse Comics, which launched a new Solomon Kane series in 2008.

== Fictional character biography ==

Solomon Kane is a tall, somber Englishman of the late sixteenth century, dressed in the plain black clothing and slouch hat of a Puritan. Although he regards himself as a servant of God, he is driven less by doctrine than by a compulsion to punish wrongdoing wherever he encounters it. He wanders Europe and later the African interior without a fixed home, intervening in local conflicts and pursuing murderers, pirates and sorcerers across great distances.

In "Red Shadows", Kane finds a dying girl on a French roadside and swears vengeance on the bandit Le Loup, whom he pursues from France to the African coast. There he is captured by a tribe ruled through fear by a hostile witch doctor, and befriends the elderly shaman N'Longa, whose magic allows him to possess the bodies of the dead. With N'Longa's aid Kane escapes and kills Le Loup. In later stories N'Longa gives Kane a carved staff with a cat's head, through which the shaman can speak to Kane over long distances and which proves effective against vampires and other undead creatures.

In Marvel's Dracula Lives! story, Kane travels through Transylvania and is lodged for a night in the castle of Count Dracula, whom he recognizes as a vampire and fights to a standstill before continuing on his way. In adaptations of "The Hills of the Dead" and "Wings in the Night", Kane destroys a city of vampires with the help of N'Longa and then confronts a race of winged, man-eating creatures preying on an African village. "The Moon of Skulls" follows him to the lost city of Negari, ruled by the queen Nakari, where he rescues an abducted English girl and brings about the city's destruction.

== Powers and abilities ==

Solomon Kane possesses no superhuman powers. He is an exceptional swordsman, skilled with the rapier and dirk, and an accurate shot with his flintlock pistols and musket. He is physically strong, extremely hardy and nearly impossible to intimidate, and his resolve allows him to resist fear and mystical influence to an unusual degree. His principal weapon against the supernatural is N'Longa's staff, which in Marvel's stories is identified as the Staff of Solomon, an artifact of great age capable of repelling or destroying undead beings.

== In other media ==

A live-action film, Solomon Kane, was released in 2009 with James Purefoy in the title role; it drew on Howard's original stories rather than on the Marvel comics.
