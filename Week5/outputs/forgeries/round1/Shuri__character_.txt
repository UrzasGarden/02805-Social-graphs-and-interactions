Shuri is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Reginald Hudlin and artist John Romita Jr., the character first appeared in Black Panther vol. 4 #2 (May 2005). Shuri is a princess of the fictional African nation of Wakanda, the daughter of King T'Chaka and Queen Ramonda, and the younger half-sister of T'Challa, the superhero Black Panther. Trained in combat from childhood and later established as one of Wakanda's leading scientists, she served as the country's Black Panther and ruler while her brother was incapacitated. After her apparent death and resurrection she acquired mystical abilities tied to Wakanda's ancestral plane and took the title Aja-Adanna, the "Ancient Future".

Letitia Wright portrays Shuri in the Marvel Cinematic Universe films Black Panther (2018), Avengers: Infinity War (2018), Avengers: Endgame (2019) and Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022), in which the character succeeds her brother as the Black Panther. The film version, a teenage inventor responsible for much of Wakanda's technology, has influenced subsequent depictions of the character in comics and animation.

== Publication history ==

Shuri was introduced by Reginald Hudlin and John Romita Jr. in the storyline "Who Is the Black Panther?", which opened the fourth volume of Black Panther in 2005. Hudlin, a filmmaker hired to relaunch the title, framed the new series around T'Challa's family and the politics of Wakanda, and Shuri was conceived as a younger sibling who had expected to compete for the throne herself. She remained a supporting character through the run, appearing in the invasion storyline that concluded in Black Panther vol. 4 #6 (September 2005) and in the wedding of T'Challa and Storm in Black Panther vol. 4 #18 (September 2006).

In 2009 the title was relaunched as Black Panther vol. 5 with a new first issue (April 2009), written by Hudlin with art by Ken Lashley. Its opening storyline, "The Deadliest of the Species", installed Shuri as the new Black Panther after T'Challa was critically wounded, a change promoted as part of Marvel's "Dark Reign" publishing initiative. Jonathan Maberry replaced Hudlin as writer with issue #7 and completed the volume with issue #12 (March 2010). Maberry and artist Scot Eaton continued the story in the six-issue limited series Doomwar (2010), and Maberry then wrote Klaws of the Panther (2010–2011), a four-issue limited series in which Shuri was the lead character.

Shuri remained prominent in Wakanda-centred stories over the following years, including the crossover Avengers vs. X-Men (2012) and Jonathan Hickman's New Avengers (2013–2015), in which she governed Wakanda during the "Incursions" storyline and was apparently killed in New Avengers #24 (2014). Ta-Nehisi Coates's Black Panther vol. 6, launched in 2016, restored the character in the storyline "A Nation Under Our Feet" and redefined her abilities in the subsequent arc, "Avengers of the New World".

Following the release of the Black Panther film, Marvel launched an ongoing series titled Shuri, written by Nnedi Okorafor with art by Leonardo Romero. The series debuted with Shuri #1 (December 2018) and ran for ten issues, with Vita Ayala scripting the final two, before concluding in 2019. The character subsequently appeared in Coates's Black Panther vol. 7 (2018–2021), John Ridley's Black Panther vol. 8 (2021–2023) and Eve L. Ewing's Black Panther vol. 9 (2023–2024), and she featured in prose fiction including the young adult novel Shuri: A Black Panther Novel (2020) by Nic Stone and its sequels.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Early life ==

Shuri is born into the Wakandan royal family as the daughter of T'Chaka and his wife Ramonda. After T'Chaka is murdered by Ulysses Klaw, the throne passes to his brother S'Yan, and the young Shuri secretly trains so that she can challenge for the mantle of the Black Panther at the annual ritual combat. Before she is able to do so, her older half-brother T'Challa enters the tournament and defeats S'Yan, becoming king and Black Panther. Shuri accepts the outcome but continues to regard the mantle as something she might one day earn.

When Klaw returns with a mercenary army that includes the Rhino, Batroc the Leaper, Radioactive Man and a Vatican-sponsored Black Knight, Shuri is ordered to remain in the royal palace. She disobeys, engages the Black Knight in combat and takes the Ebony Blade from him, then uses the sword to kill Radioactive Man. The killing, her first, disturbs her, and T'Challa later reflects that his sister has crossed a threshold she cannot return from. She is present at T'Challa's wedding to Storm and, during the "Secret Invasion" storyline, helps repel the Skrull force that attacks Wakanda.

== Black Panther ==

In the storyline "The Deadliest of the Species", T'Challa is left comatose after an ambush orchestrated by Doctor Doom, and Ramonda and the royal council ask Shuri to assume the role of Black Panther. She undergoes the ritual trials and the heart-shaped herb, but the Panther God Bast rejects her, judging that she has sought the mantle out of ambition rather than service. Shuri nonetheless puts on a Black Panther costume to defend Wakanda from Morlun, a totem-eater who has come to consume T'Challa's spirit while Storm battles for his soul on the astral plane. Resolving to die rather than let Morlun reach her brother, Shuri proves herself to Bast, who grants her the full powers of the Black Panther. T'Challa recovers but leaves Shuri in the role, and she rules Wakanda as regent while he regains his strength.

Shuri's reign is tested when Namor and Doom pursue Wakanda's reserves of vibranium. In Doomwar, the xenophobic Desturi faction stages a coup with Doom's backing, and Doom seizes the sacred vibranium vault. Shuri and T'Challa, allied with the X-Men, the Fantastic Four and Deadpool, fight to retake the country, and the crisis ends when T'Challa renders all processed vibranium on Earth inert, leaving Wakanda's economy in ruins. In Klaws of the Panther, Shuri tracks a resurgent Klaw as he attempts to obtain new vibranium and build a weapon capable of destroying Wakanda. Her pursuit takes her to the Savage Land and New York, where she works with Wolverine, Spider-Man and Black Widow, and she ultimately defeats Klaw and is invited to cooperate with the Avengers and A.I.M.'s rivals in future operations.

During Avengers vs. X-Men, Shuri allows the Avengers, among them Luke Cage, to use Wakanda as a staging ground against the Phoenix-empowered X-Men. In retaliation, Namor, wielding a portion of the Phoenix Force, unleashes a tidal wave that devastates the capital and kills thousands. T'Challa annuls his marriage to Storm, and Shuri declares Storm unwelcome in Wakanda and formally commits the nation to war against Atlantis, which she pursues with a devastating strike on the Atlantean kingdom.

In New Avengers, Shuri is crowned queen while T'Challa serves as king of the Necropolis, the city of the dead, and secretly works with the Illuminati to prevent the destruction of Earth by the Incursions. Shuri learns of the Illuminati's existence and of T'Challa's refusal to kill Namor, and she condemns her brother's secrecy. In the storyline "Time Runs Out", Namor's Cabal, including Thanos and the Black Order, lays waste to Wakanda. Shuri remains behind to hold off Proxima Midnight and Black Swan so that T'Challa can escape, and she is apparently killed.

== Aja-Adanna ==

Shuri's body is preserved in a form of living death while her consciousness travels to the Djalia, a spiritual plane holding the collective memory of Wakanda. There a griot who assumes Ramonda's appearance instructs her in the entire history of her people. In "A Nation Under Our Feet", T'Challa, with the aid of Manifold, restores her to life, and she returns transformed. Able to turn her body to living stone, to dissolve into a flock of birds, and to draw on the knowledge of every Wakandan who has ever lived, she helps T'Challa end the uprising led by Tetu and Zenzi. She is thereafter known as Aja-Adanna, the Ancient Future, and acts as a keeper of Wakanda's memory rather than as a Black Panther.

In "Avengers of the New World", Shuri joins her brother against the Originators, the ancient inhabitants of Wakanda who have returned from exile, and against the exhorter Ras, whose cult undermines faith in the orisha. When T'Challa later disappears in space while investigating a wormhole, Shuri resists calls to take up the Black Panther mantle again, instead forming a council of Wakandan women called the Elephant's Trunk. In the course of the Shuri series she works alongside Storm, Rocket Raccoon and Groot, travels to New York to team with Ms. Marvel and Miles Morales, and eventually tracks her brother to the Intergalactic Empire of Wakanda.
