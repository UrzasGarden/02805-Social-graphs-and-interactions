Elizabeth "Betsy" Braddock is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics, commonly in association with the X-Men. The character was created by writer Chris Claremont and artist Herb Trimpe and first appeared in Captain Britain #8 (December 1976), a title produced by Marvel's British imprint, Marvel UK. Betsy is the twin sister of Brian Braddock, the original Captain Britain, and was introduced as a supporting character in his series before being developed into a superhero in her own right. She is a mutant with telepathic abilities, and for much of her publication history she has been known under the codename Psylocke. She has been a member of the X-Men, Excalibur, X-Force and the Exiles, and has twice held the mantle of Captain Britain.

Betsy's history is notable for a long-running storyline in which her mind was transferred into the body of the Japanese assassin Kwannon, a change that redefined the character for nearly three decades. In 2018 she was returned to her original body, and the Psylocke name passed to Kwannon. Since 2019, Betsy has operated as Captain Britain, first in the series Excalibur and later in Knights of X and Betsy Braddock: Captain Britain.

The character has appeared in several adaptations outside comics. Meiling Melançon played a version of the character in the film X-Men: The Last Stand (2006), and Olivia Munn portrayed her in X-Men: Apocalypse (2016). She has also appeared in animated series and in numerous video games.

== Publication history ==

Betsy Braddock debuted in the Marvel UK weekly Captain Britain #8 (December 1976), written by Chris Claremont and drawn by Herb Trimpe. In her early appearances she was a charter pilot and the sister of the title character, and her psychic abilities were hinted at rather than shown. The weekly was cancelled in 1977, and the character made sporadic appearances in Hulk Comic and Marvel Super-Heroes, where the Captain Britain feature was continued by writers including Dave Thorpe and later Alan Moore, with art by Alan Davis. During this period she was depicted as a fashion model and as an agent of the psi-division of the British intelligence agency S.T.R.I.K.E.

In 1985, Marvel UK launched a second Captain Britain series written by Jamie Delano with art by Alan Davis. In this run, Betsy briefly replaced her brother as Captain Britain after he abandoned the role, only to be blinded by the villain Slaymaster. The character's appearance in this run, including her purple hair, became her established look. The series ended in 1986, and Claremont, who had returned to the character in his work on the American X-Men titles, brought her to the United States.

Betsy joined the X-Men in New Mutants Annual #2 (October 1986), written by Claremont and drawn by Davis, in which the Psylocke codename was first used. She became a regular member of the team in Uncanny X-Men beginning with issue #213 (January 1987), and remained part of the main cast through the end of Claremont's tenure. The most significant change to the character came in Uncanny X-Men #256–258 (December 1989 – February 1990), in which Claremont and artist Jim Lee transformed Betsy into a Japanese ninja in the service of the Hand. The story was later clarified in X-Men (vol. 2) #20–32 (1993–1994) by writer Fabian Nicieza, who introduced the character Revanche, revealed to be the assassin Kwannon, and established that the two women's minds and bodies had been exchanged. The body swap was initially suggested by Lee, who wanted to draw the character in a different form.

Psylocke was among the characters featured in the 1991 relaunch X-Men (vol. 2) by Claremont and Lee, which became the best-selling comic book of all time. She was a recurring cast member of X-Men and Uncanny X-Men through the 1990s, under writers including Nicieza, Scott Lobdell and Joe Kelly. In the "Crimson Dawn" storyline in Uncanny X-Men #329–330 (1996), she gained shadow-based powers, and in X-Men (vol. 2) #77–78 (1998) she was written out of the series temporarily. Her psionic powers were changed from telepathy to telekinesis in the "Psi-War" storyline later that year.

Claremont returned to the character in X-Treme X-Men, launched in 2001, and killed her off in the second issue of that series. He resurrected her in Uncanny X-Men #455 (April 2005), and she subsequently became part of the cast of Exiles under writers Chris Claremont and Tony Bedard from 2007 to 2008. She returned to the X-Men titles in the 2009 limited series Psylocke, written by Christopher Yost with art by Harvey Tolibao, which addressed the fate of Kwannon's body.

Psylocke became a central character in Uncanny X-Force (2010–2012), written by Rick Remender, which was widely praised and earned the character renewed prominence. She continued in the second volume of Uncanny X-Force by Sam Humphries (2013–2014), and was a member of the all-female cast of X-Men (vol. 4) by Brian Wood, alongside Storm, Jubilee and Rachel Summers (then known as Rachel Grey). She appeared in Uncanny X-Men (vol. 4) by Cullen Bunn (2016–2017) and Astonishing X-Men (vol. 4) by Charles Soule (2017–2018).

In the 2018 limited series Hunt for Wolverine: Mystery in Madripoor, written by Jim Zub, Betsy was restored to a body resembling her original one, and Kwannon reclaimed her own. The two characters were separated permanently thereafter. Following the "House of X" and "Powers of X" relaunch in 2019, Betsy assumed the role of Captain Britain in Excalibur, written by Tini Howard with art by Marcus To. The series ran for 26 issues and was followed by Knights of X (2022) and the five-issue limited series Betsy Braddock: Captain Britain (2023), both by Howard. The decision to return Betsy to a British body and separate her from Kwannon was described by Marvel editorial as a response to long-standing criticism that the character's depiction as an Asian woman amounted to cultural appropriation.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Early life ==

Elizabeth Braddock is born in Maldon, Essex, England, the daughter of Sir James Braddock, a scientist from the extradimensional realm of Otherworld, and his wife Elizabeth. She has an older brother, Jamie, and a twin brother, Brian. Betsy and Brian are close as children, and from an early age she displays precognitive abilities that she keeps largely to herself. Her parents are killed in an explosion caused by the malfunctioning computer Mastermind, and Brian later becomes Captain Britain after an encounter with the legendary figure Merlyn.

As an adult, Betsy works as a charter pilot and then as a fashion model. Her telepathic powers emerge fully in this period, and she is recruited into the psi-division of S.T.R.I.K.E., a British counterpart to S.H.I.E.L.D. She and her fellow telepaths come under attack from the crime lord Vixen, who hires the assassin Slaymaster to eliminate the division. Betsy survives with the help of her brother, but her romantic partner, Tom Lennox, is killed during the conflict, and she experiences his death through their psychic link.

== Captain Britain and Psylocke ==

When Brian temporarily retires from his role, the government agency R.C.X. persuades Betsy to take his place as Captain Britain. She operates in the role for a short time before Slaymaster, who has returned, defeats her and gouges out her eyes. Brian arrives too late to prevent the attack but kills Slaymaster. Betsy's blindness leaves her reliant on her telepathy to perceive her surroundings.

Still recovering, Betsy is abducted by Mojo and Spiral, who implant her with bionic eyes that broadcast everything she sees to Mojo's television audience. She is rescued by the New Mutants and taken to the X-Men's mansion in Westchester, New York, where she is examined by Professor Charles Xavier's associates and offered the chance to remain. She takes the codename Psylocke and proves herself to the team when she confronts Sabretooth alone during the "Mutant Massacre", holding him off long enough for the other X-Men to return. Wolverine accepts her into the team on the basis of this act. Betsy serves alongside Storm, Rogue, Havok and Dazzler in Australia after the X-Men fake their deaths during the "Fall of the Mutants" storyline, and she develops a psychic armor to protect herself in combat.
