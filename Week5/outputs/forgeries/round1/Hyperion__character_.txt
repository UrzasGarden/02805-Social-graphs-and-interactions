Hyperion is the name of several fictional characters appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The first version was created by writer Roy Thomas and artist Sal Buscema and debuted in The Avengers #69 (October 1969) as a member of the Squadron Sinister, a team of villains assembled by the Grandmaster. A heroic counterpart from a parallel Earth, Mark Milton, followed in The Avengers #85 (February 1971) as a member of the Squadron Supreme. Both versions were conceived as pastiches of DC Comics' Superman, and the two Squadrons were modeled on the Justice League of America.

Since then, Marvel has introduced further versions of the character in different continuities, including a reimagined Mark Milton in the MAX imprint series Supreme Power (2003), the tyrannical King Hyperion of the Exiles series, and Marcus Milton, a refugee from a destroyed universe who became a member of the Avengers in 2013 and later headlined his own series. Depending on the version, Hyperion has been portrayed as a villain, a well-intentioned but authoritarian hero, and a straightforward superhero. The character's powers are consistently drawn from the Superman template: superhuman strength, flight, invulnerability and a form of energy vision.

Hyperion has appeared in media outside comics, including the animated series Avengers Assemble, in which he was voiced by Brian Bloom, and several Marvel video games.

== Publication history ==

The original Hyperion first appeared in The Avengers #69–70 (October–November 1969), written by Roy Thomas and drawn by Sal Buscema. Thomas later said that the Squadron Sinister was devised as a homage to the Justice League of America, with Hyperion standing in for Superman, Nighthawk for Batman, the Whizzer for the Flash and Doctor Spectrum for Green Lantern. The idea was coordinated informally with DC writer Mike Friedrich, who at the same time introduced analogues of the Avengers into Justice League of America. This Hyperion, whose name was later given as Zhib-Ran, returned in The Defenders #13–14 (May–July 1974), The Avengers Annual #8 (1978) and Thor #280 (February 1979).

Thomas reused the concept two years later for the Squadron Supreme, a heroic team from a parallel Earth, which appeared in The Avengers #85–86 (February–March 1971) with art by John Buscema. The Squadron Supreme's Hyperion, Mark Milton, became the better known of the two. The team was featured again in The Avengers #141–144 and #147–149 (1975–1976), in The Defenders #112–115 (1982–1983), and in the twelve-issue limited series Squadron Supreme (September 1985–August 1986), written by Mark Gruenwald with pencils by Bob Hall and Paul Ryan. Gruenwald's series, which examined what would happen if a team of superhumans took control of their world, is generally regarded as the character's defining appearance. It was followed by the graphic novel Squadron Supreme: Death of a Universe (1989), also by Gruenwald, after which the team appeared in Quasar #13–17 (1990) during Gruenwald's run on that title, in Avengers/Squadron Supreme Annual '98 and in the one-shot Squadron Supreme: New World Order (September 1998). The Earth-712 Hyperion later appeared in Exiles #77–78 (2006) and in the "Last Days" arc of Squadron Supreme in 2015.

In 2003, writer J. Michael Straczynski and artist Gary Frank launched Supreme Power under Marvel's mature-readers MAX imprint. The series rebuilt the Squadron Supreme from scratch in a new continuity, with Mark Milton depicted as an alien infant raised by the United States government as a weapon. The character received his own limited series, Supreme Power: Hyperion #1–5 (2005–2006), written by Straczynski and drawn by Dan Jurgens, and continued in Squadron Supreme vol. 2 (2006), Ultimate Power (2006–2008), Squadron Supreme vol. 3 (2008–2009) by Howard Chaykin and Marco Turini, and Supreme Power vol. 2 (2011).

A female Hyperion, Alice Nugent, was introduced by Fabian Nicieza and Tom Grummett in Thunderbolts #103–108 (2006). Nugent, a former laboratory assistant of Henry Pym, was granted powers by the Grandmaster's Wellspring of Power and served in a new Squadron Sinister. The same storyline established that Zhib-Ran had never been an alien but was a construct created by the Grandmaster.

The villainous King Hyperion first appeared in Exiles #38 (February 2004), written by Chuck Austen and drawn by Jim Calafiore, as an antagonist of the reality-hopping Exiles and Weapon X teams.

The most prominent recent version, Marcus Milton, was introduced by Jonathan Hickman and Jerome Opeña in Avengers vol. 5 #1 (February 2013) as a member of the expanded Avengers roster. His origin was told in Avengers vol. 5 #4, and he was a regular cast member of that title and of Avengers World through the "Infinity" and "Time Runs Out" storylines. Following Secret Wars (2015), he joined a new Squadron Supreme assembled from survivors of destroyed universes in Squadron Supreme vol. 4 (2015–2017), written by James Robinson and drawn by Leonard Kirk, and starred in the six-issue series Hyperion (2016) by Chuck Wendig and Nik Virella. He was a central character of the weekly storyline "Avengers: No Surrender" (2018) and later appeared in Jason Aaron's Avengers, which also introduced a separate Hyperion, a member of the government-sponsored Squadron Supreme of America, in Avengers vol. 8 #10 (December 2018). That version was the focus of the 2021 crossover "Heroes Reborn".

== Fictional character biography ==

== Squadron Sinister ==

The first Hyperion is one of four superhumans gathered by the Grandmaster to serve as his champions in a contest against Kang the Conqueror, who selects the Avengers as his own pieces. The Squadron Sinister is sent to attack landmarks on Earth, and Hyperion battles Thor, who shrinks him and seals him inside a glass sphere. Hyperion claims to be the last survivor of a civilization from a subatomic world destroyed by a scientific accident, which he was able to escape only by growing to human size.

Freed by the alien Nebulon, Hyperion rejoins the Squadron Sinister in a plan to melt the polar ice caps with a giant laser cannon in exchange for power over the planet. The scheme is stopped by the Defenders. He is briefly allied with the Avengers' enemy Thundra, with whom he develops a romantic attachment, and the two are abandoned by the rest of the Squadron during a later confrontation with the Avengers. Hyperion then impersonates his heroic counterpart on the parallel Earth of the Squadron Supreme, imprisoning Mark Milton in limbo for several months. When the real Hyperion returns, the two fight; the Squadron Sinister's Hyperion is killed, and Milton is left blind.

The Grandmaster later restores him, and he is revealed to be not an alien at all but an artificial being created by the Grandmaster, with false memories. He fights the Thunderbolts and Quasar on separate occasions, and his true origin is confirmed when the Grandmaster uses the Wellspring of Power to empower Alice Nugent as a new Hyperion in his place. Zhib-Ran subsequently appears as a member of the Squadron Sinister in a conflict with the Thunderbolts led by Luke Cage.

== Squadron Supreme ==

Mark Milton is the Hyperion of Earth-712, also called Earth-S, where he is the most powerful member of the Squadron Supreme and that world's premier hero. He first meets the Avengers when several of them are accidentally transported to his Earth, and the two teams cooperate to prevent a catastrophe caused by the Brain-Child. He is originally believed to be the sole survivor of an alien colony sent to Earth as an infant; later stories establish that he is the last of the Eternals of his world, a race comparable to the Eternals of the mainstream Marvel universe such as Makkari.

In the storyline told in The Avengers #141–149, the Squadron Supreme is manipulated by the Serpent Crown and by the corporation that effectively runs their world's government, bringing them into conflict with the Avengers. They are later mind-controlled by the Overmind and Null the Living Darkness, which leaves their Earth devastated; the Defenders help them regain their freedom.

In the Squadron Supreme limited series, Hyperion persuades his teammates to seize control of the United States and implement a "Utopia Program" intended to end crime, hunger and disease within a year. The program includes a behavior modification device used on criminals, which Nighthawk regards as a violation of free will. Nighthawk leaves the team and recruits the Redeemers to oppose it. During this period the Squadron Sinister's Hyperion secretly replaces Milton; the imposter begins a relationship with Power Princess, and when Milton escapes, the resulting battle blinds him. Milton continues to lead the Squadron and, after a civil war against the Redeemers in which several members on both sides die, concedes that the Utopia Program was a mistake and dismantles it.

In Death of a Universe, Hyperion and the remaining Squadron are stranded in the mainstream Marvel universe, where they are held for a time at Project Pegasus and work alongside Quasar before finding a way home. On their return they discover that their world has been taken over by the Global Directorate in their absence, and they resume their role as outlaws fighting to restore it. In later appearances the Earth-712 Hyperion encounters the Exiles and is briefly manipulated by King Hyperion. He is among the heroes killed when his universe is destroyed during the incursions preceding Secret Wars.
