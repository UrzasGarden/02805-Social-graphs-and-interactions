Mockingbird (Barbara "Bobbi" Morse) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Len Wein and artist Neal Adams, the character first appeared as a supporting cast member in the Ka-Zar feature in Astonishing Tales #6 (June 1971). A biologist and agent of the espionage agency S.H.I.E.L.D., Morse briefly operated under the code name Huntress before adopting the Mockingbird identity in Marvel Team-Up #95 (July 1980). She is best known as the wife of Clint Barton (Hawkeye) and as a founding member of the West Coast Avengers, and she has since served with the New Avengers and the Secret Avengers.

The character has appeared in a number of adaptations outside comics. Adrianne Palicki portrayed Bobbi Morse in the ABC television series Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D., and the character has also been featured in animated series and video games.

== Publication history ==

Barbara Morse debuted, without a nickname or costumed identity, in Astonishing Tales #6 (June 1971), in a Ka-Zar story written by Len Wein and drawn by Neal Adams. She was introduced as Dr. Barbara Morse, a scientist assisting Ka-Zar in the Savage Land. The nickname "Bobbi" was first used in Astonishing Tales #12 (June 1972), and in later Ka-Zar stories she was established as an agent of S.H.I.E.L.D. The Ka-Zar feature was later dropped from Astonishing Tales in favor of Deathlok, and Morse did not appear again for several years.

Morse first wore a costume as the Huntress in the black-and-white magazine Marvel Super Action #1 (January 1976). After DC Comics introduced its own Huntress in 1977, Marvel abandoned the name. The character returned as Mockingbird in Marvel Team-Up #95 (July 1980), written by Steven Grant and Mark Gruenwald with art by Jimmy Janes, in a story that paired her with Spider-Man against corrupt agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.

Gruenwald teamed the character with Hawkeye in the four-issue Hawkeye limited series (September–December 1983), at the end of which the two were married. The pairing led directly to her inclusion in the West Coast Avengers limited series (1984) and the ongoing series that followed in 1985, written in succession by Roger Stern, Steve Englehart, John Byrne, and Roy Thomas and Dann Thomas. The title was renamed Avengers West Coast in 1989, and Mockingbird was killed in Avengers West Coast #100 (November 1993). She remained dead in the main Marvel continuity for roughly fifteen years.

Writer Brian Michael Bendis restored the character in the crossover "Secret Invasion" (2008), revealing that the Mockingbird who had died was a Skrull impostor. Jim McCann and artist David López then wrote the limited series New Avengers: The Reunion #1–4 (2009), the ongoing Hawkeye & Mockingbird (2010–2011), which ended after six issues, and the follow-up crossover Widowmaker #1–4 (2011), which also featured Black Widow (Natasha Romanova). Mockingbird was a regular in Bendis's New Avengers vol. 2 (2010–2013) and in Secret Avengers vol. 2 (2013–2014) by Nick Spencer and Ales Kot.

Novelist Chelsea Cain wrote the one-shot Mockingbird: S.H.I.E.L.D. 50th Anniversary #1 (2015), drawn by Joëlle Jones, followed by the ongoing series Mockingbird (2016) with artist Kate Niemczyk. The series was cancelled after eight issues; its final cover, depicting the character in a T-shirt reading "Ask me about my feminist agenda", attracted online harassment that prompted Cain to leave Twitter. The series was later nominated for a GLAAD Media Award. In 2016 and 2017, Morse was a supporting character in Dan Slott's The Amazing Spider-Man, in which she was in a relationship with Peter Parker, and she subsequently appeared in Hunt for Wolverine: Mystery in Madripoor (2018).

== Fictional character biography ==

Barbara Morse is born in San Diego, California. A gifted student, she earns a doctorate in biology and is recruited to work at Project Gladiator, a S.H.I.E.L.D. research program attempting to recreate the Super-Soldier Serum that had produced Captain America. Her mentor on the project is Dr. Wilma Calvin, and her colleagues include Ted Sallis, whose own experiments later transform him into the Man-Thing. While investigating threats to the project, Morse travels to the Savage Land, where she works alongside Ka-Zar and demonstrates the fighting ability that leads S.H.I.E.L.D. to train her as a field agent. She is given the designation Agent 19.

Morse later uncovers corruption within S.H.I.E.L.D. and reports it to its director, Nick Fury. When her findings are ignored, she adopts the identity of the Huntress to investigate on her own, and soon renames herself Mockingbird. Her inquiry draws the attention of the agents she has exposed, who shoot her and leave her near death; Spider-Man, Fury and the Avengers' Jarvis intervene and she recovers. Fury clears her name, but she leaves S.H.I.E.L.D. and begins operating independently.

While working for the private firm Cross Technological Enterprises, Mockingbird meets Hawkeye, who has been hired as the company's security chief. The two uncover a scheme by the company's owner, Crossfire, to brainwash superheroes with ultrasonic technology. During the fight Hawkeye is partly deafened, and shortly afterward the pair are married. When Hawkeye is assigned to found a West Coast branch of the Avengers, Mockingbird becomes a founding member, serving alongside Iron Man, Wonder Man and Tigra. In the team's early years she clashes with foes including Graviton, Ultron and Quicksilver, who at the time is estranged from his family and the Avengers.

During a time-travel misadventure in the Old West, Mockingbird is abducted by Lincoln Slade, the Phantom Rider, who drugs her and makes her his lover against her will. When she regains her senses she pursues Slade to a cliff, and when he loses his footing she allows him to fall to his death. Hawkeye, who holds to a strict rule against killing, discovers what happened and the marriage collapses. Mockingbird leaves the West Coast Avengers, taking Moon Knight and Tigra with her into a short-lived splinter group, and she is haunted by Slade's vengeful ghost before his descendant Hamilton Slade helps lay the spirit to rest.

Mockingbird and Hawkeye eventually reconcile and rejoin the team, whose membership by then includes War Machine, Spider-Woman (Julia Carpenter) and U.S. Agent. In the storyline culminating in Avengers West Coast #100, the team is drawn into a conflict between the demons Satannish and Mephisto. When Mephisto hurls a bolt of hellfire at Hawkeye, Mockingbird throws herself in front of it and dies in her husband's arms. A grieving Hawkeye seeks the help of Doctor Strange and even bargains with Mephisto in an attempt to restore her, but she remains dead for years, and Hawkeye is himself killed and resurrected before learning the truth about her fate.

In "Secret Invasion", it is revealed that the Mockingbird who died was a Skrull who had taken her place during the Phantom Rider affair, and that the real Bobbi Morse has been held captive on a Skrull ship ever since. She is returned to Earth with other abducted heroes and reunites with Hawkeye, now operating as Ronin with the New Avengers. Struggling to adjust after years of imprisonment, and uncertain how much of her captors' tampering with her memory she can trust, she declines to resume the marriage immediately. She instead founds the World Counterterrorism Agency, a small private intelligence organization staffed by former S.H.I.E.L.D. personnel, with Hawkeye as her partner. The pair fight Crossfire and a new Phantom Rider, Jaime Slade, and in the course of the conflict Mockingbird's mother is badly injured. The couple finally divorce, though they continue to work together.

During the "Widowmaker" storyline, Mockingbird, Hawkeye and Black Widow investigate a conspiracy within the Russian intelligence community. Shortly afterward, while serving with the New Avengers, she is shot by the terrorist Superia and left close to death. Nick Fury saves her by injecting her with a mixture of the Super-Soldier Serum and the Infinity Formula that has prolonged his own life, enhancing her strength, speed and recuperative abilities. She then joins the Secret Avengers, a covert team run by S.H.I.E.L.D. with Black Widow, Hawkeye and Spider-Woman (Jessica Drew), and later returns to full-time S.H.I.E.L.D. duty, where it is established that she was once married to fellow agent Lance Hunter.
