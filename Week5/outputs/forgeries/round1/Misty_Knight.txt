Mercedes "Misty" Knight is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Tony Isabella and artist Arvell Jones and made her first full appearance in Marvel Premiere #21 (March 1975), although an unnamed police officer drawn by Ross Andru in Marvel Team-Up #1 (March 1972) was later identified as her. A former officer of the New York City Police Department, Knight lost her right arm in a bombing and received a bionic replacement built by Tony Stark. With her partner Colleen Wing she runs the private investigation agency Nightwing Restorations, and the two are collectively known as the Daughters of the Dragon. Knight has been a long-standing ally and romantic partner of Iron Fist, a close associate of Luke Cage, and a leader of several incarnations of Heroes for Hire.

Knight is regarded as one of the first African-American female heroes in mainstream American comics, and her creation is usually tied to the blaxploitation films of the early 1970s. Simone Missick portrayed the character in the Marvel Cinematic Universe television series Luke Cage (2016–2018), The Defenders (2017) and Iron Fist (2018).

== Publication history ==

Tony Isabella has said that he and Arvell Jones devised Misty Knight for a proposed series that would have paired a Black female detective with a martial arts setting, drawing on the popularity of Pam Grier's films Coffy (1973) and Foxy Brown (1974). The project was not approved as a standalone title, and Isabella instead introduced the character in the Iron Fist feature in Marvel Premiere. She is glimpsed in shadow in Marvel Premiere #20 (January 1975) and is named in the following issue. Jones designed the character's original look, including the large afro and leather jacket associated with her 1970s appearances.

Knight became a regular supporting character when Chris Claremont and John Byrne took over the Iron Fist feature and then the Iron Fist ongoing series (1975–1977). Claremont and Byrne developed her romance with Danny Rand, established her partnership with Colleen Wing, and made her the roommate of Jean Grey in the pages of X-Men, linking the two titles. The name Daughters of the Dragon was first used for Knight and Wing in a Claremont-written story in the black-and-white magazine The Deadly Hands of Kung Fu #32 (January 1977), with art by Marshall Rogers. After Iron Fist was cancelled, Knight continued as a supporting character throughout Power Man and Iron Fist (1978–1986), where the loss of her arm and her bionic replacement were first explained in detail.

The character received her first headlining title with Daughters of the Dragon, a six-issue limited series by Jimmy Palmiotti, Justin Gray and Khari Evans published in 2006. The same creative team launched a new Heroes for Hire series later that year, which ran for 15 issues and placed Knight and Wing in charge of the team during the "Civil War" crossover. She appeared in Shadowland: Blood on the Streets (2010) by Antony Johnston and Wellinton Alves, and was then a lead in Heroes for Hire vol. 3 (2010–2011) by Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning, with art by Brad Walker, as well as the follow-up Villains for Hire (2011–2012). Cullen Bunn and Will Sliney paired her with Valkyrie in Fearless Defenders (2013). She was a prominent supporting character in Captain America: Sam Wilson (2015–2017) by Nick Spencer, and starred with Wing again in a digital Daughters of the Dragon series by Jed MacKay in 2018.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Early life ==

Mercedes Knight is born and raised in New York City. She joins the New York City Police Department and is partnered with Rafael Scarfe, with whom she builds a reputation as a tough and capable detective. While responding to a bombing, Knight attempts to carry an explosive device away from a crowd; it detonates and destroys her right arm. The department offers her a desk assignment, which she refuses, and she resigns from the force. Tony Stark, who learns of her injury, provides her with a bionic arm of his own design, giving her a degree of strength in that limb far beyond that of an ordinary person.

Knight first meets Colleen Wing when she saves the younger woman's life during a shootout. The two become close friends and open Nightwing Restorations, Ltd., a private investigation and bail bonds agency. Their reputation among the city's criminals earns them the nickname Daughters of the Dragon. Through Wing, Knight meets Danny Rand, the martial artist Iron Fist, who has recently returned to New York from the mystical city of K'un-Lun. The two begin a relationship that endures, with several interruptions, for much of the character's history.

== Iron Fist and the X-Men ==

During this period Knight shares an apartment with Jean Grey, then operating as Phoenix, and becomes acquainted with the X-Men. When the alien Firelord attacks Grey, Knight is caught up in the fighting, and she is present when a misunderstanding leads Iron Fist into a brawl with the X-Men, including Wolverine, in her own apartment. Knight is kidnapped by the mercenary Sabretooth, who was at that time an enemy of Iron Fist, and is rescued by Rand and Wing.

Knight infiltrates the organization of the crime lord Bushmaster under the alias Maya Korday. Bushmaster discovers her identity, takes her hostage, and attempts to use her as leverage to force Luke Cage to capture Knight's friends. The scheme fails when Iron Fist and Wing come to her aid, and the evidence recovered in the case helps clear Cage of the false charges that originally sent him to prison. The episode brings Cage and Rand together as Heroes for Hire, and Knight and Wing become regular associates of the firm, frequently handling investigations and acting as backup.

Knight and Rand's relationship becomes strained when he disappears while apparently dying of radiation poisoning; she is unaware that the Rand who died was in fact a plant-based duplicate created by the H'ylthri. In the interval she is briefly involved with Tyrone King, who is later revealed to be the villainous Master Khan. When the real Rand returns, the two slowly reconcile, although Knight remains wary of committing fully. She also develops a complicated working relationship with the mercenary Paladin, who alternately assists and double-crosses her over the years.

== Daughters of the Dragon ==

When the socialite and criminal Celia Ricadonna frames the pair over a stolen microchip, Knight and Wing find themselves hunted by much of New York's underworld. Ricadonna severs Knight's bionic arm during their confrontation, and Knight finishes the fight with one arm before Stark fits her with an upgraded replacement. The new limb is later described as incorporating Antarctic vibranium, the so-called anti-metal, which allows her to disrupt and liquefy other metals on contact.

== "Civil War" and Heroes for Hire ==

In the storyline "Civil War", Iron Man and Mister Fantastic approach Knight and Wing to lead a new Heroes for Hire that will hunt down superhumans who refuse to register under the Superhuman Registration Act. Knight accepts, reasoning that she can offer unregistered heroes a fairer deal than the government's agents. The team she assembles includes Shang-Chi, Humbug, Orka, the Tarantula (Maria Vasquez), the Black Cat and Paladin. Her decision puts her at odds with Rand, who sides with Captain America's resistance, and the death of Bill Foster at the hands of a clone of Thor deepens the team's unease about the side they have chosen.

After the war Knight's Heroes for Hire take on a series of jobs that bring them into conflict with Doctor Doom's forces and the Headmen. During "World War Hulk" the team attempts to infiltrate the Hulk's warbound ship and is nearly destroyed, with Humbug betraying them and Wing badly injured. Knight disbands the group, and her relationship with Wing is damaged for a time. She returns to working with Rand, and briefly believes she is pregnant with his child before learning the pregnancy was false, after which the two separate once more.
