Spider-Bitch (Ashley Parker Barton) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Mark Millar and artist Steve McNiven, the character first appeared in Wolverine #67 (September 2008) as part of the "Old Man Logan" storyline. She is the daughter of Clint Barton (Hawkeye) and Tonya Parker, which makes her a granddaughter of Peter Parker (Spider-Man). Within the dystopian alternate future of Earth-807128, she operates as a costumed criminal who styles herself after her grandfather and eventually seizes control of a territory once held by the Kingpin.

Although she originated in a single storyline, the character has since appeared in a number of follow-up series set in the same future, as well as in the multiversal crossovers "Spider-Verse" and "Spider-Geddon", where she was grouped with other spider-powered characters such as Mayday Parker, Spider-Woman (Gwen Stacy) and Spider-Woman (Jessica Drew). In later stories she has used the names Spider-Woman and Spider-Girl. The character has also been adapted for the Marvel's Wastelanders audio dramas.

== Publication history ==

Ashley Barton was introduced in "Old Man Logan", an eight-part storyline written by Mark Millar and drawn by Steve McNiven that ran in Wolverine #66–72 (August 2008 – October 2009) and concluded in Wolverine: Old Man Logan Giant-Size #1 (September 2009). The story followed an elderly Logan and a blind Clint Barton on a journey across a United States that had been divided among supervillains fifty years earlier. Millar conceived the supporting cast as the descendants and remnants of established Marvel characters, and Ashley was written as a product of Barton's third marriage, to the daughter of Spider-Man. Her first full appearance was in the third chapter, Wolverine #68 (October 2008), where she was shown in a modified version of Spider-Man's costume and identified by the name Spider-Bitch.

The character was not used again until Dan Slott's "Spider-Verse" event (2014–2015), which gathered spider-powered characters from across the Marvel multiverse. Ashley appeared in Spider-Verse #2 (March 2015) in a short story that placed her among the recruits, and she was included in related material such as the Spider-Verse trading cards and handbook entries, where her alias was given as Spider-Woman. She returned in Spider-Geddon #1 (December 2018) and in the anthology tie-in Vault of Spiders, again as part of the wider group of alternate Spider-Men and Spider-Women.

Following the success of the original storyline, Marvel published several series set in the same future. Ashley appeared as a child in Old Man Hawkeye (2018), a twelve-issue prequel by Ethan Sacks and Marco Checchetto, and as an adult in Ed Brisson and Mike Henderson's Dead Man Logan (2018–2019), which returned Logan to the Wastelands and depicted the state of the former Kingpin territory under her control. She also featured in Sacks's Old Man Quill (2019), in which Star-Lord and Rocket Raccoon of the Guardians of the Galaxy arrive on the ruined Earth. The character was subsequently used in the Wastelanders line of one-shots published in 2021 and 2022 to accompany the audio dramas of the same name.

== Fictional character biography ==

== "Old Man Logan" ==

Ashley Barton is born in the years after the villains of the world unite and overwhelm the superheroes of Earth-807128. Her father is Clint Barton, the former Avenger Hawkeye, who had previously been married to Mockingbird and who takes as his third wife Tonya Parker, the daughter of Peter Parker and Mary Jane Watson. The marriage does not last, and Ashley grows up in the territory ruled by the Kingpin, a successor to the original Wilson Fisk who had taken the region by killing Magneto. As an adult she inherits a measure of her grandfather's abilities and adopts a costume and alias modelled on his, calling herself Spider-Bitch.

Together with a gang of similarly costumed followers, including men dressed as Daredevil and the Punisher, Ashley leads an uprising against the Kingpin. The coup fails, her accomplices are executed in the arena the Kingpin maintains in a former sports stadium, and Ashley is imprisoned. Word reaches her father, who agrees to drive a shipment across the country for the money he needs and asks Logan, his travelling companion, to help him free her along the way. Hawkeye tells Logan that Ashley is the only one of his children he is still able to help, and the two men break into the Kingpin's compound and reach her cell.

Once released, Ashley kills the Kingpin by decapitating him and reveals that she never intended to be rescued for her own sake. She announces that the territory now belongs to her and orders her followers to kill her father, explaining that she has no use for a blind old man who still thinks of himself as a hero. Hawkeye and Logan escape, with Logan refusing to retaliate against her because of his vow never to use his claws again. The episode confirms Hawkeye's belief that the next generation has been shaped entirely by the world the villains built, a theme that recurs throughout the storyline.

== "Spider-Verse" and "Spider-Geddon" ==

In the storyline "Spider-Verse", the Inheritors, a family of beings who feed on the life force of spider-totems, begin hunting spider-powered individuals across the multiverse. Ashley is among those identified as a totem and is drawn into the conflict. She joins the army of spider-people assembled by the Spider-Man of Earth-616 and fights alongside counterparts such as Mayday Parker, Gwen Stacy of Earth-65 and Jessica Drew, as well as the Spider-Man who has merged with the Enigma Force as Captain Universe. Her abrasive manner and willingness to kill set her apart from most of the recruits, and she is depicted as working with the group largely out of self-interest.

She survives the war against the Inheritors and returns to her own world. When the Inheritors escape their imprisonment in "Spider-Geddon", the Web-Warriors send out a renewed call for help, and Ashley is again counted among the spider-totems who answer. In these appearances the supporting material refers to her as the Spider-Woman of Earth-807128 rather than by her original alias.

== Later appearances in the Wastelands ==

In Old Man Hawkeye, set five years before "Old Man Logan", Ashley is still a child living with her mother. Clint Barton, who is losing his sight, visits her before setting out to take revenge on the former Thunderbolts who betrayed the Avengers. The series establishes the strained relationship between father and daughter that informs her later actions.

Dead Man Logan returns to Earth-807128 some time after the events of the original story. Ashley has consolidated her hold over the former Kingpin territory and runs it as a crime lord, with the arena still in use. When Logan returns to the Wastelands to confront the surviving Hulk Gang and other threats, including the symbiote Venom, which has bonded to a tyrannosaurus, their paths cross again. Ashley initially treats Logan as an enemy, but she is eventually drawn into an alliance against a greater danger, and the series leaves her in charge of her region.

In Old Man Quill, Peter Quill and the remaining Guardians of the Galaxy, among them Rocket Raccoon, arrive on Earth in pursuit of the Universal Church of Truth and travel through the territories of the Wastelands. Their journey brings them into contact with the surviving power brokers of the region, including Ashley, who deals with the newcomers on her own terms. The character later appears in the Wastelanders one-shots, where her domain is shown alongside those controlled by the heirs of the Hulk and the Kingpin's other successors.

== Powers and abilities ==

As a descendant of Peter Parker, Ashley Barton possesses a diluted version of his spider-based powers. She has superhuman strength, speed, agility and reflexes, can cling to walls and other surfaces, and appears to have at least a limited form of the precognitive spider-sense. Her abilities are presented as weaker than those of her grandfather, and she compensates with ruthlessness and a readiness to use lethal force. In "Old Man Logan" she is shown using a bladed weapon to kill the Kingpin rather than relying on her physical powers alone.

Ashley is also a capable organizer and strategist. She assembles and commands her own gang, plans the coup against the Kingpin, and later administers a large territory in a lawless landscape, which indicates skills in leadership and intimidation that are independent of her inherited powers. Unlike her father, who retained his marksmanship but lost his sight, she has no reliance on specialized equipment beyond her costume, which is a dark variant of the classic Spider-Man design.
