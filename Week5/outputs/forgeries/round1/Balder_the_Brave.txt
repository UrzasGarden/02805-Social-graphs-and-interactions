Balder the Brave is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character is loosely based on Baldr, the god of light in Norse mythology. Created by writer Stan Lee, scripter Larry Lieber and artist Jack Kirby, he first appeared in Journey into Mystery #85 (October 1962). Balder is an Asgardian warrior and one of the closest companions of Thor; later stories reveal that he is the son of Odin and Frigga and therefore Thor's half-brother. He has twice served as ruler of Asgard, and he is frequently depicted alongside the Warriors Three (Fandral, Hogun and Volstagg) and the Norn queen Karnilla, who loves him.

Balder has appeared in animated adaptations featuring Thor and in several video games.

== Publication history ==

Balder was among the first Asgardian supporting characters introduced after Thor's debut, appearing in Journey into Mystery #85 (October 1962), the same issue in which Asgard, Odin, Heimdall and Loki were first shown. The issue was plotted by Stan Lee, scripted by Larry Lieber and drawn by Jack Kirby. Like Hercules, whom Lee and Kirby later adapted from Greek myth in Journey into Mystery Annual #1 (1965), Balder was one of several mythological figures reworked to fit the Marvel Universe, retaining his traditional association with light and his vulnerability to mistletoe while being recast as a sword-wielding warrior.

The character became a regular in the "Tales of Asgard" backup feature that began in Journey into Mystery #97 (October 1963), which recounted the early lives of the Asgardians. Karnilla, the Norn queen whose attraction to Balder would become a long-running plot element, was introduced in Journey into Mystery #107 (August 1964). Through the late 1960s and 1970s Balder appeared regularly in Thor as an ally in the title character's battles, usually in company with the Warriors Three.

Writer Roy Thomas and artist John Buscema made Balder's death the centerpiece of the "Ragnarok" storyline that ran through Thor #272–278 in 1978, following the sequence of the Norse myths in which the death of Baldr heralds the end of the gods. Writer-artist Walt Simonson substantially revised the character during his run on Thor (1983–1987), reintroducing him in Thor #344 (June 1984) as a traumatized pacifist who had renounced warfare. Simonson later wrote the four-issue limited series Balder the Brave (November 1985 – February 1986), illustrated by Sal Buscema, in which Balder recovered his resolve and emerged as a leader. During this period Balder was installed as ruler of Asgard in Odin's absence, a position he held through the remainder of Simonson's run and into the subsequent issues written by Tom DeFalco.

Balder was killed along with most of the Asgardians in the "Ragnarok" storyline in Thor (vol. 2) #80–85 (2004). He returned in the third volume of Thor, launched in 2007 by writer J. Michael Straczynski and artist Olivier Coipel, in which the Asgardians were restored and settled near Broxton, Oklahoma. Thor (vol. 3) #10 (2008) revealed Balder to be a son of Odin, and in the issues that followed he once again became king of Asgard, a status that carried into the 2010 crossover "Siege". The character has continued to appear in Thor-related titles since.

== Fictional character biography ==

== Early life ==

Balder is born in Asgard, the son of Odin and his wife Frigga, although his parentage is concealed from him and from the court for most of his life; he grows up believing himself to be the child of other Asgardian nobles and regards Thor as a friend rather than a brother. A prophecy foretells that Balder's death will be the first sign of Ragnarok, the destruction of the gods. To protect him, Frigga extracts a promise from every living thing and every object in the Nine Worlds that none will harm her son, overlooking only the mistletoe plant, and Odin reinforces this protection with his own enchantments. As a result Balder becomes nearly impossible to injure by ordinary means.

Balder grows into an accomplished warrior, earning the epithet "the Brave" for his conduct in Asgard's wars. He becomes a boon companion of Thor and frequently rides into battle with the Warriors Three, Fandral, Hogun and Volstagg, against Frost Giants, Trolls and the schemes of Loki. Odin sometimes dispatches Balder as his herald and emissary, and he is repeatedly sent to Earth to summon or assist Thor during the latter's adventures among mortals.

Balder's personal life is shaped by two women. He loves Nanna, a gentle Asgardian who returns his affection, but he is also desired by Karnilla, the sorcerous queen of the Norns, who repeatedly attempts to win him by enchantment and bargain. When Karnilla's magic places Balder under her power, Nanna sacrifices her own life to free him. Balder blames Karnilla for her death and refuses her, although she continues to intervene on Asgard's behalf when his safety is at stake.

== Death and Ragnarok ==

In the storyline "Ragnarok", Loki sets in motion the events foretold in prophecy. Knowing that mistletoe alone can harm Balder, Loki fashions a dart from the plant and guides the hand of the blind god Hoder, who throws it during a contest in which the Asgardians amuse themselves by hurling weapons at the invulnerable Balder. The dart kills him, and his death triggers the onset of the final battle. Hela takes his soul into her realm while Asgard descends into war. Odin and Thor ultimately halt the cycle of destruction before Asgard is destroyed, and Balder is returned to life, although his time among the dead leaves a lasting mark on him.

Balder dies again when Odin pours the life force of the Asgardians into the Destroyer to confront the Fourth Host of the Celestials, and is revived when Thor gathers energy from the gods of other pantheons to restore his people. His second passage through Hel is far more harrowing: in the realm of the dead he is confronted by the spirits of the warriors he killed in life. Upon his return he renounces violence entirely, lays down his sword, grows a beard and retreats from court, taking refuge in food and drink until he becomes overweight and withdrawn.

== Ruler of Asgard ==

Despite Balder's refusal to fight, Odin sends him as an envoy to Karnilla when the fire demon Surtur prepares to invade Asgard, reasoning that only Balder can persuade the Norn queen to lend her forces. Karnilla agrees on condition that Balder remain with her, and he submits for the sake of the realm. When Surtur's armies attack, Balder is drawn back into combat in spite of himself, and Odin vanishes into Surtur's dimension while locked in struggle with the demon. With the throne empty, Thor declines the crown, and the Asgardians name Balder their ruler.

Soon afterward Balder travels to Nornheim and discovers that Karnilla's kingdom has been overrun by the Frost Giants of Utgard-Loki. Freed of his beard and his despair, he takes up arms to defend Karnilla's people, defeats the giants and rescues the queen. In the course of this campaign he acknowledges that he has come to love Karnilla, though his duties as king keep the two apart. Balder governs Asgard for a long period, during which he confronts further intrigues by Loki, the machinations of Hela and the invasion of the Egyptian death god Seth. When Odin at last returns, Balder relinquishes the throne and resumes his place as one of Asgard's foremost warriors.
