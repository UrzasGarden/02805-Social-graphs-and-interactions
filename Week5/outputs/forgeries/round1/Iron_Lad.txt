Iron Lad (Nathaniel "Nate" Richards) is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character was created by writer Allan Heinberg and artist Jim Cheung and first appeared in Young Avengers #1 (April 2005). He is a teenage version of the time-travelling villain Kang the Conqueror who rejects his future self and founds the Young Avengers, a team of adolescent heroes that includes Hulkling, Wiccan and Patriot.

== Publication history ==

Iron Lad debuted in Young Avengers #1 (April 2005) as the apparent leader of the new team. Heinberg and Cheung deliberately modelled the founding members on established Avengers, with Iron Lad serving as the analogue of Iron Man. The character's identity was withheld for the first issues of the series, and readers speculated that he might be a young Tony Stark or Kristoff Vernard, the former ward of Doctor Doom, before he was revealed as a young Kang in the opening storyline.

Iron Lad left the series at the end of the first arc, Young Avengers #1–6 (2005), and his departure led to the creation of a new Vision who carried his personality. He returned in the limited series Avengers: The Children's Crusade #1–9 (2010–2012), again written by Heinberg and drawn by Cheung, which concluded the original team's story. The character has since appeared in flashbacks and in later Young Avengers material, usually as a figure defined by his relationship to Kang.

== Fictional character biography ==

Nathaniel Richards is a sixteen-year-old living in the 30th century of an alternate timeline. After he is severely beaten by other students, he is visited by his adult self, Kang the Conqueror, who heals him with futuristic technology and shows him the life of conquest that awaits. Horrified by what he will become, Nathaniel steals a suit of Kang's armor and escapes into the past, arriving in the present day in the hope that the Avengers will protect him from his older self.

He finds that the team has disbanded following the events of "Avengers Disassembled". Recovering the remains of the Vision, he downloads the android's operating system into his armor and discovers a contingency plan that identifies potential recruits should the Avengers ever fall. Taking the name Iron Lad, he seeks out Teddy Altman (Hulkling), Billy Kaplan (then calling himself Asgardian) and Eli Bradley (Patriot), and the four begin operating in New York. Cassie Lang and Kate Bishop later join them, and Iron Lad develops a romantic attachment to Cassie.

When Kang arrives to retrieve him, Iron Lad refuses to return and kills his future self during the battle. The paradox begins to unravel history, and the Young Avengers find their own pasts changing. Realising that he cannot escape his destiny without destroying the present, Iron Lad agrees to go back to his own time and live out the life he fled. Before leaving, he removes his armor; the Vision's program within it, fused with Nate's brain patterns, becomes an independent being who adopts the Vision's name and his feelings for Cassie.

In Avengers: The Children's Crusade, the Young Avengers search for the missing Scarlet Witch, whom Wiccan believes to be his mother. When the Avengers and the X-Men, led in part by Wolverine, move to capture or kill Wanda in Latveria, an older Iron Lad reappears and carries the team into the past so that Wanda can recover her memories. He also rescues Cassie's father, Scott Lang, moments before his recorded death. In the final confrontation with Doctor Doom, Cassie is killed. Iron Lad proposes taking her body into the future to revive her; when the Vision objects, Iron Lad destroys the android and departs, declaring that he will no longer be the person Kang was but something greater.

== Powers and abilities ==

Nathaniel Richards has no superhuman powers of his own but possesses a genius-level intellect and a thorough knowledge of future science. His armor, taken from Kang, is composed of a neurokinetic alloy that responds to his thoughts and can reshape itself at will. It provides flight, enhanced strength, energy projection, force fields, holographic disguises and the ability to interface with and reprogram computer systems, including the Vision. The suit also contains Kang's time-travel technology, which Iron Lad can use to move himself and others through history, though he grows reluctant to do so for fear of creating further paradoxes.

== Other versions ==

The one-shot Avengers: The Children's Crusade – Young Avengers (2011) presents a possible future in which Iron Lad has fully become Kang and the surviving Young Avengers face him as adults. In the main continuity Kang himself has stated that Iron Lad is only one of several divergent younger selves, including Kid Immortus, who appeared in the 2013 Young Avengers series as a rival to the team. Alternate takes on Iron Lad have also appeared in Marvel's Exiles and in What If? stories exploring the consequences of his killing of Kang.

== In other media ==

Iron Lad appears as a playable character in the mobile game Marvel Avengers Academy, where he is unlocked during an event based on the Young Avengers.

== Reception ==

In 2021, Screen Rant placed Iron Lad among the ten most powerful members of the Young Avengers, citing the capabilities of the armor and his connection to Kang. CBR.com has described him as one of the more tragic figures of the team because of the inevitability of his transformation into a villain.
