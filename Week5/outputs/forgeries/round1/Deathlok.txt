Deathlok (also referred to as Deathlok the Demolisher) is the name of several fictional characters appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The original Deathlok, Luther Manning, was created by writer-artist Rich Buckler and writer Doug Moench and first appeared in Astonishing Tales #25 (August 1974). The name has since been applied to a series of cyborgs built around the same basic concept: a dead or dying human whose remains are fused with mechanical parts and governed by an onboard computer, and who then rebels against the people who made him.

Later bearers of the name include John Kelly, the pacifist computer programmer Michael Collins, S.H.I.E.L.D. agent Jack Truman, the civilian Larry Young and the field medic Henry Hayes. The Collins version, introduced in 1990, headlined the longest-running Deathlok series and is the most frequently used in the wider Marvel Universe, where he has worked alongside Spider-Man, the Black Panther and the Avengers. A version of the character, Mike Peterson, was adapted for the live-action television series Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D., played by J. August Richards.

== Publication history ==

Deathlok was conceived by Rich Buckler, who had developed the idea of a cyborg soldier in a ruined future before pitching it to Marvel. Buckler drew the character's early stories and scripted them with Doug Moench, and the feature debuted in Astonishing Tales #25 (August 1974). The strip ran in Astonishing Tales #25–28 and #30–36 (1974–1976), and a further story planned for the series was eventually published in Marvel Spotlight #33 (April 1977). The character's post-apocalyptic setting was presented as an alternate future rather than the mainstream Marvel timeline, and the feature was notable for its use of the computer's dialogue, rendered in a distinct typeface, as a running counterpoint to Manning's own thoughts.

After the cancellation of Astonishing Tales, the Manning Deathlok appeared sporadically as a guest character. He met Spider-Man in Marvel Team-Up #46 (June 1976), was brought into the present day in Marvel Two-in-One #27 (May 1977) and returned in Marvel Two-in-One #54 (August 1979). A three-part storyline in Captain America #286–288 (October–December 1983), by J. M. DeMatteis and Mike Zeck, took Captain America into Deathlok's future and brought the character's original story to a conclusion. Marvel later tied Manning's timeline to the same general future era that produced Iron Man 2020 (Arno Stark).

Marvel relaunched the concept in 1990 with a four-issue Deathlok limited series (July–October 1990) by writers Dwayne McDuffie and Gregory Wright and penciller Jackson Guice, which introduced Michael Collins. The series was preceded by a short feature in Marvel Comics Presents #62 (1990), by Wright and Guice, that introduced John Kelly as the subject of an earlier Deathlok experiment. The limited series led directly into an ongoing title, Deathlok vol. 2, which ran 34 issues (July 1991–April 1994) with two annuals; McDuffie and Wright wrote the bulk of the run, with Denys Cowan and later Walter McDaniel among the regular artists. The ongoing series brought Collins into contact with much of the Marvel Universe, including Spider-Man, Misty Knight, the Black Panther, Quasar and the Abomination, and reintroduced Luther Manning and the original Deathlok's world.

A third volume, written by Joe Casey and drawn by Leonardo Manco, ran 11 issues (September 1999–June 2000) and introduced Jack Truman. Collins and Truman subsequently appeared as supporting characters in titles such as Beyond! (2006) and Thunderbolts. A seven-issue limited series, Deathlok (vol. 4, November 2009–May 2010), written by Charlie Huston with art by Lan Medina, retold the Manning concept in a new alternate future. Rick Remender introduced Deathlok Prime, a future cyborg based on the Manning template, in Uncanny X-Force (2010–2012). Following the character's adaptation on television, Marvel launched Deathlok vol. 5 (December 2014–September 2015), written by Nathan Edmondson and drawn by Mike Perkins, which featured the new Deathlok Henry Hayes; Hayes had first appeared in Original Sins #1 (June 2014).

== Fictional character biography ==

== Luther Manning ==

Luther Manning is a colonel in the United States Army in a future timeline designated Earth-7484, in which American society collapses in the early 1990s. During war games Manning is fatally wounded by an explosion. Rather than allow him to die, Major Simon Ryker has what remains of his body rebuilt as a cyborg, grafting mechanical limbs and weapons onto the surviving organic tissue and implanting a computer that shares control of the body. Manning awakens with no memory of what has happened and refers to his electronic other half simply as "'Puter."

Manning learns that five years have passed, that his wife Janice believes him dead and has married his former friend Mike Travers, and that his son Richard has grown up without him. Ryker intends to use Deathlok as the first of an army of obedient cyborg soldiers, but Manning's personality reasserts itself and he escapes into the ruins of New York, which are controlled by cannibal gangs and competing military factions. He spends much of his early career fighting Ryker, who undergoes successive transformations in pursuit of power, eventually having his own mind merged with a computer network as the Savior Machine. Manning also encounters the resistance leader Godwulf and his band of rebels, who become uneasy allies.

Deathlok is transported into the mainstream present on several occasions. In one instance the Fixer and Mentallo gain control of his cybernetic systems and send him to assassinate the President of the United States, a plan that is foiled by the Thing. He is later kept in storage at Project Pegasus, where he is briefly reactivated. In the storyline in Captain America #286–288, Godwulf brings Captain America forward in time to help overthrow the computer intelligence that has taken over Manning's world. In the course of the conflict Manning is confronted with a clone of himself that has been grown by Ryker's organization and that lives a normal human life. The clone's existence allows the original Manning to accept his condition, and the cyborg remains in his own time after Captain America returns to the present.

Manning's history is revisited in Deathlok vol. 2, in which Michael Collins is drawn into the Earth-7484 timeline and encounters both the cyborg Manning and his clone. The two Deathloks fight alongside one another against Timestream, a time-traveling criminal, and Manning afterward appears intermittently in the present, including in the company of other displaced characters.

== John Kelly ==

John Kelly is a soldier whose brain is placed in the prototype Deathlok cyborg built by the corporation Cybertek, a subsidiary of Roxxon. Kelly's personality proves unable to override the body's computer during a field test and, when he refuses to kill, the computer removes him from control and allows his mind to be destroyed. The prototype is dismantled and its components, along with the lessons learned from Kelly's failure, are used in the construction of the next Deathlok body. Kelly's consciousness is later found to have survived in digital form, and he is eventually given a new mechanical body under the codename Siege.

== Michael Collins ==

Michael Collins is a pacifist computer programmer and expert in prosthetics employed by Cybertek. He believes his work is intended for medical use until he discovers that Harlan Ryker, the head of the project and brother of Simon Ryker, has been using his software for the Deathlok weapons program. When Collins objects, Ryker has him sedated and his brain transplanted into the Deathlok body, intending to use it as a controlling organic component with no independent will. Collins's family, his wife Tracy and son Nick, are told that he has died.

Collins regains awareness during the cyborg's first mission, a covert operation in the Central American nation of Estrella, and is horrified to find himself killing on command. He manages to seize control of the body from its computer and imposes a permanent "no killing" instruction on his own systems. He escapes Cybertek and begins to investigate the people responsible, while hiding his survival from his family, whom he believes he can no longer rejoin as a machine. Over the course of his series he confronts the Deathlok program's backers, including Roxxon and the crime lord Silvermane, and faces opponents such as Mechadoom and Moses Magnum.

Collins forms a working relationship with the private investigator Misty Knight, who herself has a cybernetic arm, and is drawn into the affairs of other cyborgs in the storyline "The Souls of Cyber-Folk." He receives help from the Black Panther, who repairs and refines his systems in Wakanda, and from Nick Fury of S.H.I.E.L.D. He is pulled into the conflict against the Abomination, who has established a refuge in the sewers of New York, and is among the heroes recruited during the "Infinity War" and "Infinity Crusade" crossovers. Collins eventually reveals himself to Tracy and Nick and resumes a limited family life. In later years he appears as a reluctant participant in the events of Beyond!, in which he is one of a group of characters abducted to Battleworld, and continues to act as an occasional ally of the Avengers and Spider-Man.
