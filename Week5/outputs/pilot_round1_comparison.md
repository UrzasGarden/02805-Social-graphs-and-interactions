== Creation ==

She-Hulk was created largely in response to the success of the live-action television series The Incredible Hulk (1977–1982). Marvel was concerned that the program's producers at Universal might introduce a female counterpart to the Hulk on their own, as had happened when The Six Million Dollar Man generated the spin-off The Bionic Woman, leaving the company with no claim to such a character. Stan Lee, who by that point rarely scripted comics, wrote the first issue so that Marvel would hold the copyright and trademark. Lee later acknowledged that protecting the name had been the principal reason for the character's creation.

Lee and penciller John Buscema worked only on the debut issue of The Savage She-Hulk. == 1980s ==

After the cancellation of her series, She-Hulk joined the Avengers in The Avengers #221 (July 1982) and remained a regular in that title for several years. Following the 1984 limited series Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars, in which the Thing chose to stay behind on an alien world, she took his place in the Fantastic Four beginning with Fantastic Four #265 (April 1984). Writer-artist John Byrne made extensive use of the character during his tenure on the book, and she stayed with the team until the Thing's return. Byrne also wrote and drew Marvel Graphic Novel #18: The Sensational She-Hulk (1985), in which exposure to radiation leaves Walters unable to revert to human form, a status quo that persisted for many years.

Byrne launched a second ongoing series, The Sensational She-Hulk, in May 1989. The title was distinguished by its comedic tone and by the character's awareness that she was in a comic book: she spoke directly to readers, argued with the writer and tore through panel borders to move between scenes. The device predates its association with later Marvel characters such as Deadpool and Gwenpool. Byrne left after the eighth issue following disputes with editorial. Steve Gerber wrote a lengthy stretch of the book, and artists including Bryan Hitch contributed before Byrne returned for issues #31–50. The series ended with issue #60 (February 1994).

She-Hulk had no solo title for a decade after The Sensational She-Hulk ended. In "Avengers Disassembled" (2004), an attack orchestrated by the Scarlet Witch causes She-Hulk to lose control and tear the Vision in half, an event that later stories revisited.

Writer Dan Slott revived the character in She-Hulk, which began in May 2004 with art by Juan Bobillo. Walters and Lyra later shared the four-issue series She-Hulks (2010–2011) by Harrison Wilcox, which followed the "Fall of the Hulks" and "World War Hulks" storylines.

== 2010s and 2020s ==

Walters was a member of the Future Foundation in Jonathan Hickman's FF (2011–2012) and received a new solo series in February 2014 written by Charles Soule, himself a practicing attorney, with art by Javier Pulido and Ron Wimberly and covers by Kevin Wada. In 2015 she led the all-female team A-Force, first in a limited series tied to "Secret Wars" and then in an ongoing title.

The 2016 crossover "Civil War II" left Walters comatose after an attack by Thanos, and Bruce Banner was killed during the same event. Observing mankind's violent nature, Mahr Vehl reveals that the Kree have decided to confine humanity on Earth until the entity Gah Lak Tus can destroy them, ending the potential threat.

Another Kree force attacks the installation soon after, but is stopped by a combined team of superheroes. Another team of heroes—with Mahr Vehl's aid—storm the Kree ship while the aliens are on Earth. Mahr Vehl is under the control of the Titan Thanos when he comes into possession of the Cosmic Cube.

In the mini-series Hunger, Mahr-Vehl sacrifices himself to destroy a "merged" Galactus with a nuclear weapon.

== Powers and abilities ==

Mahr Vehl's body has been implanted with a green and white cybernetic battle suit that can be activated by a device located on his wrist (which takes the appearance of a wristwatch when he is in human form). The battlesuit flares with bio-electric energy and provides Mahr Vehl with increased strength, increased healing, speed, flight, invisibility, phasing, the ability to view the entire light spectrum, the capability to create energy shields, and the power to project energy blasts via the totalkannon on his left forearm. In later appearances, Mahr Vehl's armor suit shows other special capabilities such as shape shifting and armor expansion, causing him to bulk up into a larger, stronger physical form along with greater energy projection fired from both arms instead of just the armor control dial. They can also project massive gunnery.
